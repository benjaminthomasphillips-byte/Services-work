Written by Claude on 23 November 2026. This is a map of what a discovery sweep found, not a set of theses. Everything here was found by agents; none of it was saved by Ben. Each item is filed in Sources with its quotations and the agent's reading.

## How it was run

- **Round 1:** four scouts (people, organisations, practice, outside the frame). They started from the authors behind the sources Ben rated most highly and snowballed along citations, Substack recommendations, podcast appearances, replies and colleagues. Each made at least two deliberate jumps to languages, fields or platforms the library had nothing from.
- **Round 2:** three scouts expanded the 27 most promising leads from round 1, favouring those several scouts had reached independently.
- **Filing:** three librarian agents filed the finds. There was no verification layer: each item records who is speaking and what they gain, and is quoted from what was read.
- **Result:** 107 finds, 104 unique and filed. None was in the library before. Languages: English, German, French, Dutch, Japanese and Chinese.

## What was found, by theme

### 1. The theory of the firm is being rewritten

Economists and organisation scholars disagree about where work goes when knowledge gets cheap.

- **Garicano** (LSE): expert work moves inside the client firm, and specialists keep only the rare, hard cases. He also argues that an agent swarm is a firm and has to be governed as one.
- **Choudary and Howard Yu** (IMD): cheap coordination shrinks the unit of work instead.
- **Kim and Koning:** AI-native start-ups are 25% smaller and flatter, because the work moves into the product. In services they are about 70% smaller.
- **Kim, Kim and Koning:** a field experiment found the bottleneck is working out where AI fits, not getting access to it.
- **Ide and Talamàs:** autonomous AI helps experts, and co-pilot AI helps novices.
- **Puranam** (INSEAD): AI used top-down concentrates control; used between peers it decentralises.
- **Koçak, Puranam and Truong:** build silos on purpose around agents that organise themselves.
- **Farrell and Shalizi:** coordination is mostly mutual adjustment, so a company run as an agent fleet from the top will not work.
- **Garicano and Imas:** AI progress is speeding up but each unit costs more, so there is no intelligence explosion yet.

### 2. Operating models: incumbents redesigning themselves

- **Block** (Dorsey and Botha): a company world model replaces what middle management did.
- **Notion** (Ivan Zhao): over 700 agents alongside 1,000 staff.
- **Intercom/Fin:** 94% of code changes are agent-driven and AI approves about a fifth; eight people hold the quality bar for more than 200 builders; forward-deployed "swarms" pair an engineer with a data scientist who proves the value; a loop turns unscalable customer work into product.
- **Stripe:** "Minions", unattended coding agents that produce 1,300+ merged changes a week.
- **Mercari:** made its CTO head of HR and AI; one-person AI pods hit a quality ceiling.
- **Toyota:** quality checks on every document, not a sample.
- **C.H. Robinson:** "Lean AI" at a freight broker, used as an acquisition thesis.
- **Constellation:** AI left to each business, no CIO, no headcount cuts.
- **Lemonade:** a diligence test, customer growth divided by headcount growth. "If growth requires proportional hiring, AI is ornamental."
- **Doctolib** and **Zocdoc:** sober European and US engineering accounts.

### 3. Japan and China: a whole discourse the library did not have

- **LayerX** (Tokyo) appears five times:
  - the AI budget is set before the headcount plan;
  - token spend is treated as work moved from people to AI;
  - pay was raised to share the gains;
  - a "design harness" writes professional judgement down as versioned rules the AI must cite, or answer "cannot assess";
  - recruiting agents work to the rule "draft is the limit";
  - after uncapped use cost about ¥300,000 a person a month, it moved to managing token economics.
- **Mercari:** a week with all manual coding banned; a former CTO argues for low-context organisations.
- **Asahi Tekko:** a Toyota alumnus runs AI-first kaizen.
- **China:**
  - Chen Chunhua says managers must take back the right to judge from AI.
  - Mu Sheng calls the "AI-native organisation" label mostly a slogan that is safe for bosses.
  - Lenovo has published a five-level maturity model.
  - Haier's front-line workers have built 979 of their own AI assistants.

### 4. Practice: how the work actually changed

- **Agents as staff:** Talentsconnect (Germany) puts agents on the org chart, onboards them and retires them. It reports EBITDA moving from −30% to +30% since 2023.
- **Cars24** (India): AI token spend reached 8.88% of the salary bill.
- **Physical and regulated settings:**
  - Neil Kelly's "30 agents" are deterministic data pipelines;
  - a construction estimator's rule: keep AI off anything whose error rate is your margin;
  - NHS GP practices and Oxford hospitals on AI scribes. Time was saved, but only a quarter of clinicians saw more patients without a service redesign.
  - the UK housing ministry's casework tool;
  - claims adjusters spending 15 to 20 minutes per claim fixing AI.
- **Failures:**
  - SaaStr's log of "drift" and "aggression";
  - a refund agent killed after customers learned to game it;
  - French PE (Siparex) on scoping taking five times as long as building;
  - TNO on HelloPrint going from 100 to 20 customer-service staff, while elsewhere saved time did not become output.
- **Forcing adoption:**
  - Zocdoc retrained 300 engineers in six weeks;
  - Qonto has everyone change their own work for 30 to 60 minutes a day, and funds the bigger changes centrally.
- **Flat firms:**
  - Kalshi has about 130 people reporting to two founders;
  - Bending Spoons puts the burden of proof on anyone adding complexity;
  - Bird went from 1,000+ staff to 120, keeping high-agency generalists;
  - FINN abolished the software engineer title;
  - Lexware is moving to two-person teams.

### 5. Investment platforms (the library's thinnest area)

- **The roll-up debate:**
  - Benaich and Mrkšić: an AI roll-up is a better services firm, not a software company.
  - Ziperski: venture funds are the wrong vehicle for roll-ups.
  - Lyseng: a roll-up is three businesses in one and needs permanent capital.
  - Decio: build AI services firms from scratch instead.
- **How funds run themselves:**
  - NBIM: over 300 staff contribute to IT development, with a target of halving manual processes.
  - Bridgewater: teaches models the firm's taste from expert labels.
  - Man Group: agentic research pipelines.
  - USV: named agents and a weekly reflection on "USV Taste".
  - DVC: replaced analysts with limited partners who earn a share of carry for helping.
  - Avala and Lone Pine: one analyst with agent fleets covers 200 names, and the team changes shape more than size.
  - Andrew Ang: 44 agents governed by the investment policy statement.
- **Portfolio programmes:** Vista's "Agentic Factory", Ardian, Siparex, and Verdane on boards needing to hold through the J-curve.
- **Governance:** Methlow's AI-native family office, where the board governs what the AI knows.

### 6. Outside the frame: older ideas applied now

- **Lean and Toyota:**
  - Smalley: decide where thinking about the work sits;
  - Priolo: ask who gets the gain before you deploy;
  - Jansen: Toyota's stop-on-abnormality principle applied to agents.
  - McElheran and others: about half of old plants' AI productivity loss comes from dropping lean routines.
- **Safety science:**
  - Woods: agents will misbehave, so design for joint activity;
  - Bainbridge's ironies of automation, via Hendrick: "The work does not diminish. It concentrates";
  - Gerlach: cut the experts and the models degrade unseen.
- **Cybernetics:** Davies says an agent is a macro that loops too long without a human; Acutt finds the same ideas in today's agent design.
- **Labour economics:** Afrouzi and Hurst's skills trap; Shen and Tamkin show that learning with AI cuts understanding unless people use it to explain.
- **Sceptics with substance:** Evans on institutionalised versus improvised work; Newport on "freestyle work"; Kapoor on AI breaking the bargain behind Indian IT offshoring; Ferguson's evidence to the French Parliament that experts are swamped with requests to check AI work.

## Ideas new to the library

- Agents with job descriptions, onboarding and retirement dates (Talentsconnect).
- Token spend as a share of payroll, used as a management metric (Cars24, LayerX).
- Customer growth divided by headcount growth as a test of whether AI is real (Lemonade).
- Professional judgement written as versioned, cited rules, with an explicit "cannot assess" (LayerX).
- The forward-deployed team as a unit that includes someone who measures the value (Fin).
- The board governs what the AI knows; a steward of the information who does not report to the CIO (Methlow).
- Paying staff for making the team smarter rather than for individual targets (Chen Chunhua's cases).
- Limited partners replacing the junior bench in a venture fund (DVC).
- A roll-up as three businesses (product, aggregator, process company) needing permanent capital (Lyseng).
- Build silos on purpose for agents (Koçak, Puranam, Truong).
- Expert work moving inside the client (Garicano), against the unit of work shrinking (Choudary).
- Dropping lean routines as the hidden cost of industrial AI (McElheran).
- Experts swamped by requests to check AI work (Ferguson); and in France, a staff-council consultation is required even for AI pilots.
