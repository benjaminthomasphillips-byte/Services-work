### 3. Push the redesign further into the future

### 4. Test three things your report touches but does not settle

- **Does review actually catch anything?** Research shows reviewers often defer to an AI's suggestion. Your design leans heavily on licensed sign-off and human adjudication. What evidence is there on how much human review catches when the AI is usually right? How would you design and measure review so it is real rather than ceremonial? How does the answer change your headcount and margins?
- **Who keeps the saving?** You show flat-fee work collapsing in a price war. Is there any measured case of a services firm keeping AI savings, rather than passing them to clients, over more than one renewal cycle? How would you separate gains from AI, from repricing and from consolidation?
- **Does moving work to the source just move effort onto the client?** When work is captured where it is born (the client's systems, transactions, devices), does the client end up doing more? How would you measure and design for that?

## Part B (optional): rival hypotheses to test

Another independent piece of work reached some different conclusions. Do not adopt them. Test each against your evidence and reasoning, and say whether it holds, holds with changes, or fails, and why.

1. **The thing the service is about.** The most important design object is a shared, live record of the thing the service is about (the business, the home, the estate, the employment). It is owned by whoever owns the thing and readable by every party with a right to it. That record, plus the authority to act recorded in it, is what lets the firm act in the client's world.
2. **Money position follows legal role.** Where the firm acts as the client's agent it should act from the client's account and hold nothing. Where the law makes it the principal (for example as employer of record) it must advance money, so it should earn nothing from holding it and back the role with capital.
3. **Duties to parties who do not pay.** A durable firm must be built to be trusted by parties it does not serve: tenants, claimants, beneficiaries, employees, the person receiving care. Its revenue must never rise when their outcome worsens.
4. **Parameters you did not use.** The design depends on:
   - whether the thing served is improving, stable or declining (for example an ageing person or an estate);
   - whether rules are written by the client, the state, or by unions and employers through collective agreements;
   - how much of the value is a human relationship rather than a physical task.

   Do these change your map?
5. **Make judgement deliberately.** Because agents remove the work through which juniors learned, a firm must manufacture its future judges through a designed route and get professional bodies to recognise it. Otherwise it runs out of the people its own design depends on.
