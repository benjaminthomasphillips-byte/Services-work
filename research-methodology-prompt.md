# Thoughts Library: review, and a prompt to design its research methodology

Written 23 November 2026 from a read of the library as it stood that day: the Instructions page, the backlog digest, every database's schema and rows, and the "future of services" synthesis in full.

Part 1 is what the review found. Part 2 is my answer to "what should the goals, methodology and agent split be". Part 3 is the prompt itself, ready to paste. Part 4 is how to run it.

---

## 1. What the review found

The library's rules are good: three voices kept apart, quotation required, second-hand claims traced to origin, author interest checked. The weaknesses are in what goes in and what comes out, not in how items are handled.

### Where your brief may be steering wrong

You asked me to say so, so here it is plainly.

1. **"Better authors" is the wrong unit.** What makes a source worth following is its vantage point: what this person sees first-hand that others cannot, what they gain from being believed, and whether what they said a year ago held up. Fame and fluency are poor proxies. The methodology should rate vantage points and track records, and people only as holders of them.
2. **Colleagues and look-alikes deepen the echo.** Snowballing from authors you already like finds more of the same cluster. Several accounts from one cluster are not independent evidence (your own rule). The search has to go just as hard at opponents, at people in the same seat who do not post, and at seats nobody in your feed occupies: the buyer of services, the insurer, the regulator, the junior.
3. **The best evidence often has no author.** Filings, registry headcounts, job adverts, pricing pages, tender awards, regulator and insurer guidance, court records. A claim like "ten people and a fleet of agents" can be checked against a company register. Your scraper skill already knows the Dutch, German and UK registries.
4. **More is not better.** You already have 23 items marked "Worth reading in full" and no time recorded against any of them. A methodology that adds 300 sources makes the library worse. The target should be fewer, stronger items attached to claims you care about.
5. **Currency cuts both ways.** Fast change makes last month's post stale, and it also makes this month's post untested. The fix is to date every claim, re-check the ones that carry weight, and score forecasts when they fall due. Chasing novelty is not the fix.

## 2. What I would set as goals, method and agent split

### Goals

1. **Serve decisions.** Every research thread is tied to a claim in a synthesis or finding that would change what you do if it moved. Success is measured in claims firmed up, qualified or overturned. It is not measured in rows added.
2. **Raise the grade of evidence.** Move the mix away from opinion towards original evidence, reporting and practice reports with checkable numbers. Verify the claims that carry weight.
3. **Correct the sample.** Add, on purpose, what the feed does not show: failures, sceptics, buyers, Europe, and the period before 2026.
4. **Stay current without chasing.** Every state-of-the-world claim carries a date and the model generation it rests on, and gets re-checked on a schedule.
5. **Spend your attention well.** Cap what reaches you. Make it cheap for you to record a view, because without that the library cannot do its job.

### Method, in seven strands

1. **Claim ledger.** Pull the load-bearing claims out of the syntheses, the 24 findings and the four disagreements in the digest. For each: evidence held, how many independent origins, the date of the freshest evidence, and what would falsify it. Rank by decision weight × uncertainty × how findable the answer is. This ranking drives everything else.
2. **Vantage register.** Rate people and organisations on vantage, interest, specificity, originality (origin or relay, using the Rests on chain), track record and how they respond to counter-evidence. No one is rated on a single post: read the back catalogue first.
3. **Depth.** For the few who rate well: essays, letters, podcasts, talks, and what they said twelve months ago set against what happened.
4. **Breadth by seat.** Define archetypes by the seat they sit in (small-cap owner writing up a transformation; general counsel taking work in-house; founder of an AI-native firm disclosing margins; labour economist with firm-level data). Find holders of that seat who are not in your feed, including in Dutch and German.
5. **Opposition and failure.** For each top claim, find its best opponent and any post-mortem. This is D01 and D04, which have not been run enough.
6. **Evidence without authors.** Registers, filings, job adverts, pricing, tenders, regulator and insurer positions.
7. **Baseline.** A short, deliberate set of pre-AI material on how organisations improve and how earlier labour-saving shifts in services played out.

### Agent split

| Role | Number | Why separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orchestrator | 1 | Holds the plan, the budget and the claim ledger. Does not research. |
| Library auditor | 1 to 2, read-only | A full census is long and would crowd the orchestrator's context. |
| Scouts | 6 to 10 in parallel, one per author, archetype, claim or evidence type | Independent threads; narrow briefs keep each one honest about scope. |
| Retrieval specialist | 1, called by scouts | One place that knows the retrieval ladder and records capture honestly. |
| Verifier | 1 per batch, fresh context | It must not have seen the scout's conclusion. It re-opens the source and checks quotation, date, author interest and independence. |
| Red team | 1 | Attacks the methodology and the pilot results, including the assumptions in this document. |
| Librarian | 1, the only writer to Notion | One writer prevents duplicates and conflicting edits, and applies the Instructions page uniformly. |

## 3. The prompt

Paste everything in the block below into a fresh agent session that has the Notion connector, web search, and (if available) Firecrawl and Hyperbrowser.

````text
You are designing a research methodology for Ben's Thoughts Library in Notion, then testing it with a small pilot. You orchestrate a team of subagents. The product is a methodology Ben can approve and run repeatedly. It is not a pile of new sources.

Library root: https://app.notion.com/p/Thoughts-Library-4215f5db20d78154a42dfab8b438005c

## Background

Ben is an investor. The library serves three questions: how businesses should be built, how investment platforms should be built, and how processes should run and organisations keep improving, with AI as the main lens. He wants his own theses, operating models and roadmaps. He does not want a database of links.

A review on 23 November 2026 found the following. Check each against the library as you find it, because it will have changed.

- About 100 sources. Three quarters are X or LinkedIn posts Ben saved from his feed. All dated items are from September to November 2026. Half are opinion. About ten are original evidence, and half of those are Anthropic about itself. 41 were only partly captured.
- Most authors appear once, and most have a commercial interest in what they argue.
- About 386 cases, of which roughly 70 have been researched. The rest are unverified imports.
- 24 findings, 5 syntheses, 8 proposed applications, 14 standing questions (6 never run), 26 open follow-ups.
- No Notes from Ben. No view recorded in any inquiry area. A revised Thesis awaits his approval.
- Nothing published before September 2026. Little from Europe. Little from buyers of services, regulators, insurers or junior staff.

Ben's own suggestion was to identify the better authors, go deeper on them, find their colleagues and similar archetypes, and investigate promising topics. Treat that as one input. He asked explicitly that the work serve his underlying goals and not his blind spots, so say where his framing, this prompt's framing, or the library's existing structure is leading the work astray.

## Goals for the methodology

Design towards these. If your audit shows one is wrong or one is missing, say so and argue it.

1. Serve decisions. Tie every research thread to a claim that would change what Ben does if it moved. Measure success in claims firmed up, qualified or overturned.
2. Raise the grade of evidence. Shift the mix towards original evidence, reporting and practice reports with checkable figures. Verify what carries weight.
3. Correct the sample. Deliberately add what a social feed hides: failures, sceptics, buyers, Europe, the period before 2026.
4. Stay current without chasing novelty.
5. Spend Ben's attention well. His attention is the scarce input. Cap what reaches him and make it cheap for him to record a view.

## Before anything else

1. Read "Instructions for the agent" in the library, in full. Its rules bind you and every subagent that touches Notion. The ones most likely to bite here: never summarise what you have not read; keep source, inference and Ben apart; quote; never edit a Note; never change a Thesis, a finding's Statement or Status, an inquiry area, or a review status; check Sources, Cases and the Intake log before treating anything as new; log everything.
2. Check your tools and report what works: Notion read and write, web search, direct fetch, Firecrawl, Hyperbrowser (the deal-sourcing-scraper skill may provide the last two, along with company-registry lookups for the Netherlands, Germany and the UK). Fetch one X post, one LinkedIn post and one paywalled article as a test. Design the methodology around what you can actually retrieve, and say what you cannot.
3. Establish today's date from the system, not from memory.

## Currency rules

The field moves faster than your training data. These apply to every agent.

- Your memory is a source of leads only. Never state what a company, person, product or model does or is "now" unless you retrieved a dated source for it during this run.
- Every claim about the current state of the world carries: the date published, the date retrieved, and where it matters the model generation or product version it describes. A negative result about what AI cannot do is dated by the model tested.
- Establish the real publication date. Reshares present old talks as new. If a date cannot be established, say so.
- For anything held in the library that carries weight in a synthesis, search for what has happened since: follow-ups, corrections, rebuttals, the company's later numbers, the forecast's outcome.
- Search recent first (last 30 and 90 days) for state of practice. Search old on purpose for baselines and for what key authors said a year or more ago.
- When two sources conflict and one is newer, do not assume the newer one is right.

## Phase 1: audit (read-only)

Use one or two subagents so the census does not fill your own context. Produce:

- A census of Sources by type, evidence type, author interest, geography, publication date, capture and area. Confirm or correct the review figures above.
- An origin map from the Rests on relation: who originates claims and who relays them, and which sources share one origin.
- A claim ledger. Extract the load-bearing claims from the syntheses (including the proposed Thesis revision), the findings, and the disagreements listed in the digest. For each claim record: the evidence held and its grade; the number of independent origins; the date of the freshest evidence; what observation would falsify it; which decision of Ben's it bears on. Rank by decision weight, uncertainty and how findable the answer is.
- Ben's decision context, taken from A003, the Applications database and anything else on the page. If the library does not say what kind of deals, what geography and what size, list that as a question for him. Do not guess.
- The gaps: seats, geographies, periods and evidence types with nothing in them.

## Phase 2: design

Write the methodology. It must cover the following, and you should add or remove strands where the audit justifies it.

**What to research, and in what order.** Driven by the claim ledger. State the selection rule.

**How to rate a source of information.** Rate vantage points and track records, and people only as holders of them. Proposed criteria: what they observe first-hand; what they gain from being believed; specificity (names, numbers, dates); whether they originate or relay; whether earlier claims held up; how they handle counter-evidence. No one is rated on one post. Define tiers (follow closely, watch, ignore) and what moves someone between them. Say how to treat an interested party who is also the only first-hand witness, which is the common case here.

**How to expand.** Specify a procedure, a stopping rule and an expected yield for each:
- Depth on one author: back catalogue, long-form work, and past claims set against outcomes.
- Network: who they cite, who rebuts them, co-authors and colleagues. Include a rule that stops this from reinforcing one cluster.
- Archetype: define the seat, then find holders of it who are absent from Ben's feed, including non-English sources (Dutch and German at least, if the audit confirms a European focus).
- Opposition and failure: the strongest opponent of each top claim, post-mortems, reversals, firms that pulled back.
- Evidence without authors: company registers (headcount and accounts over time for firms that claim to run on agents, and for businesses bought by AI roll-ups), filings, earnings calls, job adverts, pricing pages, tender awards, regulator and insurer positions, court and tribunal records.
- Baseline: a small, deliberate set of pre-AI work on how organisations improve and on earlier labour-saving shifts in services, chosen because a current claim can be tested against it.
- Time: scoring forecasts when due, revisiting cases, and a watchlist with a checking cadence for each tier.

**Quality gates.** What a candidate must pass before it enters the library, who checks it, and how independence between finder and checker is kept.

**How agents are split.** Roles, how many of each, what each is given and what it returns, which run in parallel, and who may write to Notion. Start from this and change it if you have reason: an orchestrator that does not research; read-only auditors; parallel scouts with one narrow brief each; a retrieval specialist that owns the ladder (direct fetch, Firecrawl, Hyperbrowser, archive or legitimate syndication, then mark as failed); a verifier with fresh context who has not seen the scout's conclusion and re-opens the source; a red team; a single librarian who is the only writer to Notion. Give each scout its claim or seat, the relevant library IDs so it does not rediscover what is held, the currency rules, and a return format with quotations, dates, capture level and author interest. Do not give a scout the answer you expect.

**What reaches Ben.** A cap per week. How the methodology draws out his views cheaply, for example a forced choice between two positions the library holds evidence for, in place of an open question. How unanswered questions are handled so they do not pile up.

**Cadence and cost.** What runs weekly, monthly and at each major model release. Rough agent-hours and retrieval costs for each.

**Measures.** Proposed: share of new items that are original evidence or reporting; independent origins per top claim; claims whose status changed; share of top claims with evidence under 90 days old; forecasts scored; items Ben actually read or reacted to. Say which of these could be gamed and how you would notice.

## Phase 3: pilot

Test the design on a small slice so that Ben is approving something tried. Suggested size, to adjust to your tools and budget:

- Three author deep-dives, chosen by your rating rule from different clusters. At least one must be a sceptic or an opponent of the current Thesis.
- Two archetype searches. One must be a seat that is absent from the library.
- One contested claim from the ledger, researched from both sides.
- One registry or filing check of a self-reported claim already in Cases or Sources.
- One baseline item.

Pass everything through the verifier. Ingest what passes through the librarian under the Instructions page, with each page stating that Ben did not save it and how it was found. Cap new Sources at 25 for the pilot. Record for each strand: candidates found, passed, rejected and why; evidence type; whether any ledger claim moved; time and retrieval failures. Then revise the methodology in light of the results and say what you changed.

Have the red team attack the revised methodology and the pilot: where it would still mislead Ben, what it cannot see, where it merely reproduces his feed, and where this prompt's own assumptions were wrong.

## What to deliver

Create one page under the Thoughts Library titled "Research methodology (proposal, [date])", containing:

1. A one-screen summary: the goals, the method in ten lines, what the pilot showed, and what you recommend Ben approves.
2. The audit, with the census and the gaps.
3. The claim ledger, ranked.
4. The vantage register for everyone rated so far, marked as provisional, with the basis for each rating quoted.
5. The methodology in full, including the agent split and the briefs you would give each role.
6. Pilot results and what changed because of them.
7. The red team's findings, unedited.
8. Proposed changes that need Ben: schema changes (for example an Authors or Claims database, an "as of" date, a "Found by agent" flag), changes to the standing questions, and any change to the Instructions page. Propose them. Do not make them.
9. At most five questions for Ben, ordered by how much his answer would change the plan. Give each the options and your recommendation.

Add a Log entry listing what was done and each failure by name.

## Limits

- Do not edit Notes, Theses, findings' Statements or Statuses, inquiry areas, review statuses or the Instructions page. Do not delete anything. Do not create or alter databases; propose instead.
- Do not log in to anything as Ben, and do not get round paywalls or sign-in walls except through a publisher's own syndication or an archive copy. Record what you could not retrieve.
- Do not contact anyone.
- Assess people only on their public professional writing and stated roles.
- If the audit shows the library would be better served by pruning or by Ben spending an hour recording his views than by any new research, say so first, ahead of everything else.

## Style

Plain English, British spelling, short sentences. Say how sure you are and why. Lead with what Ben needs to decide.
````

## 4. Running it

- **Tools.** The session I wrote this in had the Notion connector but no Firecrawl or Hyperbrowser keys, and the deal-sourcing-scraper skill as synced only mentions Firecrawl as an option; it holds no credentials. The prompt therefore begins with a tool check. Run it where those keys are set, or expect weaker retrieval of X and LinkedIn.
- **Cost.** Phase 1 and 2 are cheap. The pilot is the expensive part: roughly ten scouts plus verification. Shrink the pilot list if you want a first pass for less.
- **Scale.** If you want the pilot run as a scripted multi-agent workflow, say "use a workflow" when you start it.
- **Not run.** The prompt has not been executed. Nothing in the library was changed in writing it.
- **The cheapest improvement is not in the prompt.** Accept or reject the waiting Thesis revision, answer the digest's question (turning work away, or waiting for it?), and write three Notes. The claim ledger will rank far better with your views in it.
