## 1. Summary

**Goals.** Serve decisions. Raise the grade of evidence. Correct the sample. Stay current without chasing novelty. Spend your attention well.

**The method in ten lines.**
1. You state your context and mark the top claims keep, strike or reword. Until then only maintenance runs.
2. A claim ledger drives all research. A thread with no ledger claim is not run.
3. Claims are ranked by decision weight and uncertainty. Whether an answer is publicly findable decides the route, not the rank.
4. Claims that cannot be tested from public sources go on a separate list for paid data, expert calls, diligence and portfolio numbers.
5. Sources are rated by vantage point and track record. Recurring primary series are followed on a calendar. Authors are used as pointers to origins.
6. Contested claims get two scouts, one per side, who cannot see each other.
7. Absent seats (insurer, regulator, buyer, junior, works council) and evidence without authors (registers, filings) are searched on purpose.
8. Every candidate is re-opened by a verifier who did not find it. Titles must stay in the source's own mood: "expects", "says", "reports".
9. Conclusions are gated as well as sources: each clause is tagged observed, company claim, forecast or inferred, with a note on whether it transfers to a firm of the size you buy.
10. One claim a month, at most five sources filed, one strand run twice to see how much is noise.

## 5. Methodology (version 3, after the pilot and the red team)

### Step 0. Your input comes first
Forty-five minutes: the five questions at the end of this page. The ledger is rebuilt on your answers. Without them, only maintenance runs: the inbox, overdue follow-ups, forecasts due.

### What to research, and in what order
- Each cycle works one ledger claim, chosen by decision weight and uncertainty, or by you.
- A claim that cannot be tested publicly goes on the "other route" list with the route named: paid deal data, trade press subscription, expert call, diligence, portfolio numbers.
- Overdue follow-ups are closed before any new thread opens. This run broke that rule.

### Rating sources
- Step one for any author, before reading them: a ten-minute standing check. Who are they, since when, what have they done, what footprint exists in registers, regulators and filings, how old are the domain and the account.
- Then the six criteria, each with a quoted basis.
- "Follow" is for recurring primary series. "Watch" is for authors read as pointers and never cited alone as evidence. "Ignore" is for relays and the unidentifiable.
- An interested party who is the only first-hand witness is kept, labelled "company claim", and paired with a non-author check wherever one exists.
- Next cycle, test the "authors as pointers" rule against one high-vantage individual you choose.

### Expansion strands
For each: procedure, stopping rule, pilot yield.

- **Contested claim, two blind scouts.** One per side. Neither sees the other. Each must report honestly if its side is weak. Stop at five candidates a side. Pilot: ten candidates; both reached a similar reading. The red team calls that weak convergence: same model, same day, same kind of source.
- **Absent seat.** Search the body's own name plus "AI" or "KI" plus the year, in the local language. Prefer the body's own document to commentary. Then turn the seat into a calendar. Pilot: five primary documents across the UK, the Netherlands and Germany in about an hour. Only the easy seat was tried. Buyers, juniors and works councils were not.
- **Register and filings.** Resolve the legal entity from the firm's own footer or imprint; a name search finds namesakes. Record the company number and next accounts due date on the Case. Read the acquired subsidiaries, which have pre-acquisition baselines. Re-run only when a filing falls due. Pilot: four organisations resolved.
- **Baseline.** One claim. Write down the variables it needs measured. Pursue an episode only if a source measures at least two. Competition-authority market studies first, then one paper. Every item is labelled "analogue" with the reason it may not hold for AI. Keep a list of paywalled papers for someone with journal access. Pilot: four measured sources in 25 minutes; one episode had no measured answer and should have been dropped after two searches.
- **Evidence at your scale.** [inferred] New after the red team. Full accounts of PE-backed European services groups; small listed European services firms; two or three paid trade and financial titles. The pilot's keyword route to European operators was thin: four candidates, one strong.
- **Author depth.** Only when an author carries weight in a synthesis or you ask. Standing check first. Then a two-column table: each headline number against the sentence in the origin that supports it.
- **Network (colleagues and look-alikes).** Demoted. Use only to find a named opponent or an origin. Never add a second source from one cluster to a claim unless it brings a new origin.
- **Time.** Score forecasts when due. Ask "what has happened since" of every source that carries weight in a synthesis.

### Quality gates
On sources:
- Read, or capture stated honestly.
- Author and interest established with a quotation, or "could not be established".
- Date taken from the face of the document, not from a file name or a proxy.
- Origin traced. Each quotation carries its own link and date.
- The whole answer or section around a quotation is read. Two of the three verification failures had the contradicting sentence next to the quotation.
- Evidence type assigned strictly. A management statement on a results call is a company claim, even inside a filing.
- The title stays in the source's mood, makes one claim and implies no cause the source does not state.
- A verifier who did not find it re-opens the live source. Next time the verifier gets the link and quotations only, writes its own reading, then sees the proposed title. In this run it saw the title first.
- Client-only or watermarked research is not filed. Copyrighted articles are filed as link plus short quotations.

On conclusions [inferred], new after the red team:
- No "what moved" line is written before verification.
- Each clause is tagged observed, company claim, forecast or inferred, and by size, geography, listed or private.
- A checker searches the captures for items that cut the other way.
- Each conclusion carries a transfer note: why it would or would not hold for a firm of the size you buy.

### Agent split
- **Orchestrator, 1.** Holds the ledger and budget and saves every report. Does no research. It should not also write the conclusions; the red team is right about that. A separate synthesiser should, and be checked.
- **Auditors, 2, read-only.** About four minutes each.
- **Scouts, as many as there are strands, in parallel.** One narrow brief each, a shared rules file, at most five candidates, not told the expected answer. Four to thirteen minutes each in the pilot.
- **Verifiers, one per fifteen candidates, fresh context.**
- **Red team, 1.**
- **Librarian, 1.** The only writer to Notion.
- Retrieval is a ladder inside every scout: search, direct fetch, reader proxy, Firecrawl, Hyperbrowser. Stop at paywalls and sign-in walls and log the failure.

The brief each scout received is in the repository file named at the end of this page.

### What reaches you
- At most five items a month while the cycle is one claim a month.
- One question at a time. It expires after two weeks.
- The red team's caution: a forced choice written by the agent records your view inside the agent's frame. So the first questions are open ones about your context.

### Cadence and cost
- This run used about 4 million tokens across 16 subagents and about two hours of wall-clock time. I do not have a money figure. Scouts cost 150,000 to 300,000 tokens each.
- Proposed: one claim a month, three or four scouts, one verifier, one checker. Roughly a third of this run.
- Maintenance weekly, small: inbox, follow-ups due, the "follow" calendar.
- At each major model release: re-test dated negative results (finding L019).

### Measures
- Notes you wrote and claims you marked.
- Decisions where a ledger claim was cited.
- Independent origins per top claim.
- Share of filed items that are filings, regulator documents, measured studies or reporting, counted strictly.
- Forecasts scored.
- Gameable: "original evidence" inflates if company claims are filed as evidence, which happened in this pilot before verification. "Claims changed" inflates by rewording.

**6. PROCESS RISKS**

- Single run. No repeat, no second seed, no variance estimate. One strand run twice would show how much is noise.
- Verifier independence is weak. The draft says the verifier writes its own reading "before seeing the scout's title". CANDIDATES.md, compiled for verifiers, contains the title, the claim direction and the quotations to check. It is the same model with the same priors. It checks that quotations exist, not that conclusions follow.
- Partial reads carry weight: Genpact, Cognizant, TR, William Blair, Mazur, NOvA, WPK, METR, OpenAI (no charts or methods).
- The orchestrator chose targets, compiled candidates, wrote conclusions and revised the method. No one checked it before this review.
- Cost is given in agent-hours only. There is no money figure and no estimate of Ben's minutes. S5 took an hour for one strong item.
- Scraping. The brief tells scouts to curl with a fake "Mozilla/5.0" agent. One scout used it on SEC EDGAR, which requires a declared agent; EDGAR refused another scout. The brief also uses an undocumented X endpoint with a dummy token, a reader proxy, and a headless browser for X and LinkedIn, whose terms bar automated collection. northdata's terms should be checked.
- Copyright and terms. The William Blair PDF is marked "Unauthorized distribution prohibited". A full FT article was captured via syndication. A Motley Fool transcript was stored verbatim. Do not load full texts into a firm workspace without a view from counsel.
- Named individuals. The pilot records "undisclosed AI investments" against Ramez Naam and "loss-making" against a six-person firm, each from a single agent run. A vantage register is a profile database of named people held by a regulated firm. It needs a GDPR basis and a defamation check before it exists. The dismissed bookkeeper story concerns a private person.
