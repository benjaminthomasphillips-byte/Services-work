## B. Dispute one: approval at each step against limits and budgets

### R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-10

- Kind: observed result (one trial, reported by its author; the full study was not read by the library or by me)

- Read: full (thread); the Anthropic post behind it not retrieved

## What this adds up to

Inference, mine, not any source's.

1. Accountability attaches to a named human in every source that addresses it, including the advocates of autonomy (Blomfield, Alan, the SRA's conditions as reported). No source shows a regulator accepting an agent or a system as the accountable party.
2. The approval dispute is narrower than it looks. Deel approves writes by "an owner or a policy". B33 hard-codes gates only on irreversible outward actions. N076's budget is itself a control. The live question is which actions get a person, and L015 gives the sorting rule: consequence, reversibility and how soon the outcome can be checked. The Tamkin trial says the person, where used, must judge before seeing the agent's argument or must be a different party from the maker. No case tests either rule against the other.
3. Agent identity and permissions are organisation design, not plumbing. The only detailed account (Sierra) ended with one agent inheriting the user's access, service accounts for scheduled work and tagging by customer. For a roll-up, tagging by client and by firm is the equivalent, and it is unbuilt in anything the library holds.
4. On recording, the evidence favours capture as a by-product of work and decisions written down, over blanket recording. The costs O'Connor names are argued, not measured. Consent and client confidentiality are named as conditions and nowhere examined.
