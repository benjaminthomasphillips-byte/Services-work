## What this adds up to

Inference, mine.

1. Who must be convinced (Q6). At Ciridae's client the owner agreed and still did not use the tool. At Proton the chief executive was convinced and staff complied in fear. Belief at the top gained entry in both and willing use in neither. What changed use was sustained work beside the person doing the task, and a named owner (weakly, B14).
2. No held case pays the people whose know-how is captured. The library shows only what not to reward: usage (Uber, Proton).
3. Junior hiring fell in share at one AI lab. Nobody shows a rebuilt training route; Furman and Catalini offer proposals. A roll-up that removes junior work must fund training as a cost or use up its future reviewers.
4. Roles move to review, judgement and coordination. Meetings rose. Agents do not save management time on this evidence.
5. The routines that exist are written ones (Alan, Kagan, Spawn). Writing came first, agents after.
6. Nearly all of this is software firms describing themselves. Transfer to a licensed services firm is untested.

## What is missing

- Any pay, equity or bonus design for seniors whose know-how is captured or whose hours fall.
- Any measured adoption cost per firm.
- Any firm that rebuilt junior training and measured later competence.
- Any management system across a group of firms: budgets, monthly reviews, escalation.
- Staff consent or resistance to being recorded.
- A services firm, not a software firm, describing role change after a year.
- How Anthropic counts "agents" and "agent hours".
