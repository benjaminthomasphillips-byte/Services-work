## What this adds up to

Inference, mine, not the sources'.

1. Three financing shapes appear. Permanent capital with venture-scale equity and bank debt on the big deal (Long Lake, Thrive). Venture equity plus a debt facility for small UK property firms (Dwelly, Supa). Little or no debt, one firm at a time (Chroma, which has done nothing yet). No one discloses seller terms beyond "rolled over significant equity" or "sellers exit".
2. Every operating group keeps local firms and names, puts engineers inside them, and runs a shared platform. The centre in the fullest account has three teams: deals, engineering, and adoption.
3. The only timing evidence is one CEO's account: over a year for the first firms, days for later ones, two years of engineers on site, two to five years in all. The two clocks measure different things.
4. What carries between firms is claimed (80 per cent shared; runbooks reused), never costed. What is rebuilt each time is workflow mapping, data clean-up and adoption.
5. No group publishes a defined margin, retention or cohort result. The design for parts 2 and 4 rests on stated designs, not outcomes.
6. The library's own analysis favours buying at small-firm multiples and treating any AI gain as temporary unless the price says otherwise.
