# WEB-COMMERCIAL: the commercial side and the money

Outside research, done 23 November 2026. Scope: (a) who kept AI savings in services in 2025 and 2026; (b) how roll-ups and AI-native firms sell, brand and price; (c) client loss after a firm is sold to a consolidator.

How to read "Read". "Full" means the whole page was fetched and read through the fetch tool. "Part" means the document was downloaded and the passages on pricing, margin and clients were read, not every page. "Summary only" means I saw a search summary or a secondary account and could not open the source. No row rests on a search snippet without saying so. Row numbers have gaps (08, 16, 17, 24, 31) where weaker rows were cut for length; what they held is listed under "What is missing".

"Ben's positions" are referred to by question number: Q5 (does the work become a commodity, who keeps the gain), Q4 (static projects against continual improvement), Q1 and Q2 (inventory, four requirements).

## What this adds up to

Inference, not source statement.

1. Who keeps the saving depends mostly on whether the client can see the supplier's cost fall and can do the work itself. Where both hold (translation, content moderation, IT run work, enterprise consulting), revenue fell and suppliers defended margin only by cutting cost (rows 05, 07, 10, 12). Where neither holds yet (US law), suppliers raised prices (rows 13, 14).
2. Price pressure arrives at renewal and through procurement, not gradually. Fixed-price contracts hold the saving for the supplier until then (rows 04, 18).
3. No AI roll-up has published a price, a margin or a retention figure. Their stated plans assume the owner keeps the saving (rows 21 to 23).
4. The only hard evidence on clients after a consolidator buys a local firm is the vets case: clients stayed, prices rose, and the regulator intervened (row 27). Quiet ownership under the old name is a regulatory exposure, not a neutral choice.
5. Small clients now have their own checker (row 25). That is a commercial fact the design should use, not only fear.

For Ben's Q5: the evidence supports "the gain is shared or lost" for enterprise and visible work, and contradicts it so far for regulated, high-stakes advice. For the two reference firms there is almost no direct evidence.
