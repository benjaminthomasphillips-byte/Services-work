## What this adds up to

All of this section is inference.

Ben's description of Marble is accurate, with one correction: Marble presents capture as employee-switched and team-level, and adds a "measure" step. His "static" criticism is true of the older pattern (Mimica's two-week study and document pack; process-mining projects that stall after the analysis, as Deloitte and the academic reviews say). It is no longer true of what the vendors claim. Marble, Skan, Celonis, KYP.ai, Scribe and Interloom all now sell a "living" model. But "kept current" in their material means the map is redrawn from fresh observation. None shows the firm's own corrections changing a playbook, a test gating that change, or anyone owning it. That is Ben's second and third kind of improvement, and no product here evidences either.

Two structural limits follow. First, observation needs people doing the work; once an agent runs four steps in five, the screen record of that process thins out, so the map cannot stay current by watching alone. Second, coverage rests on consent that UK guidance says is rarely valid as a legal basis and that staff can withdraw.

For the design: capture-and-map is a rentable input (two AI roll-ups already rent Marble), and single-task capture is becoming a free feature of the model vendors' tools. The organisation round it is not rentable. Where mining kept paying, a standing team and change process did the work.
