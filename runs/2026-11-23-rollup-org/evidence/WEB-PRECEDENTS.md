# WEB-PRECEDENTS: earlier serial acquirers with a shared operating system

Outside research, done 23 November 2026. Nothing here comes from the library. "Read: part" means I read the passages quoted and the pages around them, not the whole document. Quotations were checked against the downloaded text unless the row says otherwise. Where a page was read through a fetch tool that returns extracts, the row says so.

## A. The centralised-system model: Danaher

### R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-01
- Claim: Danaher defines its system as tools plus culture, organised in four pillars, and says the system has outlasted every change in the portfolio.
- Kind: company claim
- Quote: "DBS is not only the set of business processes and tools our operating companies use on a daily basis in the pursuit of continuous improvement, but also represents our culture" ... "the DBS tools are organized into four pillars that are designed to apply to every aspect of our business: Growth, Lean, Leadership and the DBS Fundamentals." ... "While the operating companies that make up Danaher have changed over time, DBS continues to be the guiding philosophy for the Company."
- Where: Danaher 2025 Annual Report (10-K, Item 1), https://filecache.investorroom.com/mr5ir_danaher/974/Danaher%202025%20Annual%20Report.pdf , filed February 2026
- Speaker and interest: operator describing own firm, in a filing to investors.
- Read: part
- Bears on: parts 1, 9, 10; Q4
- Against Ben's starting positions: supports "orgs constantly self improving" as a workable aim over decades; says nothing on AI.

### R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-02

- Kind: company claim
- Quote: "We put the leadership team through a three-day training that covers all the basics of DBS." ... "we establish a target of having a strategic plan in place within 100 days of the closing of the transaction." ... "We send Danaher experts into that business for an eight-hour operating review every month."
- Where: Roth and Kleiner, "Danaher's Instruments of Change", strategy+business, 8 February 2016 (roundtable held 2012), https://www.strategy-business.com/article/danahers-instruments-of-change

## D. How often roll-ups fail, and why

### R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-29
- Claim: The most-quoted base rate is that over two-thirds of roll-ups create no value, but the source gives no study for it.
- Kind: analyst interpretation
- Quote: "Research says more than two-thirds of rollups fail to create any value for investors."
- Where: Carroll and Mui, Billion Dollar Lessons (2008), roll-ups chapter as reposted at https://www.valuewalk.com/rollups-by-their-very-nature-billion-dollar-lessons/ , 31 December 2016. The authors' HBR article "Seven Ways to Fail Big" (September 2008) rests on the same work; its roll-up section was behind a paywall.
- Speaker and interest: consultants and authors selling a book on failure; their sample was chosen as failures ("750 of the biggest U.S. business disasters").
- Read: part (through an extracting fetch of a repost)
- Bears on: parts 1, 4, 14; Q4
- Against Ben's starting positions: limits confidence in the vehicle. Treat the two-thirds figure as unsourced. The causes they list are debt, the need to keep a share price up, fraud, and promoters cashing out early.

### R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-30

- Where: Brown, Dittmar and Servaes, "Corporate Governance, Incentives, and Industry Consolidations", Review of Financial Studies 18(1), 2005, https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v18y2005i1p241-270.html

- Read: summary only

## What this adds up to

All of this section is inference.

1. Two different things have lasted. Danaher runs a taught, mandated method with a fixed entry sequence and monthly reviews. Constellation, Halma and Berkshire run autonomy plus capital discipline, with practice spread by coaches and peers. Both lasted for decades. What did not last was the middle: fast buying with a promised but unbuilt shared system (US Office Products, RSM Tenon).
2. In every lasting case the shared system was small. It was a routine, a few measures, a page of values and a way of teaching. None began from a full inventory of each firm's work. That limits Ben's Q1 starting position.
3. The improvement loop was carried by line managers who taught and were rated on it, and by pay tied to long-held equity. A planned exit visibly shortened behaviour at Constellation in 2011.
4. In UK local services the measured result of consolidation was higher prices, and the regulator then acted. A design should not count on that source of gain.
5. What transfers to an AI-heavy group: the entry sequence, the monthly review, post-deal reviews with base rates, a capped centre, long-held equity, finance systems first. What may not: the precedents spread practice through people. Agents make a playbook copyable at near-zero cost, which removes the main reason Constellation kept units separate; but it also moves liability and the client's trust towards the centre. No precedent tests this.
