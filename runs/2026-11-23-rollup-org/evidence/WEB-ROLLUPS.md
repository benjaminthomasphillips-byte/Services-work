# WEB-ROLLUPS: how AI-enabled roll-ups of services firms operate today

Outside research, web only. Compiled 23 November 2026.

**How to read the "Read" field.** Every page was fetched through a tool that returns an extract of the page made by a small model, not the raw page. "Part" means I read that extract, which covered the points I asked about; I did not see the whole page. "Summary only" means I saw only a search-result summary. Short quotations are as the tool returned them. They should be checked against the page before any is published. Where the tool paraphrased, I say "tool paraphrase" and give no quotation marks.

**Shared origins.** Almost every results figure below comes from the company or its investor. Press rows that repeat a company figure are the same piece of evidence, not a second one. I found no audited result for any AI roll-up except the Amex GBT proxy (financing only) and Teamshares' filings (seen only through summaries).

## What this adds up to

Inference, mine.

1. Two operating models are visible. Dwelly migrates each agency onto one system in about two months and closes offices. Current and Shield leave brand, leadership and often systems alone and lay tools on top. Long Lake claims the first at larger scale but discloses least. Nobody has published the cost of the latest integration against the first.
2. The job advertisements are the best evidence found. They show integration is a staffed function (Dwelly's Launch: about five businesses per manager) and that most of its work is data migration, accounting, leases, people decisions and client retention. The AI work is done by engineers embedded in firms, one workflow at a time. No source describes a full inventory of services as the first step.
3. Every results figure is self-reported, most are activity or capacity, and several change between tellings. No AI roll-up has published a margin with a baseline. The claim "proven" rests on the sponsors' word.
4. Capital has run ahead of evidence: debt is about half of Dwelly's funding and conventional buy-out debt funds the Amex GBT deal.
5. No failure of an AI roll-up was found. That is weak comfort; the oldest is under three years old and none reports audited results.
6. On continual improvement, the sources show feedback loops as an engineering intention (Long Lake) and firm-built agents (Shield). None shows a running improvement system or its governance.
