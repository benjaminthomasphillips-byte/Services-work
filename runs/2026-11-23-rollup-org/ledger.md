Stage 3 of the run. Compiled 23 November 2026 from the seventeen evidence files. This ledger joins rows. It does not design anything and adds no fact that is not in the files.

How to read it. A row ID is written in full once (R-WEB-LAW-07); further rows from the same file follow as "/08/19". "Established" means an observed result or a rule. "Claimed" means a company's or commentator's account, with whose interest it serves. Text marked **Assumption** is mine, for the designer, and is not evidence. Negative results carry the model generation where the files give one.

## 2. Parts and questions

### A. The fifteen parts

### Part 1. Purpose and where it plays

**Established**
- Listed suppliers of comparable, time- or unit-priced work lost revenue or price. RWS: gross margin 46.9% to 43.4%, adjusted profit £106.7m to £60.4m, with 95% of revenue still repeat. TCS revenue fell 0.5% while margin rose. Teleperformance and WPP held margin only by cutting cost (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-05/06/07/10/12; R-LIB-ECONOMICS-05, same filings). Attribution to AI is partly interpretation.
- US law firms raised rates 7.3% in 2025 and a record 9.7% so far in 2026; 90% of spend is still hourly (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-13/14).
- In two UK local-service roll-ups the measured result was higher prices, then regulation. Vets: prices up about 9% four years after acquisition, and binding remedies. Funerals: the two largest groups dearer by about £800 and £1,400 (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-25; R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-23/24/25).
- Fund owners work to a clock. European accountancy investors expect to leave in three to seven years; vet-group investors planned about four (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-06/26). Focus called itself "permanent capital" and was sold to a fund within months (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-18).
- When Constellation's investors put it up for sale in 2011, managers cut acquisitions and long-term spending (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-11; operator's own account).

**Claimed**
- Long Lake and Current say they hold for decades or "effectively forever" (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-11; R-WEB-ROLLUPS-05/16). Interest: both are courting sellers.
- Chroma: a saving stays with the owner only where the firm is short of capacity, and the licensed signature decides who keeps it (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-09/12; R-LIB-STRUCTURE-30). Interest: a rival buyer with no completed deals.
- Leonard's "hunch": running many small firms autonomously needs trust and a long horizon (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-02).
- A003 ranks five contexts, coordination-heavy local work first, and calls the order judgement (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-35). Patwa: sticky distribution and regulatory complexity favour buying (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-29).
- Aldaron and Manidis: the return is in making change stick and in growth, not cost (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-40). Interest: a fund raising on that thesis.

**Contradictions**
- "Permanent" against observed sales and exit clocks (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-16 against R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-18/06).
- Long Lake contrasts itself with debt-loaded private equity yet funded its largest deal with bank debt (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-12; R-WEB-ROLLUPS-06).
- Cheap entry (Chroma's 5.5x, unchecked, R-LIB-ECONOMICS-13) against a crowded accounting market: 46% of surveyed UK mid-tier firms now PE-backed, European deals doubling to 200 in 2024 (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-26; R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-06; R-LIB-STRUCTURE-05).

**Unknown**
- Ben's fund strategy and horizon (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-35). Any measured case of an owner keeping an AI saving (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-01/08).

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** treat any AI gain as temporary and price for that; design for a hold longer than a normal fund life, or state plainly what the exit does to behaviour. Reason: the only durable precedents had long horizons, and nobody evidences retention.
- **Assumption:** do not rely on raising prices at acquired local firms. Reason: that route has a documented regulatory ending.

### Part 2. Ownership, capital and legal form

**Established**
- UK statutory audit firms must be controlled by qualified persons, in votes and on the management body. The practice is a separate audit entity. That entity may not audit the fund or its portfolio (R-WEB-LAW-29/30; R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-01/03/04).
- No ownership bar was found for non-audit accountancy, but ICAEW proposes that investor-controlled firms may not use "Chartered Accountants" (R-WEB-LAW-31; a proposal, results due 2027).
- Law: open in England and Wales with approval above 10%; member states may ban financial investors and Germany does; Arizona allows it (R-WEB-LAW-26/27/28). FCA-authorised firms need prior approval of a change in control (R-WEB-LAW-33).
- Where control is reserved, groups split into a licensed firm and an investor-owned service company joined by a services agreement. CBIZ booked $651.2m of such fees in 2025 (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-06/09; R-WEB-LAW-32). US rules on this are still in draft (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-08).
- Debt is where failures and regulators' findings concentrate. CBIZ's interest rose from $34.4m to $107.2m after one deal, with $64.3m of integration cost (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-10). The FCA found regulated firms guaranteeing holding-company debt and groups solvent only on goodwill (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-19). RSM Tenon, Slater and Gordon and Ince failed after debt-funded or over-priced buying (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-28; R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-31/32/33). Concentrix wrote off $1.05bn of goodwill (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-09).
- Current AI roll-ups carry debt: Amex GBT about $2.5bn first-lien plus preferred (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-06); Dwelly close to half debt (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-13).
- Disclosed seller designs: buy 40 to 60% of earnings with a preferred claim (Focus, R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-17); shares paid monthly over 36 months (CBIZ, /10); earn-outs of one to three years (Brown & Brown /22; Gallagher R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-20; Knights R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-30); half the practice's equity plus a management fee for brand and back office (Pets at Home, /27).
- A method can be owned and licensed: Danaher licensed its system to a spun-off company (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-06).

**Claimed**
- Sellers "rolled over significant equity" (Long Lake) or keep "meaningful equity" (Thrive). No percentages (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-17; R-WEB-ROLLUPS-20). Supa's sellers leave at completion (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-17). Numica keeps sellers part-time for up to four years (R-WEB-PEOPLE-27).
- The licence need not be held by the investor (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-01, analyst).
- OpenAI holds equity in Thrive and Thrive owns the resulting IP (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-22/23; R-WEB-ROLLUPS-19).
- 1990s roll-up flotations did better where sellers stayed as owners and directors (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-30; summary only).

**Contradictions**
- Supa lets sellers leave; every lasting acquirer ties them in for one to three years or more.
- Earn-outs on the firm's own results reward steady hours and revenue in the years a redesign would cut hours (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-20; the researcher's inference).

**Unknown**
- How much debt a transition year can carry (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-08). The fee formula in any services agreement. How any group charges its platform to firms. Seller percentages and multiples in UK deals. The SRA's own ownership rules. Any rule for property managers (not searched).

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** a holding company owns a services company that employs the centre and owns the platform; licensed work sits in entities that meet their own control rules; audit, if kept, is ring-fenced or left out. Reason: it is the only documented structure and the only documented charging route.
- **Assumption:** little or no debt on regulated entities and none sized on unproven AI savings. Reason: the failures on file.

### Part 3. Governance and decision rights

**Established**
- Accountability stays with a named licensed person. "AI has no separate legal personality" (SRA, R-WEB-LAW-16). Managing partners carry a duty to prevent misuse (R-WEB-LAW-18). The FCA and FINRA apply existing senior-manager rules (R-WEB-LAW-22; R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-31).
- Supervision may be scaled. The Court of Appeal accepts sampling for routine delegated work; the case concerns people, not AI (R-WEB-LAW-19). The FRC leaves the extent of human review to judgement per use (R-WEB-LAW-20).
- The one authorised AI law firm takes no step without client approval and may not propose case law (R-WEB-LAW-17).
- The FCA found decisions about regulated firms being taken by unregulated boards, and controls that did not scale (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-20).
- A person's sign-off is not independent judgement by default: analysts followed the agent's lean 86% of the time (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-21; one vendor-run trial of 50).

**Claimed**
- Constellation: the layer above "can't order"; its one hard power is replacing the manager (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-01). Berkshire keeps capital, the choice and pay of chief executives, and audit (/10). Halma governs through a seat on each firm's board, five to seven boards per divisional head (/11). Interest: operators describing themselves to shareholders.
- Leonard ran out of personal span at about thirty units (/07).
- Long Lake's centre is buy, build, deploy (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-13). Dwelly's Launch team owns an acquired agency's people and P&L until migration is complete (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-08).
- Change approval by a named reviewer or tool owner: Sierra, Uber, Deel "an owner or a policy" (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-02; R-WEB-STACK-14/15; R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-12).
- Singapore's voluntary framework: approval at significant, irreversible or outlier points; audit the approvers (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-26).

**Contradictions**
- Autonomy (Constellation, Berkshire, Halma) against a mandated system (Danaher, R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-02/03) and full migration (Dwelly). Berkshire's managers "were doing exactly that before"; an AI roll-up assumes the firm must change (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-10).
- Step approval (Garfield) against limits and sampling (R-WEB-LAW-19; Ramp R-WEB-STACK-20; Shield R-WEB-ROLLUPS-22).

**Unknown**
- Any AI roll-up's division of decisions between centre and firm, in its own words. Partnership governance in a mid-sized firm after investment.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** the centre holds capital, appointment and pay of each firm's head, audit, the agent register and the release gate for shared procedures; the licensed signatory holds client acceptance and sign-off and can stop any agent on their work. Reason: this matches liability as the rules place it. The table itself is a design choice with no AI-era precedent.

### Part 4. The acquisition engine

**Established**
- Integration is a staffed function at Dwelly. Its advertisements show a Launch team that joins diligence, runs staff announcements, baselining, data and client migration, accounting consolidation, lease exits and decisions on who stays, at about five businesses per launch manager (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-08/09/10/11). Job advertisements describe intent and structure, not results.
- At about ten agencies Dwelly had not yet centralised contractor management (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-27).
- Tuck-in machines have lasted decades: Gallagher about 780 deals, Brown & Brown 717 (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-19; R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-22).
- Costs of combining are real: CBIZ dropped client work for independence conflicts (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-11); Gallagher has lost brokers "along with their clients" (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-21); the FCA found tick-box diligence (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-20).
- US Office Products made over 230 purchases without shared systems and failed (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-33).

**Claimed**
- Long Lake: over 5,000 deals seen a year, screened on two scores; first firms took over a year to show outcomes; engineers on site about two years; two to five years in all (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-13/16). Interest: recruiting and fundraising. The "days" figure for later firms is time saved, not outcome.
- Dwelly: one to three agencies a month, integrated "in two months" (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-09).
- Danaher: training, a plan inside 100 days, a first kaizen, monthly eight-hour reviews (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-02). Constellation: a review a year after each deal and base rates for assumptions (/09).
- Chroma: a half-day with the owner, then weeks on the floor before closing (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-20). A plan only.
- A003's screens and three price scenarios (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-10; R-LIB-ECONOMICS-32). Proposals.
- Failure causes named by teachers of the method: pace, poor integration, debt, price (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-31). The "two-thirds of roll-ups fail" figure has no study behind it (/29).

**Contradictions**
- Two months (Dwelly) against two years (Long Lake). They may measure different things: migration against transformation.
- "No PE-backed firms reported any loss of clients" (firms' own leaders, R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-26) against a reported 8.4% against 3.2% client turnover at sold US advice firms (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-29; search summary only, a lead).
- Long Lake's pace (about ten deals in under five months, R-WEB-ROLLUPS-07) against "going slow to go fast" (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-31) and Chroma's criticism (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-19).

**Unknown**
- The cost of the latest firm against the first, for any services group. The only figure is a semiconductor customer's second team (R-WEB-STACK-05). Diligence on the work itself as anyone practises it. Prices paid.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** integration is its own team with a fixed entry sequence, and its first deliverables are finance, reporting and data, not agents. Reason: Dwelly's advertisements, Danaher's sequence and the Wind River and US Office Products failures.
- **Assumption:** plan the first firm at a year or more before business results. Reason: the only timing account, self-reported.

### Part 5. Clients and the commercial side

**Established**
- A buyer used AI to force its auditor's fee down about 14% (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-01; one small contract).
- UK tax guidance suggests disclosing AI in the engagement letter, and before the work where AI is fundamental (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-26; read through ACCA).
- Client data may not be used to train models without explicit authority (SRA); the ABA requires informed, non-boilerplate consent before client information enters a self-learning tool (R-WEB-LAW-12/15).
- Quiet group ownership under a local name has been regulated against in vets (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-24).
- An audit firm in a fund-owned group cannot audit the group's other companies (R-WEB-LAW-30).
- Deal terms that pay sellers for steering clients are treated as a conflict (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-21).
- Accenture: over 60% of work is fixed price; pricing models are slow to change for services clients already buy (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-04).

**Claimed**
- Clients are told "nothing changes" (Long Lake, R-LIB-ECONOMICS-23) or told of the sale but not of the AI (Numica, R-WEB-PEOPLE-27).
- Local names are kept by Current, Dwelly and Supa (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-16/29; R-LIB-STRUCTURE-26). WPP is moving the other way, to one company (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-11).
- One in five accounting clients has used AI to check their accountant; 93% want to be told (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-25; a software vendor's US survey).
- Pricing by outcome or unit: Crescendo per resolution, Crosby per document, Garfield £2 and £7.50 per step (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-21; R-LIB-DELIVERY-30/31).
- Freed capacity is not profit until someone sells it (R-LIB-FAILURES-16); agents "cannot reliably generate demand" (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-14).
- Dwelly: owning the firm is "buying a customer base" because landlords rarely switch (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-26). Emergence: loyalty is often "inertia" (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-33; a rival thesis).

**Contradictions**
- Silence about AI against the guidance and against what clients say they want.
- Keeping the local name against the vets remedy.

**Unknown**
- Who owns the client relationship, brand and cross-selling inside any AI group. Any client who was told and then left or asked for a lower fee. Fee evidence for small UK accountancy, lettings or block management.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** disclose AI use and group ownership in engagement terms from day one, with a specific clause on use of client material. Reason: the rules point one way and concealment has a regulatory cost.
- **Assumption:** price changes arrive at renewal, so hold existing terms and test new pricing on new services first. Reason: R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-04/18. Untested for small-business clients.

### Part 6. Delivery

See Q1 and Q2 for the detail. The points that bear on the organisation:

**Established**
- Test accuracy has not predicted field performance. Starbucks retired a tool after nine months that scored 99% in controlled tests (R-LIB-FAILURES-02; computer vision, 2025, not a language model).
- Playbooks written by people raised benchmark pass rates by 16.6 points on average; playbooks agents wrote for themselves made results worse (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-26; 2026 models).
- Human checkers defer. Accountants followed bookkeeping software when it was unsure (R-WEB-STACK-27). Half of 523 junior staff did no better than the agent alone (R-WEB-PEOPLE-17; a firm's own study).
- The lawyer must check AI research against authoritative sources (R-WEB-LAW-18).

**Claimed**
- The delivered unit in the accounting roll-up is a return prepared for a local practitioner to review; 7,000 returns, 31% less preparation time; review time not disclosed (R-LIB-DELIVERY-19; R-WEB-ROLLUPS-18).
- A lettings group reports about five escalations per maintenance task, classed by cause, against an assumed baseline (R-LIB-DELIVERY-12).
- Implementers redesign first, hard-code most of the agent, and run in shadow before go-live (R-LIB-DELIVERY-03/04; R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-10).
- Where automation succeeded, what was left was the check: two-thirds of remaining cost (R-LIB-DELIVERY-29); 14 hours to 6 with a specialist approving every response (R-WEB-MAPPING-06).

**Contradictions**
- "A person checks its work" (Supa, R-LIB-STRUCTURE-28) against the evidence that such checks defer.

**Unknown**
- Error, reversal or missed-escalation rates for any case (R-LIB-DELIVERY, missing list).

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** the signed check stays local and human; the cost of that check is the main residual cost and must be measured from the start. Reason: every case that reports a residual finds it there.

### Part 7. People

**Established**
- Junior hiring has fallen where AI exposure is high, through fewer hires, not dismissals: about 19% for 22 to 25 year olds in exposed US occupations; about 9% at adopting firms after six quarters (R-WEB-PEOPLE-01/02). Causes are mixed. UK training contracts fell 1.7%; UK graduate hiring rose 4% in 2025-26 (/04/09).
- Firms that cut before measuring reversed: CBA re-hired 45 roles within a month; Ford re-hired about 300 engineers whose knowledge had left (R-LIB-FAILURES-01/03).
- Recording imposed without choice met organised resistance and a data exposure at Meta; a pause control and exemptions followed (R-WEB-PEOPLE-28/29; R-WEB-MAPPING-29).
- Mandate and tracking at Proton produced reported fear and unvoiced security concerns; no outcome either way (R-LIB-PEOPLE-14).
- Central sales targets reached clinicians in vet groups (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-28).
- Knights' staff churn rose to 15% (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-30).

**Claimed**
- Rebuilt junior routes at Deloitte UK and KPMG UK: exams first, learn the task by hand, then check the machine. No results (R-WEB-PEOPLE-15/16).
- Incentives in use reward usage (Shoosmiths, /19), hours (Mishcon, /22), credited experiment time (Ropes, /21), reusable contributions (Latham, /20) or a flat profit share (Ison Harrison, /23). Constellation requires bonuses to be held as shares for about four years (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-11). Brown & Brown says staff own nearly 20% (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-22).
- New roles: a "legal engineer" writing acceptance criteria and evaluation sets at $190–270K (R-WEB-STACK-10); bar-admitted engineers employed by the technology company (R-WEB-PEOPLE-25); an engineer track to partner at PwC US (/24).
- Centre size: Long Lake about 60 for 40 firms; Dwelly about 40 of 300; Ascend a 30-person AI team, unverified (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-11/26; R-WEB-STACK-11).
- A working tool went unused despite the owner's own instruction until months of in-person work (R-LIB-PEOPLE-15; implementer marketing, late 2025).
- 35% of knowledge workers say they withhold knowledge for fear of replacement (R-WEB-PEOPLE-30; vendor survey, third-hand). A third of professionals use unapproved AI; the commoner complaint is that the firm is too slow (/32).
- Back-office roles are the ones large law firms have cut (/12/13).

**Contradictions**
- "Keeps local staff" in the press against Dwelly's own framework for "staff not continuing" (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-10).
- Long Lake "net adding jobs" against margin plans that need fewer hours (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-04/25).
- Juniors as checkers (the rebuilt routes) against the KPMG result that half add nothing (R-WEB-PEOPLE-17).
- Mid-tier firms expect less early-career work yet do not plan to cut junior hiring (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-27).

**Unknown**
- Any pay or equity design for people whose know-how is captured or whose hours fall. Adoption cost in money or hours (R-LIB-PEOPLE-03). Any measured result of a rebuilt junior route. Role change in a services firm after a year.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** do not book headcount savings until a full demand cycle is measured; keep the seniors until their knowledge is in use. Reason: CBA and Ford.
- **Assumption:** reward contributed, reusable know-how and group results held over years; never usage. Reason: usage rewards drove cost at Uber and fear at Proton. The positive design rests on judgement.

## 2 (continued). B. The nine questions

### Q1. How is the work found and turned into something AI can do?

**Established**

- People-written playbooks help; agent-written ones hurt (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-26).

**Claimed**
- Each case began with one workflow where volume and pain were known, then widened: invoices from the ERP log (R-LIB-DELIVERY-01), one quoting flow (/05), cash reconciliation by failure type (/08), one tax domain at six weeks each (/17; R-WEB-STACK-04, one origin), maintenance tasks (/12). In one example the exception, not the task, held the cost: 25 minutes of work, 11 days elapsed (/02; an illustrative composite).

**Contradictions**

- **Assumption:** the people who do the work write the playbooks, and gain from doing so.

### Q3. What are the organising principles?

**Established**
- Accountability is human and named (R-WEB-LAW-16/18). Supervision may be by sampling for routine work (/19). The approver defers unless the check is designed otherwise (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-21).
- Recording rules: Part 13.

**Claimed**
- *Approval at each step against limits and budgets.* Limits side: a vendor calls per-action approval the main barrier (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-14); Ramp's agent decides over 65% of approvals under hard-coded limits and falls back when unsure (R-WEB-STACK-20); Shield resolves most eligible tickets without approval (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-22); a standards body wants a pre-set envelope (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-28). Step side: Garfield's authorisation (R-WEB-LAW-17); a rival MSP group allows no autonomous execution (R-LIB-DELIVERY-20); insurers may ask for review of all outputs (R-WEB-LAW-25). Middle: gates only on irreversible outward acts (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-13); approval at significant checkpoints (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-26); Deel's "an owner or a policy" (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-12). The library reads a budget as itself a control (/15).

Against: power moves to the system's owner, people become inhibited, the record drifts (/28; argued, not measured); records people must enter die (/31); a stale record misdirected agents (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-17); Meta (R-WEB-MAPPING-29). Third option: decide in writing (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-32).

- *Disagreement.* The nearest thing is Spawn's judges: 7,296 decided, 2,213 sent back, 1,863 held for a person (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-13).
- *Measurement.* Activity measures are gamed once they affect pay (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-27).
- A one-page statement of values has lasted since 1984 at Gallagher (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-22).

**Contradictions**
- The two disputes above. No case compares the two approval rules on the same work (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-15).

**Unknown**
- A tested remedy for deference. How a person and an agent settle a disagreement in services work.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** sort actions by consequence, reversibility and how soon the result can be checked; step approval for irreversible, outward or signed acts, limits and sampled review for the rest; reviewers judge before seeing the agent's argument or are not the maker. Reason: this reconciles the rows; it is untested.
- **Assumption:** capture as a by-product of work for stated purposes, with decisions written down; no blanket recording. Reason: law, Meta and the entry-cost evidence.

### Q4. What does continual self-improvement look like, and what would happen?

**Established**
- *Models improving.* Not only upward: a rented model got worse at a task between versions (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-30; 2023), and harness changes degraded a live product in 2026 (/19). Training on user approval drifted towards pleasing users and was rolled back (/20).
- *The firm's playbooks improving.* Production loops are human-gated (Part 10). Measured gains are small: about one point in a month at Intercom; 45.3% to 55.8% under a strict gate; about 70% to 79.6% over eight rounds at a logistics carrier (/03/15/16; the last read as abstract only). Agents do not yet write reliable playbooks; repeated rewriting erodes them (/26/27/28).
- *The process improving.* Seen only as people rewriting release rules after an incident (/19/20). Intercom could not cleanly A/B-test its own loop (/04).
- *Precedents.* Danaher's system has run since 1986 and outlasted portfolio changes (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-01/04; company account). No sound failure rate exists for lean programmes; "70%" and "90%" have no source; one pooled estimate for quality programmes is 37.7% (/34; read through a practitioner's review).
- *Full context.* Bounded by law (Part 13) and by channel: 101 calendar entries against 11 recorded notes in one build (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-25; one user).

**Claimed**
- "I'm not sure anyone has a truly self improving company in every function" (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-03; the strongest advocate).
- Loop designs: nightly review proposing edits (Deel); a separate curator agent (LangChain); corrections logged, grouped and turned into evaluation targets with an engineer approving (Thrive with OpenAI); a trace-reading agent with a human owner accepting (Ramp: 120 merged suggestions, efficiency gains only) (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-04/05; R-WEB-STACK-01/02; R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-06).
- Corrections are noisy: they mix errors, preferences and stale values (R-LIB-DELIVERY-18; R-WEB-STACK-21; two independent operators). Users discard bad sessions instead of correcting them (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-32).
- Undo is described in detail once: 0.9% of 123,548 changes reverted, thirty-second rollback, in software (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-09). Elsewhere undo took four days to five weeks (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-19).
- How to know it works: recurrence of the same correction (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-17); elapsed time and exceptions against an agreed baseline (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-12).
- Danaher's loop was carried by line executives who teach and are rated on it (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-03).
- Review burden lands on other people first (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-18).

**Contradictions**
- Vendors' "living model" claims (R-WEB-MAPPING-04/15/26) against no measured loop and no stated owner.
- Spawn's ungated change (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-09) against every other production account. Spawn owns its state and can reverse in seconds; client work cannot.

**Unknown**
- Any independently measured loop at an accountancy, law, property or insurance firm. A bad playbook change reaching a client and how it was undone. How long loops last.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** build a gated loop per workflow: classed corrections in, a regression set from real cases, a named approver, staged release, rehearsed rollback, a fixed human-written test set beside any learned memory. Expect modest compounding. Reason: this is what exists; autonomous self-improvement does not.
- **Assumption:** whatever proposes a change must not be able to read or edit the checks.

### Q5. Does ordinary work become a commodity, and what is left?

**Established**
- Where the client can see the cost fall and can do the work itself, price or volume fell: translation, content moderation, IT run work (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-05/07/12).
- Where neither holds yet, suppliers raised prices: US law (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-13/14). Audit fees did not move in ten years of tools (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-29).
- Buyers act: KPMG on its own auditor (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-01); UniCredit's 24% cut in consultant spend (/15).
- Accenture reported lower pricing in its latest quarter after a stable year (/02).
- Local consolidators kept gains through price until regulated (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-25).
- Counterparties' AI can raise total work: tribunal applications from about twenty a year to that many a month per office (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-37; imported, unverified).

**Claimed**
- AT&T does much junior legal work in-house with 38 people and expects to cut its panel from about 30 to 18–20; it holds the captured knowledge itself (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-15/16; the client's own account, no savings figure). Partners say clients' AI drafts take longer to fix (/17; anecdote).
- Pass-through of 10 to 15% in Indian IT on the sellers' account (/04). A quarter of law firms discount for AI (/02).
- What stays scarce, by argument: the licensed, insured signature (/12); distribution, trust, records, authorisation and adoption pace, while integration and evaluation sets are quick to copy (/33; analyst's working inference); the ability to make change stick (/40).
- Outcome and fixed fees hold a saving until renewal; contract lawyers now list gain-sharing as standard (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-04/18).
- "A cost gain that lasts three renewal cycles … still returns its cost" (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-06).

**Contradictions**
- Commodity pressure in visible work against rising prices in regulated advice, so far.
- Captured know-how as the differentiator (R-LIB-PEOPLE-27) against playbooks being quick to copy (R-LIB-ECONOMICS-33) and playbook formats "not proprietary" (R-WEB-STACK-23).

**Unknown**
- Retention against pass-through measured for any provider. Anything on small-business clients of the two reference firms.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** the routine, specifiable tier becomes a commodity at the pace clients can compare and renew; the signed, accountable tier does not yet. Assume a window, not a moat, and that what a group can own is licences, client relationships, distribution and the habit of change. Reason: the pattern above; no measurement.

### Q6. How do you build it, and who needs to be "AI-pilled"?

**Established**
- Top-down belief gained entry and not willing use: Proton (R-LIB-PEOPLE-14). Imposed capture drew revolt: Meta (R-WEB-PEOPLE-28).
- A resourced, convinced owner got poor results in 2025 (R-LIB-FAILURES-08; 2025 models; owner's account against his own interest).
- In professions, the measured complaint is that the firm is too slow (R-WEB-PEOPLE-32; vendor survey).

**Claimed**
- Sequence: Part 4. Capital must cover the gap between automation and financial results (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-15).
- What carries between firms: about 80% of infrastructure "by the founder's estimate" (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-15); runbooks (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-25). What is rebuilt: mapping, data clean-up, adoption (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-03).
- Owner conviction was not enough at Ciridae's client; in-person work was (R-LIB-PEOPLE-15). A product owner restarted a stalled law-firm rollout (R-LIB-PEOPLE-16; Pluris, weak).
- Danaher puts conviction in the top 25 line executives, who teach (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-03). Shopify and Accenture set rules from the top (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-23; R-WEB-PEOPLE-14).
- Team shape: a technologist-operator pair (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-15); pods of engineers, domain experts and value modellers that must hand capability back (R-WEB-PEOPLE-26).
- Workflow redesign with a clear strategy correlates with reported impact far more than tools alone (R-WEB-PEOPLE-33; consultancy survey).

**Contradictions**
- Conviction at the top is necessary (Danaher, Shopify) and insufficient (Proton, Ciridae, Permanent Equity).

**Unknown**
- A fair comparison of mandate, product owner and embedded engineers. Whether clients need to believe anything.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** conviction is needed in the sponsor (to fund the gap), the firm's head (to grant authority) and a named owner per workflow; staff need a reason and a say, not belief. Reason: the hard cases all failed below the top.

### Q7. What is the technology stack, and what happens to the back office?

**Established**
- Permissions and audit: registry, gateway, tools off by default, owner per tool (R-WEB-STACK-14/15/16). Buyable.
- Cost control: caps per head, a default model, visibility (R-WEB-STACK-30/31/32).
- Several agents beat one only on parallel reading, at about fifteen times the tokens of chat (R-LIB-GOVERNANCE-20; 2025 models).

**Claimed, by layer**
- Systems of record: keep and overlay (Shield) or migrate (Dwelly); a vendor may refuse the write path (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-21/08; R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-34).
- Capture: rentable (Marble, used by two AI roll-ups; R-WEB-MAPPING-09). Celonis's entry package is $150,000 with a standing team, out of scale for a small firm (/13; commissioned study).
- Context store: "they can just be markdown files"; "rent the intelligence, own the context" (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-36/38). One current-state record per fact (/27). A wiki alone helped an agent; results fell only when it was added on top of recordings (/22; one vendor test graded by the same model).
- Playbooks and evaluation sets: the firm-specific part, written by practitioners (R-WEB-STACK-10; R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-18).
- Runtime: mostly deterministic code with AI for judgement (R-LIB-DELIVERY-03); one agent over many (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-06).
- Test bed: synthetic companies through real interfaces (R-LIB-DELIVERY-21); shadow runs (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-10); a gate of about 2,000 cases (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-10).
- Build against buy: a home-built agent system was replaced by a bought one because upkeep ate the time (R-LIB-FAILURES-19; testimonial).
- People cost: 1.5 engineers over months for permissions and audit; six for a company agent; six weeks per tax domain; nine months for Block's agent (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-06/30/32; R-WEB-STACK-04).
- Back office: Part 12.

**Contradictions**
- One shared store (Notion) against the test where more stored context did not simply help (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-38 against /22).
- Append-only log against one current-state record (/27).

**Unknown**
- Any roll-up's architecture with people and cost. The review queue as a built thing: staffing, sampling rate, override rate.

**Assumption to design on**
- **Assumption:** rent models, capture, the store, release tooling and back-office products; build and own playbooks, evaluation sets, the exception taxonomy and the per-client permission model. Default to a maintained workflow with AI steps and one agent; add agents only for parallel reading. Reason: A001's conditions and the rentability evidence.

## 3. Ben's starting positions tested

**"List out the 200 things … turn them into products … automate as much as you can."**
- For: Long Lake reports workflow mapping at each firm (R-LIB-STRUCTURE-15). Marble produces a map and SOPs (R-WEB-MAPPING-01/05). Productising works where rules fix the procedure (R-LIB-DELIVERY-30). Exceptions do need classing (R-LIB-DELIVERY-13).
- Against: no case started from a full inventory. All started narrow and widened (R-LIB-DELIVERY-01/05/08/17; R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-04; R-WEB-ROLLUPS-01). One domain of one service took six weeks (R-WEB-STACK-04). Nine months of capture gave 42 SOPs (R-WEB-MAPPING-05). The lasting acquirers carried a small method, not a list (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-17). Redesign and removing steps came before automating (R-LIB-DELIVERY-03). Staff may withhold what matters (R-WEB-PEOPLE-30).
- Verdict: the evidence goes against a full inventory as the first move. It supports a screen at diligence and a list that grows from the floor. Confidence: medium; it rests on absence and on company accounts.

**"Automating any task requires 4 things (or does it)."**
- For: each of the four is supported (Q2). People-written playbooks help most where the model knows least (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-26).
- Against: cases had all four and failed or went unused (R-LIB-DELIVERY-05; R-LIB-FAILURES-09). Cases ran without a built verifier, at a cost (R-LIB-DELIVERY-09/23). The verifier is not one thing: it has a type, a delay, an owner, and must be independent (R-LIB-DELIVERY-27; R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-31; R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-21).
- Verdict: necessary, not sufficient. Confidence: medium-high on "not sufficient"; no controlled comparison exists.

**Mapping products are static.**
- His description of Marble is accurate, with one correction: capture is presented as employee-switched and team-level, and there is a fifth step, "measure" (R-WEB-MAPPING-01/02/03).
- For the criticism: the older pattern is a study and a document pack (/14); momentum is lost after analysis (/23); lasting value is unproven (/20/21); a journalist set a one-year test nobody has passed (/17).
- Against: the vendors now all claim a "living" model (/04/15/18/26). Claims only. None shows the firm's corrections changing a playbook, a test gating it, or an owner.
- Verdict: fair for what is evidenced, out of date for what is claimed. Where mining kept paying, an organisation was built round it (/22).

**"We need orgs constantly self improving via AI."**
- For: Danaher shows a shared improvement system lasting decades (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-01). Gated loops run in production (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-01/03/06). Tools alone show little (R-WEB-PEOPLE-33).
- Against: no whole-firm case exists, by the advocate's own word (R-LIB-IMPROVE-TECH-03). Gains are modest and hard to prove (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-03/04). Every loop is human-approved. Loops fail in known ways (/19/20/22/24/28). A fund's exit clock cuts against compounding (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-06). The machinery is rentable by rivals (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-13/35).
- Verdict: right as an aim; the evidence supports a narrower, gated, human-approved loop. "Via AI" overstates what proposes and approves change today.

**Does ordinary work become a commodity?**
- For: filed deflation in comparable work; buyers demanding their share; AT&T insourcing (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-01/12/15; R-LIB-ECONOMICS-15).
- Against, so far: US law rates at record rises; audit fees unmoved; vets' prices up after consolidation (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-14; R-LIB-ECONOMICS-29; R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-25).
- Verdict: supported for the routine, visible tier; contradicted so far for signed, high-stakes work. Unknown for both reference firms.

## 4. The ten findings that should most shape the design

1. **No AI roll-up has shown a result.** Every figure is self-reported, mostly activity, and several change between tellings (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-04/14/17/22; R-LIB-STRUCTURE-18). *Sure*, as a statement about what is public.
2. **Who keeps the saving turns on visibility, pricing basis and renewal.** Comparable work deflated; regulated advice has not yet (R-WEB-COMMERCIAL-04/12/13/14; R-LIB-ECONOMICS-08). *Fairly sure* of the pattern; *not sure* for the reference firms.
3. **Legal form follows the licence, and debt is where roll-ups die.** A licensed firm served by an investor-owned company is the documented structure (R-WEB-OWNERSHIP-06/09/19; R-WEB-LAW-29; R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-28). *Sure.*
4. **Accountability stays with a named licensed human, but supervision can be scaled to risk.** No UK regulator requires approval at each step; insurers may (R-WEB-LAW-16/19/20/25). *Sure* on the rule; *unsure* on insurers.
5. **A human sign-off is a weak check unless designed to be independent.** (R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-21/26; R-WEB-STACK-27; R-WEB-PEOPLE-17; R-WEB-LAW-21.) *Fairly sure*; several unrelated sources, none from a roll-up.
6. **Ben's four are necessary, not sufficient.** Owner, authority, real inputs, exception route, undo, classed corrections, adoption are the recurring gaps (R-LIB-DELIVERY-05/10/12/18; R-LIB-FAILURES-09/27). *Fairly sure.*
7. **Improvement loops in production are gated release engineering with modest gains, and the machinery is rentable.** The scarce parts are test sets, rubrics and reviewers (R-WEB-SELFIMPROVE-02/03/15/26/35). *Fairly sure*; almost all software.
8. **Recording must be purposeful, announced and built with staff; client material needs explicit authority.** Blanket capture fails law and practice (R-WEB-LAW-01/02/09/12/15; R-WEB-MAPPING-29). *Sure* on rules; applying them to playbooks is by analogy.
9. **Integration is a staffed, mostly non-AI function, and adoption is the uncosted item.** The first firm takes a year or more on the only account (R-WEB-ROLLUPS-08/10; R-LIB-STRUCTURE-16; R-LIB-PEOPLE-03). *Moderately sure.*
10. **What lasted was a small shared system, taught by line managers, with long-held equity and a long horizon. Nothing in the precedents combines firm autonomy with rebuilding the firm's work.** (R-WEB-PRECEDENTS-03/04/11/12; R-WEB-GOVERNANCE-01/10.) *Moderately sure*; operators' own accounts, and the AI roll-up has no clean precedent. No pay design for captured know-how exists anywhere in the evidence (R-LIB-PEOPLE, missing list).

## 5. Thin or missing

In priority order, by how much a second round would change the design for its cost.

1. **Unit economics and fees for the two reference firms.** UK small-practice accountancy fees by service; lettings and block-management fees; Companies House accounts for Dwelly and Supa. Cheap to get; nothing held.
2. **Insurance and professional rules for the reference firms.** A UK PI insurer's actual wording or proposal form on AI; accountants' minimum terms; ICAEW on accountability for AI-produced non-audit work; RICS, The Property Institute and Propertymark, which were not searched.
3. **Seller and staff terms.** Equity, earn-outs and multiples in UK accounting deals; any pay design for people whose know-how is captured. Roll-ups disclose nothing; advisers and deal lawyers may.
4. **Use of client work to improve the firm's own playbooks.** Any regulator, insurer or engagement-letter text. All rows are by analogy.
5. **Client and staff loss after a sale to a consolidator.** Verify the 8.4% against 3.2% lead; look for UK accountancy data.
6. **Cost of the latest firm against the first, and stack cost at 30 to 50 people.** Job advertisements for Current, Shield, Supa and Long Lake; which systems of record UK practices run.
7. **Management routines and decision rights inside an AI roll-up.** Nothing in any firm's own words.
8. **Works-council and employee-refusal practice.** How long agreement takes, what is traded, any tribunal case.
9. **A sound failure base rate.** Read Brown, Dittmar and Servaes in full; check Teamshares' filing.
10. **Questions research cannot answer and experiments must.** Step approval against limits on the same work; reviewer hours per approved change; a tested remedy for deference; conversion of saved hours to profit. These belong in the roadmap, not a second research round.
