# Review from the captured employee's seat

Reviewer seat: (a) a senior employee of an acquired firm, fifteen years in, not a partner, whose know-how is being written down and whose hours are being removed; (b) a second-year trainee. Document reviewed: `design/draft-v1.md` only, read in full. 23 November 2026.

## Verdict

From this seat the design asks for everything early and pays late, conditionally and mostly to other people. The senior's knowledge is captured in days 100 to 365; the job pledge lapses at month 12 to 18; the only reward for captured know-how that is more than a courtesy (the reuse award) cannot be paid before a second firm exists, only if the firm-two test passes, and never for client rules, which are what a senior bookkeeper actually writes. The sellers get cash at completion. The junior gets "the training contract honoured" and a design whose only counted gain is "an avoided hire, an unreplaced leaver", which describes them on the day they qualify. Worse for the design: its own stop rule makes the status quo the result of failure, and the measure that triggers it (review time per unit) is produced by the people whose hours depend on it. A sensible senior does not need to sabotage anything. They review carefully, class corrections as judgement, keep the awkward client exceptions in their head, and decline to own the agent's version. The design has no answer except to stop, which is what they wanted. Until the success state is plainly better for staff than the failure state, Part 7 is a statement of good intentions and the weakest premise in the Thesis is being tested by people paid to see it fail.

## What is in it for each, as written

**The senior (fifteen years, manager or senior bookkeeper, not a signatory).**
- Gets: a written pledge that lasts one demand cycle; time on rule-writing "credited as chargeable"; their name on the work; "first call" on reviewer, exception and client roles; a flat share of a profit pool that Part 14 says will be lower; if made a workflow owner, a pay step (size open), group units held about four years in a group with no planned exit, and a "right to buy into the firm over time" (price and funding unstated).
- Gives: the client-by-client rules that make them hard to replace; "a year of extra effort" (the agreement table's own words); acceptance of a per-reviewer timer, seeded-error tests and classed corrections; and the partnership route that existed before three partners sold the majority.

**The second-year trainee.**
- Gets: the contract honoured; intake held in year one; a training route (by hand on closed files, then checking the machine against known answers) that Deloitte describes "with no results".
- Gives: the lines they were learning on, which are handed over first; a measured personal catch rate; and any expectation of a seat on qualifying. The pay table's entry for them under "Paid on" is a dash.

## Ranked objections

### 1. The stop rule hands staff a veto, and the design measures success with numbers the affected people produce
**Where:** Thesis ("The weakest premise"; "What would change the view"); Part 14 stop rules; Part 9 measures 3 to 5; Part 4 days 1 to 100; Part 6 item 13; Roadmap test 2.
**What is wrong:** If review time per unit has not fallen after one cycle, the rule is "stop building; run the firm as bought". For a senior whose hours are the target, that is the best available outcome, and the pledge covers them until exactly that reading is taken. Review time is baselined in the first hundred days by reviewers who have been told on day one what it will be used for. Corrections are classed "error, preference or stale value" by the reviewer who made them. Seeded-error catch rates reward slow, thorough review. Every incentive at the reviewer's desk points toward the premise failing honestly, with nobody behaving badly. The draft names deference as a risk to the check; it does not name the opposite risk, defensive over-review by people protecting their work and their sign-off.
**What would have to change:** Make success pay staff more than failure does, in terms they can read on day one: a stated share of the measured gain per case to the people on that case, and a pledge that lengthens (not lapses) where a case succeeds. Take the baseline independently, from re-performance and timing in diligence and from system timestamps, not from self-timed review after the announcement. Have a second person class a sample of corrections. State in the Thesis that the first-cycle reading is a test of staff incentive as much as of the check, and say what the design does if review time stays flat while seeded-error catch rates are high.

### 2. The job pledge is a deferral of about a year, is not a non-negotiable, and the plan's only counted gain is fewer people
**Where:** Part 7 "The job pledge"; Part 3 non-negotiables and decision table ("hiring and pay below the head: firm decides"); Part 14 "What counts as a gain"; Thesis decision 1; "Where the parts strain".
**What is wrong:** The pledge says no role is removed "because of a handover until a full demand cycle has been measured", then reductions come "first" from avoided hires and leavers. "First" is not "only". It says nothing about hours cut, regrading, a role redefined out from under someone, performance management on the new measures, or non-retention of a trainee at qualification. It is absent from the list of non-negotiables, so the centre cannot audit it and the group partner cannot order on it; the head, who holds a minority and whose staff-retention payments end after about three years, decides. The Thesis admits that with an exit clock the pledge is "the first thing to give way". The draft also admits that in a demand-limited firm the pledge and the economics "pull apart" and offers nothing. Staff will read all of this correctly: the capture period and the protected period are the same period.
**What would have to change:** Put the pledge on the non-negotiable list, with the head of quality or the controller auditing it. Write the second half as terms, not tone: what happens after the cycle (a named alternative role with pay protected for a stated period, or a stated severance multiple well above statutory, at the person's choice), who decides, and that the same applies to hours reductions and regrading. Make it survive a change of owner by contract. For demand-limited firms, either do not buy or say plainly that the pledge is not offered.

### 3. The pay design for captured know-how does not reach the people whose know-how is captured
**Where:** Part 7 pay table ("Anyone who writes client rules, playbooks or test sets"; "Workflow owner"); Part 2 "Who owns the know-how" and "Sellers"; Part 10 "What may be shared"; Part 14 centre economics.
**What is wrong:** Four separate holes. (a) The reuse award is paid "when their playbook passes the gate and runs at another firm". Client rules "never" pass the gate (Parts 2, 8, 10, 11). Bookkeepers and payroll staff "write client rules". So the largest group of authors is excluded from the award by construction. (b) The award "cannot be paid" until a second firm exists, and no library exists unless the firm-two test passes: at least two years out, on a coin the author does not control. (c) It is paid in group units "held about four years" in a holding company with "no planned exit" that "loses money by design"; nothing says how a unit ever becomes money. (d) The senior's old route to equity, partnership, was sold at completion for cash to three people; the replacement is a "right to buy" at no stated price. "Time credited as chargeable" and "their name on the work" are not pay; the first only stops rule-writing counting against them. The draft says honestly that this part is judgement. From this seat the judgement is that knowledge is free.
**What would have to change:** Pay in cash, at the firm, at the time of capture and while the thing runs: a fee on acceptance of a rule file or playbook by its owner, and a running payment while it stays in use at the author's own firm, whether or not it is ever shared. Give client-rule authors their own instrument since the gate excludes them. Define how units pay out (dividend right, buy-back formula, dates). State the terms of the right to buy, and offer non-signatory seniors of long service a small local stake at completion, funded from the sellers' proceeds or the group, since they hold the client relationships the price paid for.

### 4. The roles offered to the senior are the ones the eight measures are built to shrink, and nobody has counted the seats
**Where:** Part 7 firm chart and "Roles"; Part 9 measures 3 and 5; worked example, Firm A "People".
**What is wrong:** The senior is offered "first call on reviewer, exception and client roles". Success is defined as review time per unit falling and the same correction not recurring, that is, less reviewing and fewer exceptions. Client-lead work is described as "selling capacity", which is the partners' job and not a skill the design tests for or trains. The worked example accounts for four managers, the bookkeepers as a class, and seven trainees; it does not say how many reviewer and exception seats a 30-person firm has in year three on the plan's own assumptions, or who fills them. "Growth is meant to absorb them" is the whole answer, resting on what the draft calls a weak basis (one rival buyer's hypothesis).
**What would have to change:** A seat map as a required output of the first hundred days: every current person, by name, against a role in the year-three firm under two cases (capacity sold; capacity not sold), shown to staff. If the map shows fewer seats than people, say so then, alongside the terms in objection 2. Add a tested route into client work for seniors who have never sold, or stop listing it as a destination.

### 5. For the junior, the training route and the definition of a gain point in opposite directions
**Where:** Part 7 "Juniors" and pay table; Part 14 "What counts as a gain" and Firm A table ("training explicit"); Part 7 "One, five, thirty" (career path).
**What is wrong:** (a) The only gains the design will count are "an avoided hire, an unreplaced leaver" or sold capacity. A trainee whose contract ends at qualification is the cheapest unreplaced leaver in the building; the pledge does not cover a job that was never offered. (b) Intake is "held in year one" only. (c) Training becomes "an explicit cost line" of 2,300 hours in a year when profit is down and the staff pool is a share of profit, so colleagues are given a reason to resent it. (d) Whether time spent "checking the machine against known answers" counts as practical work experience for the professional body is not asked, though the insurer and the institute are asked about everything else. (e) A second-year is mid-contract: the route does not say whether they redo a cycle by hand or carry on. (f) Each trainee's catch rate is measured "before their check counts", and the draft itself cites that a quarter of juniors do worse than the agent alone; the consequence of a poor score is unstated. (g) The promised career path, reviewer to owner to resident to group partner, exists "at thirty", which Part 1 says is "a decade or more away". The quiet response is obvious: do the exams, qualify at the group's expense, leave. The design loses the reviewers it says it needs most (measure 7, "regretted leavers, reviewers first").
**What would have to change:** Add to the written questions in diligence one to the institute on whether the new route satisfies training requirements. Commit to intake for the pledge period, not one year, and to a stated offer rate or a minimum notice on qualification. Fund training from the group in the transition years so it is not taken from the pool. Say what a low catch rate leads to (more by-hand time, not exit). Give trainees a line in the pay table.

### 6. Reviewers are measured as individuals, which contradicts the design's own principles and invites gaming
**Where:** Part 8 principles 6 and 11 and "An employee who refuses to be recorded"; Part 9 measure 3 ("by reviewer") and 4; Part 6 "The check, designed" ("a timer on review"); Part 7 pay table (reviewers).
**What is wrong:** "Record the work, not the worker" sits beside review time "by reviewer", a timer, personal seeded-error catch rates and pay on "disagreements with the agent that prove right on audit". The draft says an employee who refuses recording "has little to refuse". A reviewer has a great deal to refuse and no stated right to. Nothing bars these records from use in redundancy selection once the pledge lapses; a senior will assume that is their purpose. Paying on proven disagreements rewards disagreeing; paying on catch rate while watching the timer rewards whichever is being looked at this month. Principle 10 requires a hand-written line for every disagreement, and the draft itself cites that "records people must enter by hand die".
**What would have to change:** Report review time and acceptance by case, not by person, at board level; keep per-person catch rates for training and qualification of a reviewer only. Put the timer and seeded files through the same stated-purpose process as other capture. Add a written bar on using correction logs, timings and catch rates for selection or discipline for a fixed period, enforced as a non-negotiable. Capture the reason for a disagreement inside the review screen, not as a separate note.

### 7. Question C gives every manager a free veto, and saying yes is unpriced and exposed
**Where:** Part 6 question C; Part 8 principle 1 and "Review and retirement"; Part 7 pay table (workflow owner); "Events" (an agent's error reaches a client).
**What is wrong:** "If nobody will own it, it is not handed over." There is no cost to declining and no account of what the centre does when all four managers decline, or accept and let the case idle. The cost of accepting is real: weekly exception reads, the playbook, the test set, quarterly presentations, on top of a client book, for a pay step of unstated size. The owner is paid partly on "capacity sold", which Part 5 gives to partners. When an agent's error reaches a client the owner "stops the workflow, rolls back, re-reviews"; whether the owner carries personal disciplinary or professional exposure for an agent's mistake is not said. And an agent is retired when "its sponsor leaves and nobody takes it on", so one resignation ends a case: the owner holds the lever and the design has no deputy.
**What would have to change:** Write the owner's bargain down: the pay step as a figure or a formula, time released from client work, a statement that an error within approved limits and with the check run is the firm's, not the owner's. Take "capacity sold" out of the owner's pay or give the owner the selling. Require a named deputy per case. Say what happens at day 100 if no manager will own any of the three cases, since that is a more likely first-firm outcome than the ones the stop rules cover.

### 8. Withholding is rewarded and cannot be seen
**Where:** Part 7 (35% withhold knowledge, cited and then left); Part 6 item 2 and "Exceptions"; Part 10 "How you would know it works".
**What is wrong:** The exception handler is the person the unwritten rule routes to. Every rule a senior leaves out of the client file is an exception that arrives on their desk and justifies their seat; every rule they write removes one. The test of a playbook is "corrections falling", which a senior can satisfy on the easy rules while classing the hard ones as "judgement", one of the four permitted exception causes. Authors are paid on "adoption, and corrections falling", that is, on the shrinking of their own next job. The design has no test of whether a rule file is complete.
**What would have to change:** Test completeness directly: a second person (or the trainee, which also serves objection 5) does the client's work from the rule file alone on closed files, and the gaps are counted. Review the "judgement" class by sample with someone outside the case. Break the link between unwritten rules and job security by making the protection in objection 2 independent of exception volume, and pay the author for exceptions retired, by name, in cash.

### 9. Staff have no voice and no route to enforce anything promised to them
**Where:** Part 3 "Boards" and decision table; Part 4 day one and days 1 to 100; Part 1 exclusion of Germany and the Netherlands; Part 7 "Who needs to believe".
**What is wrong:** The draft says staff need "a reason, a say and a guarantee". The say is consultation on "what will be logged". The firm board is the head, "one or two senior staff owners" (who hold group units and pay steps tied to the programme) and the group partner. Nobody represents the bookkeepers, administrators or trainees. No row in the decision table covers who decides a role is removed, how a breach of the pledge is raised, or who hears it. The head of quality can stop an agent but has no remit over people. The design avoids works-council countries until it has "run a works-council process once" and has nowhere planned to run one, which reads from the floor as avoiding the one arrangement that would bind it.
**What would have to change:** An elected staff representative attending the monthly firm board for the people and measures items. A row in the Part 3 table for "removing or materially changing a role after a handover", with the holding board as the stop. A route to the head of quality or the holding board for a claimed breach of the pledge or of principle 6. Consultation before the three cases are chosen, not only on logging.

### 10. The "everyone" reward is a share of a profit the plan itself lowers, and from year two staff help pay for the engineer
**Where:** Part 7 pay table ("Everyone in the firm"); Part 14 Firm A table (profit "lower; lower again"); Part 2 "Charging".
**What is wrong:** The only reward that reaches every employee is "a flat share of a firm profit pool". Part 14 says year-one profit falls, then falls again when partners move to salary, with staff cost up and review time possibly rising. From year two the firm "pays at cost for what it chooses to keep", including a share of an engineer the draft calls "the largest new cost", out of the same profit. So the staff's share shrinks in the years they carry the extra effort, and the more of the programme the firm keeps, the smaller it gets. The pool's size and whether it existed before the deal are not stated.
**What would have to change:** Floor the pool for the transition years at a stated amount, funded by the group as a cost of the deal like the resident and engineer. Calculate it before group charges and before the partners' salary change. State the size. If no pool existed before, say so and say what it replaces.

## Where I would quietly not cooperate, in one list

| Who | What they do | What the draft does about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Senior reviewer | Reviews every file fully and carefully; review time does not fall | Stops the programme at month 12 to 18, which is what they wanted |
| Senior bookkeeper | Writes the easy client rules; keeps the awkward ones as "judgement" exceptions | Nothing; no completeness test |
| Manager | Declines to own the agent's version, or accepts and lets it idle | "Not handed over"; no consequence, no escalation |
| Signatory | Uses the stop right on their own work "without giving a reason first" | Reviewed afterwards; no limit |
| Any reviewer | Classes their own corrections to suit | Nothing; self-classed |
| Workflow owner, once the case is live | Resigns; the agent is retired with them | Nothing; no deputy |
| Trainee | Qualifies and leaves | Counted as a regretted leaver; no retention term |
| Everyone | Says the right things at the quarterly review | "If changes are many and these do not move, the loop is theatre" - noticed, not prevented |

The resident "does not manage" and the group partner "may order only on a non-negotiable", so none of this can be ordered away, and none of it is misconduct.
