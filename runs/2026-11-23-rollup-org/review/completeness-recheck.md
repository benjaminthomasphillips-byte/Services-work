# Completeness review, second pass

Reviewer: completeness. Date: 23 November 2026. Read: section 3 of the brief (the fifteen parts) and `design/final.md` from the title to the end of "Challenges and open questions" in full. I did not re-read the Sources list or the revision history, and I did not check any quotation; that is the evidence auditor's job. I looked at the first review's table only to see which parts it had marked partly decided.

## Verdict

No part is missing. Three of the fifteen are partly decided: Part 7 (People), Part 13 (Risk, regulation and liability) and Part 14 (Economics). Twelve are decided, and every part is shown at one firm, five and thirty, though thinly at thirty for five of them. That matches the count the design gives against itself in its coverage table, so the table is honest. All eleven required contents are present. The design may be filed under the brief's rule ("do not file while any part is marked missing").

The remaining weakness is no longer a missing part. It is fit between parts. The design says the whole return must come from the conventional plan, and then pays nobody for delivering it. It pays the people on a converted case from a gain that is measured largely by their own review minutes, and names nobody to calculate or certify that gain. It makes a long list of open-ended promises (an uncapped indemnity, a three-year job pledge, a funded pool floor, puts, premium loading) from a holding company that by its own arithmetic loses money and may not borrow. And one stop rule fires in the wrong direction in the event the thesis itself names as a view-changer. The nine events are mostly answered; I found 31 places where the document does not say who does what, of which about eight matter.

A note on thirty. Part 4's pace rule allows one firm until about month 18, then at most two in their first 18 months at once. That is about four firms every three years. Either the pace rule is expected to loosen, and the design should say when and who decides, or the thirty-firm column describes an organisation beyond any planning horizon and should be labelled so.

## 2. Pairs of parts that contradict or do not fit

Ranked. The design's own list ("Where the parts strain against each other") names six; I do not repeat those except where I add something.

1. **Part 14 against Parts 2 and 7: the return must come from the conventional plan, and nobody is paid for it or put in charge of it.** Part 14 says the purchase "must earn its return on A alone", delivered by "the head and a resident who is an operator". The head's salary is fixed; deferred consideration is paid on a profit floor and "never ... on short-term profit above the floor"; the growth payment is for net new fees only. Billing leakage, lock-up and repricing are rewarded only through the head's minority dividend. The resident's role is defined in Part 7 as "method, baselines, coaching; does not manage". So the base case has an owner in name, no executive authority behind it and little pay attached.

2. **Part 7 against Parts 6 and 10: the people who produce the measurement share in the result.** "Everyone on a converted case" receives a share of the case's measured gain. Reviewers are on the case. The gain is, for the most part, a fall in attention per unit, which is their own review minutes. The same table says reviewers are paid on "the role, not a score" and never on review time. Both cannot hold. The design's weakest premise is reviewer deference; this pays for it. The blind sample and the catch rate are the safeguard, but the size of the sample and who re-performs it are not set. Principle 4 ("held by someone not paid on its result") is also breached where the firm quality lead is a signatory, since signatories receive a growth payment on capacity sold, and where a full-time head of quality at five firms holds phantom units paid on "the eight measures across firms", which are the measures that person audits.

3. **Parts 13, 7, 2 and 5 against Part 14: a centre the economics cannot pay for has made open-ended promises.** The holding company gives an uncapped indemnity for shared items; pays any AI-attributable rise in premium and excess for three years; funds the job pledge, the pool floor, the backfill, the opt-out manual line, the quality function and a reserve for latent errors; and must fund puts that trigger at year three, on new debt, on a change of horizon or on removal without cause. It may not borrow until the centre covers its cost. Part 14 says the centre is not covered "on any figure I have". None of these is sized, and no statement says what committed capital stands behind them. The pledge in particular "is in each contract" with the firm, and "borne by the group": the mechanism by which the group pays a firm for carrying surplus staff is not written.

4. **Part 13 against Parts 2 and 7: an error "does not touch anyone's pay", and it does.** Penalties a client suffers from a stop or agent error "are met by the firm without a claim". That is a firm cost. It lowers the profit on which the minority's dividends, the firm pool above its floor and the profit floor for deferred consideration are all calculated ("before group-borne costs" does not cover it, because the firm bears it). Liability for a method the centre mandated falls partly on the minority.

5. **Part 5 against Part 2: the give-back rule cuts the head's deferred pay.** Deferred consideration is paid on client fee retention over about three years. The give-back is offered at the first renewal after a case passes its full-cycle reading, at about month 18. The baseline excludes only first-year losses citing the sale or AI. From month 18 to 36 the head who applies the give-back rule as written lowers the measure they are paid on, and it is the head who "words and times" what clients are told.

6. **Part 14's stop rule against the thesis and Part 15.** "Handovers pause if ... client losses pass their thresholds." When a rival cuts price by a third, client losses are the first thing to rise. The thesis says that if fees fall fast "speed of cost-out would then matter more than depth". The rule slows the programme in exactly that case. The events table calls a firm that cannot follow the price "a stop-rule matter", but no stop rule is written for fee decline.

7. **Part 3 against principle 4 and Part 7: the head of quality decides what it audits.** The head of quality is "never on the release gate" and "nobody audits what they approved". But under "Disputes" the head of quality decides any disagreement between firms about the correctness of a shared procedure, and then audits its use. The same person also writes the report on which the stop rules are decided, can stop any agent, and at one firm is a part-time outsider. Nobody is named as appointing, paying or removing them, and if they are an outside firm their fee depends on the programme continuing, which sits badly with "has no stake".

8. **Part 3 against Part 13: the firm must claim against its own majority owner.** The indemnity for a faulty shared item runs from the holding company to the firm. The firm's board includes the group partner, and the centre appoints and pays the head. Nobody is named to decide that a claim is made, and a disputed services charge goes "to the group partner, then the holding board", who are the other side.

9. **Part 1 against the worked example.** The size screen requires a firm that can pay for its own engineer from the conventional plan's gain. Part 1 says a firm of 30 cannot. The whole of "Firm A in the first 18 months" is a firm of 30. The screen says "from year two"; Parts 2 and 11 say the firm pays from month 19.

10. **Part 4 against Parts 7 and 14 on the centre's size.** The thesis calls the centre "one operating partner and a part-time controller"; the table gives about two with a part-time head of quality; and Part 7 admits that with the per-deal team "these are the same five people as in the first draft". The cost at one firm is that of five people plus backfill, whatever the standing count is called. The five-firm column lists six roles and totals "about 5".

11. **Small mismatches.** The decision table gives client acceptance to the signatory ("the nominated officer where a rule names one"); the events table gives it to the nominated officer. Centre staff are paid on "cost of the latest firm against the first", which rewards starving a later conversion or under-recording its hours, and nobody outside the centre keeps that count.

The design's own six strains still stand. Of them, "full review and the return" and "the centre and its pay" are admissions, not resolutions.

## 3. The nine events

For each: what the document assigns, then where it does not say who does what. Numbered gaps run through the section.

**A new client is won.** Assigned: seller, quote, off-list approval, capacity check, relationship holder, acceptance, conflict check, engagement terms, rule file, limits, first cycle by hand, recording the win.
1. Who decides acceptance: the signatory (Part 3) or the nominated officer (events table).
2. Who sets the reserve fraction that limits how much freed capacity may be sold, and who tells the head it has been reached.
3. Who sets the per-client flag at the gateway, and who checks it is set before the client's material enters a workflow.
4. Who makes the separate request to use the client's files in test sets.

**An agent makes an error that reaches a client.** Assigned in detail by the error protocol: classing, disclosure, insurer, stop, rollback, re-review, penalties, restart, shared items, pay.
5. Who decides the error was "inside approved limits with the check run". Pay and blame turn on it, and the owner, who would be judged, is the only person placed to say.
6. Who decides how much of a later rise in premium or excess is "attributable to AI-assisted work", and so paid by the group.
7. Who decides the firm claims on the holding company's indemnity (pair 8 above).
8. Who warns other firms when the fault is in the model, not in a shared item. The trade lead's duty covers shared items only, and there is no trade lead before the firm-two test passes.
9. Who does the re-review when the reserve is too small. "From the reserve" assumes it is there; its fraction is open.

**A senior partner resigns and takes clients.** Assigned: leaver terms, covenants, re-assignment of signing, agents to the deputy, notices, records to clients, interim head, perimeter check, the pledge.
10. Who classes the leaver as good or bad.
11. Who pays for the leaver's minority and from what; the "reserve for puts" is unsized.
12. Whether the remaining sellers' deferred consideration falls when fee retention drops because a colleague left. The clause is firm-wide as written.
13. Who steps in if the leaver is the firm quality lead or the nominated officer; neither has a deputy.
14. Whether the resulting client losses trip the "at any time" stop rule and pause handovers, and who decides they should not.

**A regulator asks who signed.** Assigned: the head deals with the regulator; the record of work supplies the answer; the sampled-out case, firm B and a departed signatory are covered.
15. Who answers if the regulator addresses the holding company or an approved group person, not the firm.
16. Who answers for work done before completion, when the signatory was a seller who has since left.
17. Who speaks for a unit where the stop or the method was decided by the head of quality or adopted from the centre's list. "The firm's board adopts the method" is the paper answer; who attends is not said.

**The group buys its sixth firm.** Assigned: holding board, funding order, pace rule, resident and consent, diligence, adoption of the non-negotiables, the quality team's qualification, the controller, heads consulted, a second group partner.
18. Who runs the diligence on the work. Part 4 says the resident-to-be; at six firms that person is still seconded elsewhere unless "the deal waits".
19. Who decides that the pace rule is relaxed, if it ever is (see the note on thirty).
20. Who introduces the shared library to the new firm and in what order, given "one firm at a time" and "may refuse item by item".

**A better model is released and a rival cuts its price by a third.** Assigned: watching prices, re-test, acceptance per case, pricing to a floor, the retention baseline, a pause on purchases.
21. Who decides to bring the give-back forward, and who sets its share (pairs 5 and 6).
22. Who goes back to the insurer and the professional body. Their written agreement to sampling was given for a line on a given method; nobody is named to ask whether a new model reopens it.
23. Who pays for the re-test after month 18, when the engineer is the firm's own, shared or gone.
24. Who funds the pledge in a firm that "competes on service or shrinks" inside the three years.
25. Which stop rule applies. None is written for falling fees.

**An employee refuses to be recorded.** Assigned: the head runs the stated-purpose process with the staff representative; work is done uncaptured; a reviewer is qualified by observed files; the head may leave the case with people.
26. Who hears an objection to the one thing that cannot be refused, the logging of a person's corrections in an agent's trace. The only route written for staff is to the head of quality, and only for a breach of the pledge.
27. Whether a person who refuses still receives the case gain share and authorship pay, and who decides.
28. Who runs this in stage one, at a firm the group does not own. "A staff communication plan agreed with the firm's head" is all there is.

**The monthly close.** Assigned: practice manager prepares, head signs, controller consolidates, working day ten, late close to the group partner, client money at firm B, accounts to the minority, disputed charges.
29. Who calculates and certifies the figures that pay people: the measured case gain net of give-back, authorship and running payments, the growth payment, the pool, the deferred-consideration measures. This is the largest gap in the walk-through. The practice manager and the head would be calculating their own pay.
30. Who closes and reviews the centre's own books, given a part-time controller and an audit once a year; and who rules on a disputed charge when the group partner is, at one firm, the managing partner.

**A procedure needs changing and two firms disagree.** Assigned: each keeps its client rules; taste goes to the trade lead with variants at the firm's cost; correctness goes to the head of quality; a signatory may still refuse.
31. Who decides whether a dispute is taste or correctness. The two routes have different deciders. Also unassigned: a dispute about the method itself with two firms, where "a majority of heads" can tie; and a dispute before a trade lead exists, which the design dismisses ("no shared items to dispute") although the task test, the measures and the error protocol are shared from the first day of firm two.

The gaps that matter most: 29, 5, 21, 7, 8, 31, 13 and 22.

## 5. Objections, ranked by how much they matter

1. **Nobody is named to calculate and certify the numbers that pay people and trigger the stop rules, and the people on a case share in a gain measured by their own review time.** Name an outside calculator (the controller with the head of quality's sign-off would do). Take reviewers and the quality lead out of the case gain share, or pay it only on the blind-sample result. Exclude signatories on growth pay from the quality lead role, or change their pay. Keep the head of quality off phantom units.
2. **The return rests on the conventional plan and no one owns it.** Either pay the head on defined conventional-plan results (lock-up, recovered billing) for the first three years, or give the resident stated authority over those lines. Say which.
3. **The promises are not sized against the capital.** List the group-borne commitments in one table with a worst-case amount each, even as a range, and state the committed capital that must stand behind them before completion. Say how the group pays a firm for the pledge. Without this, Part 13 and Part 14 stay partly decided however many experiments run.
4. **The fallback ownership structure is not designed.** The design admits the answer on majority control "may overturn the equity design". Draw the alternative on half a page: who appoints the head, who holds the stop rules, what the sellers keep.
5. **The head of quality and the firm quality lead carry more than their definition supports.** Say who appoints, pays and removes the head of quality; take correctness disputes on shared items away from the person who audits them, or accept the conflict in words; give the firm quality lead a deputy and say who does the blind re-performance and who pays for it.
6. **The price-cut event is answered against the grain of the thesis.** Write a stop or review rule for falling fees, exempt market-wide client loss from the handover pause or say why not, name who sets and who may bring forward the give-back share, and take the give-back out of the head's fee-retention measure.
7. **An agent error does reach pay, through the firm's profit.** Either the group meets client penalties arising from the mandated method, or the sentence "does not touch anyone's pay" is narrowed to individuals' pay steps.
8. **Two to four firms is the least designed stretch.** No trade lead, no tie-break among two heads, no cross-firm alert for a model fault, and an unsettled question of whether the firm-two test happens before or after the second purchase.
9. **Stage one has no one to do the floor week.** The diligence is scored by a resident who is not hired until a firm is bought. Name who does it in experiment 1.
10. **Thirty firms is about two decades away on the design's own pace rule.** Say so, or say when the rule loosens and who decides.
11. **The worked example is a firm the size screen would reject, scored on a test the design has replaced.** Label it as a method illustration, or re-score a sample of lines.
12. **Smaller gaps:** the acceptance decider; who classes a leaver; who sets the per-client flag; who answers a regulator that addresses the group; the register for the centre's own agents; legal work at the centre; A003's three uncompared contexts.
