# Completeness review of draft v1

Reviewer: completeness. Date: 23 November 2026. Read: section 3 of the brief (the fifteen parts) and section 6 (what to hand back); `design/draft-v1.md` in full. I did not open the ledger, the inventories or the evidence files, so I cannot say whether the draft reports them correctly. Part and line references are to draft v1.

## Verdict

No part is missing and none is left as a heading. Ten of the fifteen parts are decided; five are only partly decided: 2 (ownership and capital), 7 (people), 9 (management system), 13 (risk) and 14 (economics). The draft's own coverage table admits four of these (2, 7, 13, 14) as having "open numbers"; it does not admit Part 9, which has no budgets, no targets and no routine for the holding board or the centre itself. Three things the brief names are absent outright: unit economics per service (Part 14), hiring (Part 7), and any definition of the "group units" on which a large share of the incentive design rests (Parts 2 and 7). Every part is shown at one, five and thirty firms, though Parts 5 and 12 do so in a sentence. All eleven required contents are present. The more serious weakness is fit, not coverage: the pay design does not reward the one thing the economics counts as a gain (selling freed capacity), the people who own the checks are paid on the results of those checks, the head of quality both approves and audits shared items, and the gate for buying the second firm (month 12) falls before the first honest reading of the weakest premise (month 15 to 18). Of the nine events, only one (a regulator asks who signed) is close to fully answered; the senior partner leaving and the sixth purchase are the thinnest.

## 2. Parts that contradict each other or do not fit

Ranked. The draft's own "Where the parts strain" section already names six strains (pledge against economics, local equity against sharing, signatory liability against shared playbooks, fast review against deference, mandate against autonomy, centre against its pay). I do not repeat those except where the draft's answer does not hold.

1. **Incentives (Part 7) against economics (Part 14): nobody who sells is paid to sell.** The only gain the design counts is an avoided hire or capacity sold. Selling is done by partners and client leads (Part 5). The head and signatories are paid on fee retention, staff retention, milestones and quality, and "never" on profit. Workflow owners are paid on "capacity sold", but they do not sell. The second stop rule ("stop new handovers until someone is selling the hours") will therefore fire with no one whose pay depends on preventing it.

2. **Incentives (Part 7) against the principles (Part 8, principle 4): the owner marks their own work.** The workflow owner "owns the playbook, the client rules, the limits, the check, the test set" and the reviewers report to them. The owner is paid on review time, sampled error rate and recurrence. So the person paid on the result owns the instrument, the sample and the people that produce the result. The draft says an agent may not read or edit its tests; it gives the human owner both. Who draws the blind sample and who seeds the errors is not said.

3. **Head's pay contradicts itself (Parts 2, 7 and the "agreeing to" table).** Part 7: the head is never paid on firm profit in the transition years. Part 2: "a head with a local minority, paid from the firm's profit". The agreeing-to table: the cost to the head includes "a lower profit share in year one". Part 14: partners "move to salary". A minority shareholder is paid from profit by definition. Which is it, and who sets the salary?

4. **Quality function (Parts 3, 7, 10): the checker of shared items is also their approver.** The head of quality sits on the release gate with the trade lead, then audits registers and can stop the same items. At one firm the role is part-time. The controller likewise prepares group accounts and is the internal audit. Principle 4 is applied to agents and reviewers and not to the centre.

5. **Timing (Parts 4, 14, 15): the second firm can be bought before the first has been read.** The next purchase may be signed after the month-twelve review. The draft says the first honest reading of capacity is at month 15 to 18 and that the weakest premise is tested only after a full cycle. The stop rule itself is dated "month 12 to 18". So firm two can be bought on a result that does not yet exist.

6. **Pace (Part 4) against scale (Parts 4 and 7).** "The next is not signed until the last has had its month-twelve review" allows one firm in its first year at a time. The five-firm picture has "two residents, so at most two firms in their first year at once". One of the two is wrong.

7. **Residents from earlier firms (Parts 4, 7) against local incentives (Part 7).** At thirty, and at the sixth firm, residents are former workflow owners. Their old firm loses its best owner; its head is paid on staff retention and holds only local equity. Nothing says who decides the move, who backfills, or what the lending firm receives.

8. **Decision rights (Part 3) against the stop rules (Part 14).** "Which work is handed to an agent" is the firm's decision, and the group partner "may order only on a non-negotiable". The stop rules ("stop building", "stop new handovers") are not on the non-negotiable list. As written the centre cannot enforce its own stop rules, other than by replacing the head.

9. **Decision rights against liability (Parts 3, 13).** Three points. (a) The centre sets the floor and ceiling of every agent's limits, mandates the task test and appoints, pays and removes the head, who is usually a signatory; the draft quotes the FCA criticising "decisions materially affecting regulated firms … made by unregulated boards" and does not test its own table against that sentence. (b) The services company carries the risk of a faulty shared check "by contract", while the draft notes that PI wordings commonly exclude cases where recovery rights were limited by contract; the two have not been reconciled. (c) Part 3 says the holding board sets "risk appetite" and hears the head of quality, and in the next sentence that "risk … never goes to the holding board".

10. **Technology (Parts 2, 11) against client separation (principle 7).** The services company "owns the group's contracts with tool and model suppliers". The firm is the data controller and client material "serves that client only". If the centre holds the contract through which client material flows, the centre is in the processing chain of every firm's clients. Who is the contracting party, and what the client is told about it, is not decided.

11. **Recording (principle 6) against the measures (Part 9) and reviewer pay (Part 7).** "Record the work, not the worker." Measure 3 is review time "by reviewer"; reviewers are paid on their individual seeded-error catch rate; each trainee's catch rate is measured. That is recording the worker. It may be justified, but the draft says an employee who refuses recording "has little to refuse", which is not true of a reviewer.

12. **The first trade (Part 1) against the weakest premise (Thesis, Part 13).** The draft leans to accountancy first. It also says that at firm A every tax deliverable is reviewed by a person until the insurer and institute say otherwise, so the gain "must come from faster, better-supported review", and that fast review is how a nodding reviewer looks. The trade chosen for the first test is the one where the premise is most tightly held by rule.

13. **The reuse award (Part 7) against what may be shared (Parts 2, 10).** The award is the main reward for people whose know-how is captured. It pays only when a playbook "runs at another firm". Client rules are never shared; firms may refuse anything off the non-negotiable list; nothing is shared unless the firm-two test passes. In firm one, for the 18 to 24 months that decide the design, the central incentive cannot pay, and if the test fails it never does.

14. **Staff retention pay against the counted gain.** The head's deferred payment depends on staff retention. The gain is counted from "unreplaced leavers". Unless retention is defined as regretted leavers only, the head is paid against the saving.

15. **The centre and the economics.** Acknowledged by the draft. What it does not add: from year two the firm pays "at cost" only for a core of finance reporting, audit and quality and "may refuse" the rest, so the two or three engineers at five firms have no assured funding. And the claim that a centre of nine to eleven is "tight" at five firms is made without a money figure for the centre.

16. **Capital authority at thirty.** Group partners have "their own capital authority" (Part 3, thirty firms); the decision table gives buying a firm to the holding board alone.

## 3. The nine events

For each: what the draft says, then what it does not.

**A new client is won.** Says: a partner or manager sells; the reporting officer runs acceptance; an agent prepares checks; terms carry the clauses; the practice manager adds the rule file; the owner sets limits.
Not said: who quotes the price (the head "decides prices"; is every quote the head's?). Who decides there is capacity and which case the client enters. Who runs the conflict check across group firms, given the centre has a veto and no client data. Who writes the client's rules: the event table says the practice manager, principle 8 and Part 7 say the people who do the work. Whether a new client's first cycle is done by hand or by the agent. Who names the relationship holder. What happens if the client declines the AI clause at the outset, and who prices the non-AI route. Who records the win against a capacity-to-P&L line. Who the "reporting officer" is at firm B.

**An agent makes an error that reaches a client.** Says: signatory tells the client and corrects; head and head of quality within a day; insurer as the policy requires; owner stops, rolls back, re-reviews, classes; firm board.
Not said: who decides that something is "an error that reached a client" and not a query. Which named person notifies the insurer, and the regulator or professional body where required. Who does the work while the workflow is stopped, and who re-reviews the back-work when reviewers are already the scarce resource. If the fault is in a shared check: who tells the other firms, who stops it group-wide, and whose cover pays (firm PI or the services company's). Who agrees any refund or compensation. Who decides the workflow may restart. What it does to the sponsor's, reviewer's and head's pay.

**A senior partner resigns and takes clients.** Says: head and group partner manage retention; deferred consideration and equity terms apply; rule files stay; the loss goes into base rates.
Not said (this is the thinnest of the nine): what happens if the leaver is the head. What the equity and deferred terms actually are: forfeiture, buy-back, price, who buys. Whether there are restrictive covenants and who decides to enforce them. Who becomes signatory on the leaver's in-flight work and sponsor of the agents on it, and what the agents do meanwhile. Who tells the clients, the insurer and the professional body. What happens if the leaver is the responsible individual who controls the audit entity outside the group. Whether the firm still meets any rule on qualified principals with one fewer. That clients are entitled to their records regardless of where "rule files" stay. Whether the job pledge survives a material loss of fees.

**A regulator asks who signed a piece of work.** Says: the firm answers with the named signatory, review record, playbook version, check results and trace; the register shows the sponsor; the centre supplies release history. This is the best-answered event.
Not said: who at the firm deals with the regulator. How long traces, versions and check results are kept, and where, when the tools are rented. Whether the model version is recorded. What the answer is when the unit was sampled out and no person read it. What the answer is at firm B, where nothing is "signed" and the accountable person is whoever approved an outward act. What happens if the signatory has left. Whether the centre and the insurer are told.

**The group buys its sixth firm.** Says: deal partner and a resident from an earlier firm; the same four tests; the group partner's span decides whether a second group partner is needed.
Not said: who decides (holding board, by the table, but the event does not say) and from what money: retained dividends or new capital. Whether the pace rule still applies. Who releases the resident from their old firm and who backfills (contradiction 7). Who does the diligence week when both residents are in firms. Whether the sixth firm receives the shared library or may refuse it. What changes if it is in a second trade: there is no trade lead, and one head of quality holds one profession's qualification. Whether one controller can close six. Who sits on the new board. Whether existing heads have any say. When the second group partner is appointed and by whom.

**A better model is released and a rival cuts its price by a third.** Says: the engineer re-tests within the quarter; the head decides price client by client; no debt or platform fee, so the firm can follow; the holding board re-runs scenarios for deals in progress.
Not said: who is responsible for noticing the rival. Whether a quarter is fast enough. Any rule for whether to match, a floor, or a point at which to let a client go. Who coordinates when several group firms face the same rival (the centre sets no prices). What a one-third cut does to the job pledge, to the head's fee-retention payment and to the stop rules. Firm B's margin is put at 8 to 15%; a one-third cut cannot be followed there, and the draft does not say what is done instead. Who decides to move to the new model across firms when "done once" re-testing passes: each owner separately, per the table. Whether purchases pause.

**An employee refuses to be recorded.** Says: little to refuse; where a team wants capture the stated-purpose process applies and that person's work is done uncaptured.
Not said: who runs the stated-purpose process and who agrees for the staff. What happens when the person is a reviewer and objects to being timed, to seeded errors or to a personal catch rate, all of which are mandated measures (contradiction 11). Whether refusal bars someone from the reviewer or author roles and their pay steps. Who owns the data-protection assessment. Whether agent traces of an exception handler's corrections count as recording that person. Who decides when the refusal makes a case unworkable.

**The monthly close.** Says: firm finance prepares on the group chart with a rented product; a person signs; the controller consolidates; the board sees one page.
Not said: who "firm finance" is in a firm of 30, and who the signer is. The deadline. Who compiles the eight measures and who tests that they are honest, when the controller both consolidates and audits and is part-time at one firm. Who sponsors the agent that prepares the close in the firm (the consolidation workflow at the centre has a sponsor; the firm's does not). Who reconciles and signs client money in the same cycle at firm B. How charges from the services company are set and disputed. What happens when the close is late. What minority shareholders receive.

**A procedure needs changing and two firms disagree.** Says: each keeps its own client rules; the trade lead sets the shared default through the gate; a firm may keep a variant at its own cost; variants are reviewed quarterly.
Not said: who decides when the disagreement is about correctness or compliance, not taste. The head of quality can stop a variant and any signatory can refuse the default, so both sides hold a veto and nobody holds a casting vote; the group partner may order only on a non-negotiable. Who reviews variants across firms: the quarterly review named is inside each firm. What happens before a trade lead exists, or for a procedure that crosses trades (back office). The authoring firm earns a reuse award on adoption, which gives it an interest in the outcome; nothing manages that. The firm bears the cost of its variant on its P&L, but its head is not paid on profit. Who decides a change to the method itself when heads disagree, beyond "after consulting heads".

## 5. Objections, ranked by how much they matter

1. Nobody who sells is paid to sell, yet sold capacity is the only gain counted. Add a growth element to the head's and client leads' pay, or say why not.
2. Workflow owners own the checks, the test sets and the reviewers and are paid on what those report. Give the sample, the seeded errors and the test set to someone outside the owner's line.
3. The second firm may be bought at month 12; the first reading of the weakest premise is at month 15 to 18. Move the gate or say the second purchase is made blind.
4. Group units are undefined, and with no exit they have no stated route to value. The workflow-owner, author and centre incentives all rest on them.
5. The centre cannot enforce its own stop rules under its own decision table.
6. The head's pay is described three incompatible ways.
7. The head of quality approves shared items and audits them; the controller prepares and audits. Separate each, or name who checks them.
8. A senior partner leaving is unanswered in every respect that matters: leaver terms, covenants, successor signatory, the audit entity, the case where the leaver is the head.
9. Part 9 has no budgets, targets, holding-board routine or routine for the centre. Mark it partly decided in the coverage table.
10. Part 14 has no unit economics per service and no centre cost in money; the claims "tight at five" and "works at thirty" are unsupported by arithmetic on the page.
11. The pace rule and the five-firm staffing contradict each other; residents drawn from earlier firms cut against those firms' heads.
12. Who holds the supplier contracts puts the centre in every client's data chain, against principle 7.
13. "Record the work, not the worker" does not describe what is done to reviewers.
14. A one-third price cut has no decision rule, and firm B cannot follow one.
15. Two firms disagreeing has two vetoes and no casting vote.
16. The reuse award cannot pay during the period that decides the design.
17. The draft's own FCA quotation about unregulated boards is not applied to its own decision table.
18. Smaller gaps: hiring; the audit entity's staffing; trace retention; the two undefined cases in the task-test status rule; who writes a new client's rules.
