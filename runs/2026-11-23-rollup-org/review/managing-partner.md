# Review from the managing partner's seat

Reviewer seat: managing partner of an acquired firm. First the 30-person accountancy practice (firm A), then the 50-person block manager (firm B). Document reviewed: `design/draft-v1.md`, read in full, 23 November 2026. Nothing else was read.

## Verdict

I would not sign this as drafted. The design is careful about what the group must not do to my firm, and almost silent on what I am paid, what I can be made to bear, and what happens when the centre and I disagree. It asks me to swap a partner's profit share for a salary, an illiquid minority with no exit and no dividend right, and deferred money tied to things the group's own programme puts at risk. It leaves the professional liability with me while it changes how the work is made and measures how fast I review it. It asks a firm chosen because it is short of capacity to find a year of extra effort with no backfill. And it tells my staff on day one that the plan needs fewer hours, with a guarantee that lasts one cycle. The fit is worse at the block manager, where clients can leave on short notice, the staff I most need are the ones whose portfolios the plan enlarges, and a 2027 law change will hit the very measure my deferred payment rests on. Most of this is fixable in the deal terms and in Parts 2, 3, 7 and 13, but the fixes cost the group money and authority the draft has not budgeted.

## Objections, ranked by how much they would change the design

### 1. The partners' economic deal is undefined, and every open item falls on me (Parts 2, 3, 14; "What each party would be agreeing to")

What is wrong:
- Partners "move to salary" (Part 14, firm A table). The salary is not stated. Firm A's profit of 28% to 36% is before partners' pay, so the salary is the price of my year, and it is missing.
- The centre alone decides dividends (Part 3 table). My minority therefore has no income right. With "no planned exit" (Part 1) it has no sale route either. A minority that pays nothing and cannot be sold is worth close to nothing to me.
- Price "works with no AI gain", on a marker of 0.8 to 1.3 times recurring fees. I am paid for an ordinary firm, then carry year-one profit falling "by design" through my minority and through "a lower profit share in year one".
- From year two the firm pays "at cost" for the engineer (called "the largest new cost" in Part 7) and for the non-negotiable core "by headcount". Neither is capped. The audit and quality functions exist for the group's benefit and are charged to my P&L.
- "Everyone in the firm" gets a flat share of a profit pool, and workflow owners get "the right to buy into the firm over time" (Part 7). Nobody says whose share funds the pool or whose shares are sold.
- Decision 1 (long-hold company or fund with a clock) is not made. The draft itself says that with a clock the pledge and the loop "are the first things to give way". I would be signing without knowing which owner I have.

What would have to change: a term sheet section in Part 2 covering partner salary at a stated relation to prior drawings; a dividend policy with a minimum payout that the minority can enforce; a put option at a set formula after year three and on any change of control or new debt at the holding company; a cap on central charges as a share of fees, with the quality function and group audit borne by the group; a floor under partners' total income for the transition years; and the no-debt and no-guarantee rules written into the firm's articles with a minority veto, not left as group policy.

### 2. Liability stays with me while the centre changes the work and times my review (Parts 3, 6, 9, 13)

What is wrong:
- Part 13 puts professional error on "the firm entity and its named signatory", with an excess of up to about £75,000, and admits no UK insurer's terms or price are known. Any rise in premium or excess caused by the programme lands on my firm's P&L.
- A faulty shared playbook is carried by the services company "by contract", and the same row admits PI wordings commonly exclude claims where recovery rights were limited by contract. That is an open hole in my cover, noted and left.
- Measure 3 is "review time per unit, by reviewer", there is "a timer on review", and the stop rule turns on review time falling. I answer to my institute personally. A system that needs my review to get faster to justify itself is a standing pressure on the one act I am disciplined for. The draft names this strain ("Fast review and deference") and offers only untested counterweights.
- My right to stop an agent is real, but the cost of a stop in a deadline week falls on my firm, and nothing says a stop cannot count against my milestones.
- "Three cases attempted" is a deferred-payment milestone. I am paid for handing work over, and I sign for the result.

What would have to change: a holding-company indemnity to the firm and to each signatory for loss arising from any shared item or centre-supplied tool, uncapped by the services contract; the group pays any increase in premium and excess attributable to AI-assisted work for at least three years, plus run-off cover; review time reported per case, never per named signatory, and never used in anyone's pay or in the head's assessment; a written rule that exercising a stop cannot reduce deferred consideration; milestones reworded so that "attempted and stopped for cause" counts as met. The insurer's written answer must be a condition of exchange, not a diligence note.

### 3. A capacity-short firm has no spare year, and the calendar ignores the trade's deadlines (Parts 4, 7, 9; worked example)

What is wrong:
- The target is defined as short of capacity. The cost to me is "a year of extra effort from managers and seniors; authors' time". The group pays for its resident and engineer, and for none of my people's time.
- "Time credited as chargeable" for authors is an accounting entry. The client work still has to be done by someone. Four managers become workflow owners and the bookkeepers write rules; nobody is named to do the returns meanwhile.
- "Days 100 to 365" takes no account of January self-assessment, quarter ends, or the new quarterly reporting work that arrived in April 2026, which the design also wants me to sell as the growth line in the same year.
- The standing load is heavy for 30 people: weekly P&L and cash note, weekly exception sampling, monthly close on a new chart, a half-day board each month, monthly register review, quarterly workflow reviews, re-scoring at each model release. The practice manager is given the inventory, the register and the measures on top of their job.
- First cash is month 15 to 18 at best. That is two peak seasons of extra effort for nothing.

What would have to change: a backfill budget paid by the group as deal cost, sized in practitioner-weeks before signing (locums or early hires, which the pledge then has to square with); blackout periods set by the head in which no build work or handover happens; the head, not the centre, sets the order and timing of cases, with one case in year one as an acceptable outcome; a second pair of hands for the practice manager; and the reporting load cut to monthly until the first cycle is done.

### 4. The staff deal will lose me the seniors and managers first (Parts 7, 8, 9)

What is wrong:
- The pledge lasts until "a full demand cycle has been measured", and staff are told on day one that "the plan needs fewer hours per unit of work". In a market where 48% of firms are capacity-bound, my best people can move in a month. A one-cycle guarantee with that sentence attached is a notice period.
- Seniors become "reviewers", a "defined, tested job" with seeded known-error files, a timer, a catch rate, and review time reported "by reviewer". Principle 6 says "record the work, not the worker". This records the worker. Good seniors will not accept being covertly tested, and the best of them have options.
- Managers become workflow owners paid on review time, sampled error rate and "capacity sold". They did not train to sell or to own software. Partners "spend freed time selling"; many are not sellers.
- Bookkeepers and payroll staff are asked to write the rules that replace their making, in return for "first call" on other roles. First call on a smaller number of roles is not a job.
- Trainees do "the task by hand on closed past files for a cycle" and intake is held "in year one" only. Whether work on closed files counts as practical experience under a training contract is not addressed. With the audit book gone, audit experience goes too. Trainee recruitment is how the firm renews itself; this makes my firm a worse place to qualify.
- Measure 7 watches regretted leavers but there is no stop rule tied to it, only to review time and errors.

What would have to change: the pledge extended to a fixed term (three years, matching my own tie-in) for named roles, with redeployment at protected pay; seeded-error testing run openly, agreed with staff under the stated-purpose process, reported at team level and not used for individual pay until staff agree; workflow ownership voluntary, with a non-selling track; a staff-loss stop rule (pause all handovers if regretted leavers pass an agreed figure against the firm's own history); a written check with the institute on training-contract experience before any trainee work is redesigned; and retention payments for the six to ten people the firm cannot lose, funded by the group.

### 5. The centre can remove me, stop my work and change the method, with no appeal and no leaver terms (Part 3)

What is wrong:
- "Appointing, paying, replacing the firm's head: Centre decides; firm consulted." My deferred consideration depends on staying three years. Nothing defines cause, or what happens to deferred money and my minority if I am replaced. The draft says a "competent, unwilling head can stall a firm for a year or two before the centre acts", which tells me how disagreement ends.
- The method changes yearly "after consulting heads"; firms "may add, not subtract". Consulting is not consent. The list of non-negotiables can grow.
- The head of quality is part-time, reports to the holding board, can stop any agent and audit without notice. There is no route to challenge a stop, no time limit on it, and no one bears its cost but me.
- The firm board is me, two of my staff and the group partner. My staff's group units and career path ("reviewer to workflow owner to resident to group partner") point their interest at the group.
- At thirty firms residents are "drawn from earlier firms". The group will recruit my best managers.

What would have to change: removal of the head only for defined cause, with deferred consideration accelerating and the minority bought at fair value on removal without cause; the non-negotiable list fixed in the shareholders' agreement and changeable only with heads' majority consent; a stop by the head of quality reviewed within a set number of days by someone other than the person who ordered it; a no-poach rule, or a paid transfer with my consent, for staff moving to the centre.

### 6. Carving out audit is treated as tidy; from inside it splits the partnership and the client base (Part 2; worked example)

What is wrong:
- One of three partners leaves with the audit book, or straddles two entities. The draft does not say who employs the audit staff, how the 2,200 hours are resourced, or whether that partner still holds a minority and a board seat.
- Audit clients usually buy accounts and tax from the same firm. Sending the audit to a separate entity, or "referring the book on", puts those clients in front of a rival.
- "The group takes no fee from it", so the firm loses the income and keeps the overhead it shared.
- The "Chartered Accountants" title risk is listed as "watch". For me it is the name on the door.

What would have to change: a worked structure for the audit entity (staffing, shared premises and systems at arm's length, independence position cleared with the institute in writing), the price adjusted for audit-linked fees at risk, and the title question answered before exchange rather than watched until 2027. If the audit partner will not stay in the group firm, the deal should be re-screened.

### 7. The client plan invites fee cuts and leaves me to run two processes (Part 5, Part 10)

What is wrong:
- Clients are told of the ownership change on day one and of AI use at renewal, with a right to object, and the plan "assumes some of the gain goes back at renewal". That is a letter inviting a discount, sent before any gain exists. What small UK clients do when told is "unknown".
- A client may ask for work "without AI, and we can still do it". Running a manual line beside the new one costs money. It falls on the firm.
- Deferred consideration is paid on client fee retention. Losses caused by the group's disclosure rule and its presence reduce my payment.
- Test sets are built "from the firm's own past files", while the draft admits whether client work may lawfully improve even the firm's own playbooks is "argued by analogy". I owe those clients confidentiality personally.

What would have to change: fee retention measured against a baseline that excludes clients lost within a set period citing ownership or AI; the group bears the cost of the manual line for opt-out clients for the transition; legal advice on use of past files obtained before signing and addressed to the firm; and disclosure wording and timing fully in the head's hands, with the centre's "floor" written out in advance.

### 8. At the block manager the design is weaker on every point above, and the draft says so without changing the deal (Part 14; worked example, firm B)

What is wrong:
- Clients are resident-run companies and freeholders on management agreements with short notice periods, often with change-of-control or consent clauses. Diligence does not list these. The RICS standard requires AI notice "in writing and in advance" with an opt-out, to boards of volunteers who can simply change agent.
- The growth plan is "units per property manager". Portfolio size is why property managers leave, and when they leave, blocks follow them.
- Recovery of insurance commission ends on 1 November 2027. That cuts revenue inside my deferred period through no act of mine.
- Lettings is "demand-limited", and there "the job pledge and the economics pull apart". The pledge to lettings staff is therefore not one I could give honestly.
- Margins of "perhaps 8% to 15%" cannot carry central charges, an engineer and a profit pool.
- The centre "audits" client money held on trust. On what authority, and with what access to client data, is not said.

What would have to change: change-of-control and termination terms added to diligence, with board consents sought before completion on the largest blocks; deferred consideration adjusted for the 2027 change; no units-per-manager target without the managers' agreement and a ceiling on portfolio size; lettings either excluded from the purchase or given an honest, different staff promise; central charges waived until margin passes a stated level.

### 9. "The firm keeps hiring and pay" is contradicted by the pay design (Parts 3 and 7)

What is wrong: the summary table says I keep "hiring and pay below the head". Part 7 then sets pay steps for owners and reviewers, a flat firm profit pool, group units for my managers and authors, credited authorship time, and what nobody may be paid on. That is the centre designing my pay structure. Group units held by my managers, with awards for reuse at other firms, give them a reason to spend time on the group's library instead of my clients; the draft admits the head "has little reason to lend an owner's time to another firm" and answers it only for authors.

What would have to change: either Part 7's pay design is offered as a menu the firm board adopts or declines, or the table is corrected to say pay structure is mandated. If staff time goes to shared items, the firm is paid for it at a commercial rate. The head needs a group element too, or the reuse scheme should wait.

### 10. Diligence exposes my firm before there is a deal (Part 4)

What is wrong: before exchange the group wants a week on my floor, re-performance of past client files, timing of my reviewers, written questions to my system vendors, and "written questions to the firm's insurer and professional body on AI-assisted work". Asking my insurer and my institute in my firm's name marks my file whether or not the deal completes. Staff will know within a day. Client files are opened to a buyer without client consent. There is no exclusivity, cost cover or confidentiality term in the draft.

What would have to change: insurer and regulator questions put through a broker or adviser without naming the firm until heads of terms are signed; file re-performance done by my own staff or an independent reviewer under my supervision; a confidentiality agreement, a staff communication plan agreed before anyone arrives, and a break fee covering my costs if the group walks away.

## What I would need in writing before signing

1. Partner salary, dividend policy, put option and change-of-control rights (objection 1).
2. The identity and horizon of the capital, with a covenant that it will not change without triggering the put.
3. No debt and no guarantees in the firm's articles, with a minority veto.
4. A cap on central charges; quality function and group audit at the group's cost.
5. Holding-company indemnity for shared items; premium, excess and run-off cover funded by the group; the insurer's written terms as a condition of exchange.
6. Leaver terms for the head and signatories, with cause defined.
7. The non-negotiable list fixed, and a review route for any stop ordered by the centre.
8. A group-funded backfill budget, blackout periods, and the head's control of pace.
9. A fixed-term job pledge and a staff-loss stop rule.
10. Fee-retention baseline adjusted for disclosure-driven losses and, at firm B, for the 2027 commission change.
