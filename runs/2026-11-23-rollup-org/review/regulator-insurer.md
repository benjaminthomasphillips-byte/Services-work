# Review of draft v1 from the regulator's and the PI underwriter's seat

23 November 2026. Reviewer read only `design/draft-v1.md`. Rule references below are from the reviewer's working knowledge of the UK regimes and were not checked against primary text in this review; each should be confirmed with the body named before the design relies on it.

## Verdict

The design puts liability in the right legal place (the firm and a named person) but does not yet give that person the means, the time or the independence to carry it, and it leaves the group's own position unlicensed and largely uninsured. Three things would stop a regulator or underwriter today. First, the supervision design is a list of controls with no owner who is independent of the gain: the workflow owner owns the agent, the check, the test set and the limits and is paid on review time and capacity sold; the signatory's deferred pay depends on handovers; the head of quality is part-time and approves the shared items he then audits. Second, the group is treated as an owner outside the regulatory perimeter when it appoints and pays the head, sits on the firm's board, mandates the method and can stop work; several regimes (ICAEW principal and affiliate rules, AML approval of owners and managers, FCA change in control where a firm arranges insurance, RICS and client-money scheme conditions) attach to exactly those facts and none is in the plan. Third, insurance is handled as a written question before exchange, when the real issues are change of control, non-binding answers on a claims-made annual policy, notification of the errors the design sets out to find, aggregation of one shared fault across many firms, and an intra-group recovery chain the document itself admits PI wordings exclude. The design is insurable only in its fallback form (every outward deliverable reviewed by a person, no shared items); the economics are written for the other form.

## Ranked objections

### 1. The supervision and signature design is not real yet: nobody independent of the gain controls the check, and the signatory does not see what they sign for

Sections: Part 3 table; Part 6 "The check, designed" and status rule; Part 7 firm chart and pay table; Part 9 measures 3 and 4; Part 13 "A regulator asks who signed".

What is wrong:
- The workflow owner "owns the playbook, the client rules, the limits, the check, the test set", sponsors the agent, approves local changes, and is paid on "review time ... capacity sold". Principle 4 says the checker cannot be reached by the maker; here the maker's sponsor owns the checker. That is not an independent check in any sense a regulator would recognise.
- Signatories are partners whose freed time goes to selling. Review is done by seniors, sampled. The design never says what the signatory personally sees before signing, how many signed outputs one signatory may carry in a period, or what minimum evidence sits in front of them. "The named signatory, with the review record" fails for every unit outside the sample: the honest answer to "who reviewed this file" is nobody.
- Signatories' deferred consideration is paid on milestones including "three cases attempted". The person with the stop right is paid to proceed, and the holding board appoints, pays and can replace the head. A stop right with no protection for using it is decorative.
- Review time per unit, by named reviewer, is a headline measure and a stop rule ("if review time has not fallen ... stop building"). The organisation is committed in writing to less supervision per unit before any body has agreed to it. An underwriter will read this as a target on the control.
- The head of quality is part-time at one firm, sits on the release gate that approves shared items and then audits them (self-review), and at one firm reports to a holding board that is "the same few people".
- Sampling is assumed in the status rule ("hand over now, with sampled review") and in 60% of model hours, but the document concedes no ICAEW statement on sampling for non-tax work was found and that tax guidance reads as review of each deliverable.

What would have to change:
- Make 100% human review of every outward or signed deliverable the base case for firm A, not the interim state, and write the economics on that basis. Sampling becomes an upside that needs written agreement from the insurer and the body, per line of work.
- Move ownership of checks, test sets and seeded-error files out of the workflow owner's hands to a quality role in the firm that is not paid on throughput.
- Write a signatory standard: what must be on screen before signature, a ceiling on signed units per signatory per period, and a rule that the signatory signs a statement of what was and was not reviewed.
- Remove handover milestones from signatories' deferred consideration; add contractual no-detriment protection and a log, seen by the holding board, of every stop and every stop overruled or reversed.
- Take review time out of pay and out of the stop rules as a target; keep it as a monitored figure paired with a floor.
- Head of quality: full-time or an outside firm from day one, never on the release gate he audits, with a right to report direct to the regulator and insurer.

### 2. The group sits inside the regulatory perimeter and the design treats it as outside

Sections: Part 2 structure and "Basis" (open items); Part 3 "The rule", non-negotiables and boards; Part 13 table (title risk as "watch").

What is wrong:
- "Mandate the method, not the content" does not keep the centre out of regulated decisions. The centre appoints and pays the head, has a director on each firm board, sets the task test and review format, operates a release gate over the checks, and its employee can stop work. The FCA quotation the draft itself uses ("decisions materially affecting regulated firms were made by unregulated boards") describes this structure.
- ICAEW: a holding company with a majority and a group partner on the board changes who the firm's principals are. Non-member principals need affiliate status; control tests govern use of "Chartered Accountants", member-firm status, eligibility for the DPB (investment business) licence and probate accreditation, and the PII regulations. The draft treats this as a marketing risk to watch in 2027. It is a condition of the firm's present status on day one.
- AML: owners, officers and managers of a supervised firm must be approved by the supervisor before they act. The holding company's owners, the group partner and arguably the head of quality fall within that. Not mentioned. Firm B needs HMRC supervision for estate and letting agency business; not mentioned.
- FCA: a block manager that arranges buildings insurance and takes commission is carrying on insurance distribution, as an authorised firm or an appointed representative. Buying a majority of an authorised firm without prior FCA approval is an offence; an appointed representative needs its principal's consent. The draft notes the commission ending in 2027 and never asks whether firm B is authorised. An accountancy firm may also hold consumer-credit or DPB permissions.
- Firm B: client money protection scheme membership (mandatory for letting agents), a redress scheme, and "Regulated by RICS" status each carry ownership, principal and notification conditions. "Any ownership rule for property managers (not searched)" is not an acceptable state for a design that names block management as the second trade.
- HMRC: the tax-adviser registration regime and the agent standard attach to the firm and its officers; a change of control and new officers need to be reported and may need to meet conditions.

What would have to change:
- Add a perimeter map per trade as a founding document: every status the firm holds, the control and principal test for each, who must be approved, and the notification or prior-consent step. Make "all consents in hand" a condition precedent to completion.
- Decide deliberately whether the holding company can hold a majority of votes at all in firm A, or whether control must stay with members and the group take economics by another route. This may overturn the Part 2 equity design.
- Put the group partner, head of quality and holding board members through fit-and-proper and AML approval; accept that they are then personally within reach of the regulators.
- Write down which centre decisions are binding on the firm and have the firm's board formally adopt the method, so the regulated entity has made the decision.

### 3. Insurance is treated as a question to ask; the acquisition itself is what breaks cover

Sections: Part 4 diligence test 4; Part 13 table and "Where I chose"; Part 15 "Founding"; open question 2.

What is wrong:
- A written question to the firm's current insurer is not a term of cover. PI is claims-made and annual; an underwriter's letter in month zero does not bind the renewal in month nine, when the cases go live.
- Change of control is not addressed. Many PI wordings restrict or end cover for work done after a change of ownership unless the insurer agrees. The plan has ownership disclosed to clients on day one and no step where the insurer endorses the policy.
- A material change in how work is produced mid-term (agents preparing, sampled review) is a change of risk that needs disclosing under the duty of fair presentation at renewal, with the knowledge of group officers attributed to the firm.
- Prior acts and run-off are absent. If the practice moves entity, successor-practice rules and run-off (ICAEW expects six years, two as a minimum) apply; if shares are bought, the group buys every past error. No warranty, indemnity, retention or escrow against pre-completion negligence is in the seller terms.
- AI exclusions and sub-limits are now appearing in PI and cyber wordings. The draft records that none was retrieved. A "silent" position is not safe to plan on.
- The excess (up to about £75,000 on the model) falls on a firm whose profit pool is shared flat among all staff; nobody is named as bearing uninsured loss, the excess, or a premium loading.

What would have to change:
- Appoint a specialist broker before the first approach. Obtain terms, not answers: a change-of-control endorsement, an affirmative AI clause, retroactive cover confirmed, and a renewal commitment or a multi-year policy covering the build period.
- Make indicative terms from at least two markets a founding gate (Part 15 "Founding"), and bound terms a condition precedent to completion.
- Add to seller terms: warranties on claims and circumstances, a retention against prior-act claims for at least the limitation period of the main work, and who pays run-off.
- State in Part 14 who bears excesses and premium loadings, and model premium as an open cost line that scales with the share of work under sampled review.

### 4. A shared check or playbook creates one correlated loss across the group, and the recovery chain behind it does not work

Sections: Part 2 "Who owns the know-how" and charging; Part 3 release gate; Part 10 "One, five, thirty"; Part 13 row "A shared check or playbook with a fault".

What is wrong:
- The client sues the firm; the firm's insurer pays and looks to subrogate against the services company, a sister company with "its own cover" of unstated type and limit. If the intra-group contract caps the services company's liability, the firm's PI may exclude the loss (the draft says so itself). If it does not cap it, the services company carries unlimited exposure to every firm on a thin balance sheet. Either way the loss returns to the group.
- One faulty shared check running at many firms on one day is a series of related claims. Aggregation wording will decide whether that is one limit or many, and on separate policies per firm it may be one excess per firm and one event per insurer. "One firm's claim cannot exhaust another's cover" is the wrong test; the real risk is the same fault exhausting all of them at once.
- Local acceptance by the firm's owner moves responsibility back to the firm without removing the centre's. A regulator will hold the signatory liable for a playbook they "accepted" on a test set; an insurer will ask whether that acceptance was competent.
- The practice engineer is a group employee writing checks and acceptance criteria inside a regulated firm, then "the firm's own, or shared". Whose employee, under whose supervision and whose policy, is not stated. Same for the resident.
- Model re-testing is "done once ... for every firm" by the centre, which is a shared item that bypasses the local-acceptance rule.

What would have to change:
- Decide and insure the allocation: either the services company carries technology errors-and-omissions cover with a limit sized to group-wide simultaneous failure and uncapped liability to the firms, or shared items are barred and the firm-two test is about method only.
- Place the firms and the services company with one insurer or one coordinated programme with agreed aggregation and waiver of subrogation, and accept that this contradicts "separate policies per firm".
- Cap the blast radius by rule: no shared change live at more than one firm inside a set period; 100% review at each firm for a set number of units after any shared change.
- Second the engineer and resident formally into the firm, under the firm's supervision and policy, with that stated in the engagement terms.

### 5. The design builds an error-finding machine with no duty-to-correct or notification protocol

Sections: Part 4 diligence test 3; Part 6 seeded errors and sampling; Part 9 measure 2 and escalation; experiments 1 and 2; "An agent's error reaches a client".

What is wrong:
- Re-performing past files before purchase will find real errors in filed returns and accounts. Once known, the firm has professional duties (tell the client; for tax, advise disclosure to HMRC and cease to act if the client refuses) and a policy duty to notify circumstances. Found during diligence under the seller's policy and not notified, they are uninsured after renewal. Found by a buyer who then completes, they are known prior circumstances excluded from any new cover.
- Blind random sampling of live work and shadow runs against "the firm's own output" will keep finding errors, in the firm's own human work as well as the agent's. Measure 2 counts them; nothing says who decides whether each is a notifiable circumstance, a client disclosure, a refund, or an HMRC correction, or in what time.
- The escalation rule covers "an error that reached a client" only when it is recognised as an agent error. Errors that did not reach the client count as a statistic; errors found in old files are not covered at all.
- Seeded known-error files in a live review queue create a risk that a seeded error is filed. No control on that is described.

What would have to change:
- A written error protocol before experiment 1: classification, who decides (the signatory, not the workflow owner), client disclosure, tax-authority correction, insurer notification, time limits, and a record.
- Agree with the seller and the seller's insurer, before any re-performance, that findings will be notified under the current policy; take a specific indemnity.
- Run seeded errors only in a segregated shadow queue that cannot reach a filing path.
- Add remediation cost to Part 14 as a year-one line.

### 6. Client confidentiality and data protection are broken in the sequence as written

Sections: Part 4 diligence and sequence; Part 5 "What clients are told"; Part 3 "The centre may audit ... without notice"; Part 8 principles 6, 7 and 12; Part 9 measures 3 and 4; Part 11 test sets; experiments 1 and 5.

What is wrong:
- Disclosure timing. AI and ownership clauses go into engagement terms "at each renewal" during days 100 to 365, while the three cases run on client work in the same period. Clients whose renewal falls late have AI used on their material before they are told. The RICS standard the draft quotes requires notice "in writing and in advance"; the tax guidance points the same way. The opt-out is meaningless if it arrives after the work.
- Pre-acquisition access. Diligence takes a practice-system export, a week "on the floor" and re-performance of past files by a buyer who does not yet own the firm. That is disclosure of confidential client information to a third party without consent. Consent is mentioned only for experiment 5.
- Group staff inside the firm. The resident, engineer, controller and head of quality are employees of a separate company with access to client files; the centre "may audit ... without notice". No engagement-term authority, processor contract or confidentiality undertaking is described. Model and tool suppliers are contracted by the services company, not the controller, which inverts the processor chain.
- Purpose. "Trains nothing" sits beside test sets built from the firm's own past client files, playbooks refined from corrections on client work, and a trace-reading agent. The draft admits the lawfulness is "argued by analogy" and then proceeds locally anyway; the caution is applied only cross-firm.
- Third parties. At firm B the data subjects are mostly leaseholders, tenants, residents and contractors, not the client. No notice to them, no assessment of automated decisions affecting them (repair triage, arrears, referencing), and no route to a human.
- Assessment. A data-protection assessment is planned only for staff logging. The client-data processing is the high-risk processing.
- Staff. "Record the work, not the worker" is contradicted by review time per named reviewer, a timer, seeded-error catch rates per person tied to pay, and trainee catch rates as a condition of their work counting. That is monitoring and profiling of individuals and needs the full process the draft reserves for screen capture.
- Retention. Agent traces hold client data in rented stores; no retention period, deletion route or position on a vendor's own log retention is given, against a need to keep evidence for the limitation period.

What would have to change:
- No client's material enters an AI workflow until that client has had written notice and a period to object; reorder the sequence so notice precedes the cases, not renewal by renewal.
- Diligence on anonymised or seller-performed samples only, or under client consent; no buyer access to live files before completion.
- The firm contracts with the tool and model suppliers as controller, or the services company is a named processor under a proper contract, disclosed in engagement terms and privacy notices.
- A data-protection impact assessment per case covering clients and third parties, done before the build; a lawful-basis and confidentiality opinion on test sets and playbook learning before experiment 2.
- Treat reviewer measurement as worker monitoring: consult, assess, and limit its use in pay.

### 7. The audit ring-fence does not achieve independence and may not be a viable firm

Sections: Part 2 "Audit is out"; worked example, firm A "Structure".

What is wrong:
- An audit entity "controlled by its responsible individual" that shares a name, premises, staff, systems or referral flow with Firm 1 Ltd is likely a network firm, or at least connected, under the ethical standard. The group's non-audit services to those twelve audit clients (accounts preparation, tax, payroll, bookkeeping, done with agents) then fall under the independence restrictions the carve-out was meant to avoid. "The group takes no fee from it" does not settle the test.
- A single-responsible-individual audit firm with about twelve opinions needs its own registration, quality-management system, cover arrangements for incapacity, PI and monitoring visits. Who does the audit fieldwork (about 2,200 hours) if the staff belong to Firm 1 Ltd? Supplying staff back is a service relationship that reopens the network question.
- The responsible individual is also, in the worked example, a partner who "sells a majority ... and becomes head or signatory on salary" in Firm 1. One person controlling the audit firm while employed by and holding equity in the group-owned firm is the connection.

What would have to change:
- Default to referring the audit book to an unconnected firm and price the lost fees and the client-loss risk on the rest of the relationship. Keep the carve-out entity only with a written view from the audit regulator on network status, and with no shared name, staff or systems.
- Add to diligence: which audit clients also buy non-audit services, and what leaves with them.

### 8. Firm B: an agent that places orders commits trust money, and safety-critical triage cannot be dip-sampled

Sections: worked example, firm B cases 1 and 2; Part 3 rows on client money and on "outward, irreversible ... anything that moves money"; Part 8 principle 3; Part 13 client-money row.

What is wrong:
- Service-charge funds are held on statutory trust. "Orders within the client's limit" placed by an agent create a liability payable from that trust; that is committing money, even if payment release stays under dual authorisation. The design's own band rule ("anything that moves money" needs a person each time) is evaded by drawing the line at payment, not commitment.
- Statutory consultation thresholds apply per leaseholder and can be crossed by a run of small related orders. An agent bounded by a per-order limit will not see the aggregate; the cost of a breach is capped recovery, borne by the client and claimed against the firm.
- Repair triage includes damp and mould, fire doors, gas, electrics, lifts and cladding. A wrong triage is a bodily-injury and building-safety exposure, not a professional error, and may sit outside PI altogether (public liability, or uninsured). "Dip samples of triage" is the stated control. The accountable-person duties on higher-risk buildings are not mentioned.
- "The centre audits" client money gives a non-regulated entity access to client accounts and no defined standing; meanwhile the regulated annual accountant's report is left to the firm. If the group later has an accountancy firm do "the books of the property firms", the reporting accountant is not independent.
- "Notices" and "tenancy documents" are listed as productisable. Statutory notices are validity-critical; an invalid one cannot be undone and loses the client a statutory right.

What would have to change:
- Redefine the money band as "commits or moves": any order is approved by a person, or sits under a per-client, per-building, per-period aggregate limit with consultation thresholds built in as a hard stop.
- A safety category list for triage where a person decides every case, with a maximum response time; confirm public-liability and PI treatment of triage with the insurer.
- Statutory notices stay in "with a person's approval each time" permanently, checked by someone who did not prepare them.
- Client-money assurance by an independent reporting accountant outside the group; define the centre's access in the management agreements or remove it.

### 9. Anti-money-laundering duties are placed on a person the design then surrounds with logs, agents and group auditors

Sections: Part 3 client-acceptance row; Part 8 principles 3 and 6; "A new client is won"; Part 3 audit right.

What is wrong:
- "Everyone can see what is logged about their own work", agent traces, classed corrections and a group head of quality reading registers and traces across firms: any of these can hold or reveal an internal suspicion report or the fact of one. That is a tipping-off and confidentiality exposure for the nominated officer, who is personally liable.
- "An agent prepares checks" for client due diligence. Reliance on automated identity and risk checks must be documented in the firm's risk assessment and policies, with the reporting officer answerable for the tool. No such assessment is in the first hundred days.
- The centre has "a conflict veto" on client acceptance. A veto exercised by a non-supervised entity over acceptance, with no stated basis, will be read as the group taking part in the decision.
- The firm-wide risk assessment must be redone on a change of ownership and of delivery method; the monthly board and eight measures contain nothing on AML.
- Ongoing monitoring: agents that process bank feeds and bookkeeping see the transactions on which suspicion arises. Nothing says how an agent's flag reaches the nominated officer without being written into a widely readable trace, or what happens when the agent does not flag.

What would have to change:
- A segregated AML record that agents, workflow owners and the centre cannot read; agent traces designed so that a referral to the nominated officer leaves no visible marker.
- The firm-wide risk assessment and policies rewritten before any case goes live, signed by the nominated officer, with the tools named.
- Remove the centre's veto or define it narrowly as a group conflict check with the decision recorded by the firm.
- Add an AML line to the monthly review and to the head of quality's audit scope, with access limits.

### 10. The evidence a regulator or insurer would ask for in year four may not exist

Sections: Part 13 "A regulator asks who signed"; Part 8 identity and register; Part 10 undo column; Part 11 models and context store; Part 15 "throw scaffolding away".

What is wrong:
- The promised answer (review record, playbook version, check results, agent's trace) has to be retrievable for the limitation period, which for negligence can run to fifteen years and for tax records at least six. The stack is rented, models are pinned then retired by the vendor, scaffolding is "thrown away" each release, and no retention schedule or archive owner is named. A pinned model that the supplier has withdrawn cannot be re-run to explain an output.
- "Re-review work done since" a rollback has no bound: no way is described to identify every unit touched by a given playbook or model version, or to fund the re-review.
- Agent identity "distinct from any person's" collides with tax-authority and system credentials that are issued to the firm or a named person. Either the agent uses a person's or the firm's credential (so the log shows the person filed it) or it has none. The draft does not say which, and filings "on client approval" still need a named agent submitter.
- The spend cap stops a workflow mid-cycle and "the work falls to people". Near a statutory deadline that is a foreseeable cause of late filing with no named contingency owner or capacity.
- If the group sells or closes a firm, or changes a tool supplier, who holds the traces and release history is not stated; the services company holds part and the firm part.

What would have to change:
- A retention standard: per unit of work, an immutable record of inputs, playbook and model version, check results, reviewer, time and decision, held by the firm in a store it controls, for a stated period, independent of any vendor.
- A version-to-unit index so a faulty version can be traced to every affected file, and a reserve for re-review.
- A credentials policy agreed with each outside body: whose authority the agent acts under and how the submission is attributed.
- No hard spend cap inside a set window before a statutory deadline; a named person and capacity plan for fallback to manual.
