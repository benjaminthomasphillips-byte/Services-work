# The architecture of an AI-native services firm, version 1

Consolidated by Claude, 24 November 2026.

## How it was derived

The architecture comes from seven level-3 redesigns of services firms. Each was designed from the client's outcome, not from today's task list, then reviewed by a builder and a practitioner and revised:

- accountancy for small businesses;
- residential lettings;
- commercial legal for growing companies;
- insurance claims;
- probate;
- home care;
- cross-border payroll.

The elements were then scored three ways:
- by the designs' own authors;
- by two independent scorers who saw the designs with all verdicts removed (a strict architect, and an operator asking whether the wording would guide or mislead a builder);
- by a reconciliation of the three.

Where at least two of the three sources pointed the same way, the change is made here. The working files are in this folder: `architecture-scorecard.md`, `blind-score-a.md`, `blind-score-b.md`, `blind-reconciliation.md`.

**What it is.** A reasoned design, tested against seven cases and two independent readings. It is not evidence that such firms work. All headcount and economics figures are conjecture.

---

## 1. The architecture in one paragraph

An AI-native services firm:
- keeps a shared, live record of the thing it serves, with authority to act recorded in it;
- acts where its client's acts take effect, in whatever money position its legal role requires, within policies written in layers by those with the right to write them;
- sells conditions kept true and moments made fast, and backs what it promises with capital sized for a bad year;
- decides, component by component, whether agents, owned crews or people produce the work, and puts a named person wherever judgement, accountability, opposition, crisis or consent meet the service;
- names every party it affects and whose outcome it serves, and builds its revenue so it never gains from their loss;
- designs for capacity in time and for the service to survive the firm;
- runs on an engine that turns every case into a tested improvement, governed by people who own the definition of good, while it deliberately grows the next generation of those people.

---

## 2. Where it applies: by component, not by service

Every service mixes components. Ask what each component produces.

| What the component produces | What the architecture is for it | Seen most in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Information, a decision, money, or uncontested legal status** | The whole design | Accountancy, payroll, most of probate |
| **Contested status** (an opponent is involved) | The design, plus rules for privilege and for adversaries | Legal, claims |
| **A physical change to a thing** | The control layer, plus owned capacity and capital | Lettings, claims |
| **A human act** (care, comfort, presence) | The governance, payment and workforce design around the act, not the design of the act itself | Home care, probate at the moment of death |

## 3. The core: thirteen elements in five groups

Thirteen elements is close to a checklist. Grouping them shows what each is for.

### Group A. The record and the right to act

- **G1. Shared record.** A live record of the thing the service is about: the business, the home, the estate, the person, the employment. It is owned by whoever owns the thing. Every entry has a known source and is added to, never edited. It is readable by every party with a right to it, and able to outlive the relationship and the firm. Where disclosure to an opponent is possible, advice sits in a separate protected layer, and the working record is written for its most hostile reader.
- **G12. Authority as a record.** Who may act, on what, within what bounds, granted by whom and until when, is signed and held in the record. Where counterparties will not yet rely on it, the firm buys them the same assurance and argues for the law to recognise it.
- **G8. Evidence and adversaries.**
  - Collect only for a stated purpose, more narrowly in a home or on a body.
  - Never track what can be declared.
  - Evidence may always help the person. It may count against them only where law requires or permits it and they were told when it was collected.
  - Design for parties who deceive and who learn: gaps in where evidence came from trigger scrutiny, and fraud handling is kept separate from settlement.

### Group B. Acting in the client's world

- **G6. Capability where information is born.** The firm's capability sits where information is born and decisions are taken. That may be the client's systems, the thing itself, counterparties' systems, or a third party the client deals with in calm times. Where a regulator holds the client responsible, the client oversees the firm live.
- **G2. Acting in the flow, with the money position set by role.** The firm acts where the client's acts take effect (money, commitments, work, destinations), under a mandate that can be withdrawn, and is never a payee that nobody checks.
  - As the client's agent, it acts from the client's account.
  - Where law or contract makes it the principal, it advances money only by necessity, earns nothing from holding it, prices any credit openly, backs the role with capital and makes it transferable.
- **G3. Policy as work, in layers.** The firm makes, tests and maintains the client's policy, and acts within it.
  - Rules come in layers, each owned by whoever has the right to write it: the state, collective agreements, the client.
  - Overrides are allowed and named.
  - A decision that harms a party who did not set the policy may apply only rules that party was shown in advance, with a fast appeal to a person. Otherwise a person decides.
  - The firm works with the rule-writers to change the layers it does not own. Law reform is part of the product where the law is the bottleneck.

### Group C. What is sold and promised

- **G4. Conditions and moments.** The firm sells conditions kept true ("tax already set aside", "the home is dry and legal", "the estate is routed") and moments made fast (a contract signed today, a claim settled this week). Each condition is designed to shorten its moment. Where the thing declines, conditions are judged against its expected path.
- **G13. Promises priced and backed.** The firm guarantees what it controls and never guarantees what the law already gives. Physical promises, credit and losses that hit many clients at once are backed by capital sized for a bad year. Outcome risk beyond that is placed with a capital partner, priced from the firm's own evidence.

### Group D. Who produces, and who is trusted

- **G5. Who produces, and where people stand.**
  - Decide what produces each component: agents for information, decisions, money and status; people or owned crews for physical change and human acts.
  - Where people produce, agents exist to return their time.
  - Put a named person wherever judgement, accountability, opposition, crisis or consent meet the service.
  - Size each group from measured exception rates, and from the review rule that insurers, regulators and courts will accept: sampling, or review of every output.
- **G7. Parties, principal and conflicts.**
  - Name every party affected, whose outcome the firm optimises, and the duty owed to each, ranked by law where the law ranks them. Where the person served is not the payer, the person is the principal.
  - Where a revenue line could rise against a party's interest, keep deciding separate from earning, price by band or form, check a sample independently, and disclose what remains.
  - The firm earns nothing from:
    - holding money;
    - less care;
    - a party's loss;
    - a client staying in a form or relationship that no longer suits it.
  - There is no self-dealing.
- **G9. Succession of judgement.** Agents remove the work through which juniors used to learn. The firm therefore designs and funds a route to judgement that replaces it (simulation, supervised exceptions, rotations), and agrees with the professional bodies that it counts.

### Group E. Resilience in time

- **G11. Demand in time.** Capacity is shaped to deadlines, seasons, daily peaks, correlated physical surges, and promises that run for decades.
- **G10. The service survives the firm.** The client's record, authority and service can move to another provider, or be taken over by one, if the client leaves or the firm fails. That includes custody over long periods where the promise outlasts the firm. This is not proven until a successor has actually run the service.

### Underneath all five: the engine (from step 1)

Every case produces a signal, and an owned process turns signals into tested changes. That process covers:
- corrections sorted by what they mean;
- outcomes captured;
- test suites built from real cases;
- changes released in stages and rolled back if they fail;
- each new model tested and adopted.

People own the evaluator, meaning what counts as good. Agents improve against it and never touch it. Autonomy is a dial for each type of work, raised on evidence and lowered after failure.

## 4. Conditional elements, with the condition that switches each on

| Element | Switches on when | What it requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C2. Standards** | Several parties rely on the record or the rules | Run the standard as a company only where those who must rely on it would trust one the firm runs. Maintain rules standards jointly with the rule-writers |
| **C4. Pooled data** | Pooling is lawful, and either untraceable or used only for purposes the law names | Pool error, outcome and fraud data across clients; price and improve from it |
| **C8. Selling the definition of good** | Clients, peers or their AI can produce the work themselves | License, open or sell layers of the firm's work. Where buyers are rivals, do it through a separate company |
| **C9. Pace set by the person** | The person served is the principal, and the service acts on their body, home or bereavement, or they are in crisis. Not where that person is the opponent or physics sets the clock | Pace and dignity outrank speed |
| **C10. A payer who gains from less service** | The payer benefits from providing less: councils, and insurers on some lines | Separate finding need from paying for it |
| **C11. Decline as the trajectory** | The thing served declines: a body, an ageing building, an estate | Measure against an expected path; plan the end |
| **C12. Unpaid co-producers** | Parties other than the client produce part of the service: family carers, tenants, employees declaring their location | Bring them inside the service; pay them where they want it, otherwise protect them and reduce their burden |
| **C13. Producing workforce** | People or crews produce the service | Supply, contracts, pay and skill are designed to the shape of demand. Own the workforce where speed or continuity decides the outcome |

## 5. The parameters that predict the form

The form the architecture takes in a given service is predicted mainly by these:

| Parameter | Range | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Principals: how many, how aligned, and who is the principal** | One aligned owner; opposed parties; a payer whose interest runs against the beneficiary's | Record ownership, neutrality and advocacy duties, the money position |
| **Agent or principal in law or contract** | The firm acts for the client, or the law makes it the employer or payer | Money position, capital, credit risk |
| **Who writes the rules** | The client; the state; unions and employers through collective agreements | How policy is layered; who must co-own the standard |
| **Continuous or episodic** | Daily cash flow to once in a lifetime | Conditions or moments; surge capacity; how clients are reached when there is no repeat purchase |
| **Who pays and who benefits** | The same party, or split | Who the principal is; checks that revenue does not rise against the beneficiary |
| **Who controls the outcome** | The firm, or a court, a counterparty, a family, physics | What can be guaranteed; when a capital partner is needed |
| **Client size and in-house capability** | Sole trader to a scale-up with its own general counsel, or an insurer | The firm as the client's function, a layer inside the client's systems, or a licensor |
| **What is produced** | Information, contested status, physical change, a human act | Section 2: what the architecture designs |
| **Trajectory of the thing** | Improving, stable or declining | How conditions are judged; whether the end is planned |
| **Honesty of inputs and likelihood of dispute** | Calm and honest, to fraud and litigation | Evidence rules, a protected advice layer, separating fraud from settlement |
| **Horizon of the promise** | Weeks to decades | Custody, long-tail liability, capital, slow feedback |

**Scale parameters** set cost and boundary, not shape. These are volume, the number of jurisdictions and density.

## 7. What the tests taught, across all seven

1. **Redesign, do not automate.** Automating today's steps (level 1) is already being done, and fast, by AI roll-ups such as Dwelly. The gains come from moving to where information is born (level 2) and redesigning the service and the firm's boundary (level 3).
2. **The bottleneck is usually outside the firm.** Owners who do not answer, landlords and contractors who go silent, counterparties and authorities. Redesign removes the wait through contracts, standing policies, money and moving to the source. Sending more messages does not.
3. **The product changes more than the headcount.** In several services the firm sells something bigger than before (a finance function, a yield, prevention, a routed estate), so its people move to different work rather than simply falling in number.
4. **Risk and capital come back** once the firm controls the work. It can then price and back promises, but only for what it controls.
5. **The firm must be trusted by the parties it does not serve:** tenants, claimants, beneficiaries, employees, the person receiving care. That is now core, not hygiene.
6. **Where the product is a human act, labour does not compress.** Agents are judged by the human time they return. The workforce becomes the firm's main design problem.
7. **Judgement must be manufactured.** Agents remove the junior work through which people learned, so the firm needs a deliberate route to its next generation of judges.
8. **Law and institutions are design variables, not only constraints.** Several designs depend on law reform (statutory nominations, wills approved in life, recognised authority records), on counterparties' interfaces, and on the review rule regulators and insurers accept.

## 8. Limits and open questions

- **Origin bias.** Every element's wording owes something to these seven services, and the first wording came from accountancy. The next blind test should use a service none of them shaped. **Statutory audit** is the obvious candidate: the payer is the audited company, while the beneficiaries are shareholders and the public.
- **Thirteen core elements is a lot.** The grouping helps, but further use may show some can merge.
- **The review rule is the biggest unknown for headcount.** Whether regulators, insurers and courts will accept sampling instead of review of every output decides the size of every firm here. Nobody can settle it by reasoning.
- **Limiting the blast radius,** so that one bad change cannot reach every client at once, was raised by one scorer and is untested as an element.
- **"The service survives the firm" is unproven** until a successor has actually run a service.
- **All economics are conjecture.** All company figures cited in the designs (Dwelly, Pennylane, Crosby and others) are self-reported.
