# Blind reconciliation: two blind scorers against the authors

24 November 2026. Sources: `blind-score-a.md` (A, strict architect), `blind-score-b.md` (B, operator), the authors' verdicts in `architecture-scorecard.md` sections 1, 7 and 8, and the architecture tests in `level3-legal.md` (9.1), `level3-claims.md` (9.1, 11.5), `level3-probate.md` (9.1, 11.5), `level3-homecare.md` (10.1) and `level3-payroll.md` (10.1). H = holds, B = bends, X = breaks, NT = not triggered.

**Mapping.** The authors scored E1 to E8 for accountancy, lettings, legal, claims and probate. I mapped E1 to G1, E2 to G2, E3 to G3, E4 to G4, E5 to C1, E6 to C2, E7 to G5 and E8 to G6. Home care scored G1 to G8 and C1 to C9; payroll scored the full list. Their C3 maps to G2 and their C6 to G10. That gives 77 comparable cells. The mapping is uncertain in four places:
- **E5 to C1.** E5 bundled pooled data with guarantees, so an E5 bend may partly be a C4 bend.
- **E7 and E8 to G5 and G6.** Both were rewritten from the authors' own bends, so like is not being compared with like.
- **Home care C7 to current C7.** The authors' break concerned daily peaks, which are now G11. The blind scorers scored only seasonal surge.
- **Payroll C10 and C11.** The design's own table scores them as holds. The scorecard (8.1) treats them as not triggered. I use the scorecard's reading.

For the first five services, the authors left G7, G8, G9, G10, G11 and C4 to C12 unscored as cells. They recorded them only as needs (scorecard section 3).

## 1. Agreement

All three sources agree in 31 of the 77 cells.
- **Accountancy:** G1 to G6 and C1 hold. The architecture was cut from this service, so this is expected and proves little.
- **Information core:** G4, G5, G6, G11, C1, C4, C5 and C8 hold in payroll. G4 holds in claims and probate.
- **Home care:** G5 breaks; G4, G7, C1 and C8 bend; C2 holds. This is the most important agreement. Three independent readings found that people are the centre of home care and agents the edge. They also found that a declining, relational product strains "moments made fast".
- **Legal:** G1 bends, for the same reason in all three sources: a privileged layer that is not written for every reader.

The sources also agree on direction where the authors did not score cells:
- **G7** is the weakest core element. A bends it in five services, B in four, and the authors bent it heavily in home care and payroll.
- **C4** is correctly off in legal.
- **G10** holds in payroll on paper but has not been tested.

## 2. Disagreement with the authors

**Authors harsher (29 cells).** The authors bent elements that both blind scorers held, or broke ones they bent:
- legal G2, G3, G4, G6, C1, C2;
- claims G2, G5, G6, C1, C2;
- probate G2, G3, G5, G6, C1;
- lettings G5;
- home care G1, G6, G8, G10, C4, C7, C9;
- payroll G2, G3, G7, G8, C2.

The likely reason, in about 26 of them, is that the blind scorers read elements rewritten from these very bends. Some examples:
- "commitments" and "destinations" in G2 came from legal and probate;
- the oversight clause in G6 came from claims;
- the layers in G3 and the principal branch in G2 came from payroll;
- the collection limits in G8 came from home care.

So these cells show that the restatements fit the cases they were built from. They are not independent evidence that the elements are general. Three disagreements are real:
- **Home care G6.** The payer still holds band authority and cannot read the live view.
- **Home care C4.** The evidence for prediction is weak, and pooling should be for named purposes only.
- **Home care G1, in part.** The person's own voice comes first, and some parts are hidden from some readers.

**Authors more generous (5 cells).**
- **Lettings G2.** The authors held it; both blind scorers bend it. The yield contract makes the firm a principal by contract, and G2 allows a principal only by law.
- **Payroll C7.** The authors held it; both bend it. Correlated client default is a call on capital, not on capacity.
- **Payroll C10, C11 and C12.** The design held all three. Both blind scorers say they are not triggered or bend. The scorecard had already withdrawn C10 and C11, and the same reasoning applies to C12, because social partners write rules rather than co-produce the service.

The pattern is that authors hold elements that their own case pushed into new ground. The blind scorers ask whether the element as worded tells a builder the right thing.

**Splits (12 cells).** In these the blind scorers disagree with each other and the authors side with one of them: accountancy, lettings and probate C2; claims G1 and G3; probate G1; home care G2, G3 and C5; payroll G1, G9 and C9. Section 3 covers them.

The elements home care proposed (G9, G10 and G11) are the only test outside the original sample. Scored blind against the five earlier services, they did not all hold. A bends G9 in four services and G11 in three; G10 holds almost everywhere.

## 3. Where the two blind scorers differ

A recorded 88 holds, 40 bends, 1 break and 18 not triggered. B recorded 103 holds, 23 bends, 2 breaks and 19 not triggered. The difference comes from method:
- **A asks whether an element was used as stated.** A bends any element whose words do not match the design's mechanism.
- **B asks whether following the words would mislead a builder.** B holds where the purpose carries, and bends or breaks where the literal words give a wrong instruction.

**A is harsher on:**
- **G9 (four bends, against none from B).** The designs train their own judges, not a workforce on the client's path. A says that is outside G9's words; B counts it.
- **G11 (three bends, against none).** Accountancy flattens demand, legal demand is lumpy and probate runs over decades.
- **C5 (five bends, against none).** A requires a literal chain with portable proof. B accepts any designed handling of authority.
- **Accountancy G7.** Subscription revenue does not rise with outcome.
- **The conditions of C7, C9, C11 and C12.**

**B is harsher on:**
- **G1 in claims and payroll.** There are several owners, and payroll needs versioning.
- **G8 in claims (a break).** Read literally, it would forbid declining a fraud on the claimant's own evidence.
- **C2 in lettings and probate.** The one-owner test points the wrong way.
- **C4 in claims.** Fraud pooling must identify people.
- **G2 in lettings,** in agreement with A.

These are cases where obeying the text would do harm. A treats G8 in claims as a bend, because the design simply replaces the rule.

**Their lists of what is missing differ in emphasis.**
- **Only A** names the record as a liability and the separation of the firm from its own platform.
- **Only B** names blast radius (circuit breakers against one error repeated across many clients), naming the principal, and the boundary rule (own the accountable layer, rent the rails).

## 5. Does the reach claim survive?

The claim: the architecture is the whole design where the product is information, a decision, money or legal status, and only the support layer where it is a human relationship.

**It survives in outline but not as worded.** All three sources agree on the extremes:
- Accountancy and payroll are designed almost wholly by the architecture.
- Home care is not: G5 breaks there.

**Both blind scorers find a third category the claim omits: a physical thing kept right** (lettings, claims). Its margin and its main risk sit in owned crews and capital that the architecture does not design. The authors had briefly said "or a scheduled physical task" (7.5) and then dropped it (8.5). The blind evidence says it should come back, but as a separate case, not as part of the whole-design category.

**Both also say "support layer" undersells home care.** There the architecture shapes the payer, the price, the workforce, the record and the market. It does not shape the act of care itself.

A adds that contested legal status bends more than the claim allows. B shows the split runs by component, not by service.

**Restated:** for each component, ask what is produced.
- **Information, a decision, money or uncontested legal status:** the architecture is the design.
- **Contested status:** it is the design, plus adversarial and privilege rules.
- **A physical change to a thing:** it is the control layer, plus owned capacity and capital.
- **A human act:** it is the governance, payment and workforce design around that act, not the design of the act.

## 6. Recommended final element list

**Core**
- **G1. Shared record.** A live record of the thing, owned by whoever owns it. Every entry has a known source and is added to, never edited. It is readable by every party with a right to it, and able to outlive the relationship and the firm. Where disclosure to an opponent is possible, advice sits in a separate protected layer, and the operational record is written for its most hostile reader.
- **G2. Acting in the flow; money position by role.** As now, but the principal branch applies "where law *or contract* makes the firm principal".
- **G3. Policy as work, in layers.** As now, with two changes:
  - Decisions that harm a party who did not set the policy apply only rules that party was shown in advance, with a fast appeal to a person. Otherwise a person decides.
  - The firm works with the rule-writers to change the layers it does not own.
- **G4. Conditions and moments.** As now. Where the thing declines, conditions are judged against its expected path.
- **G5. Who produces, and where people stand.**
  - Decide what produces each component: agents for information, decisions, money and status; people or owned crews for physical change and human acts.
  - Where people produce, agents exist to return their time.
  - Put a named person wherever judgement, accountability, opposition, crisis or consent meet the service.
  - Size each group from measured exception rates and the review rule that insurers, regulators and courts accept.
- **G6. Capability where information is born.** Unchanged.
- **G7. Parties, principal and conflicts.**
  - Name every party affected, whose outcome the firm optimises, and the duty owed to each, ranked by law where law ranks them. Where the person served is not the payer, the person is the principal.
  - Where a revenue line could rise against a party's interest, separate deciding from earning, price by band or form, check a sample independently and disclose what remains.
  - The firm earns nothing from holding money, from less care, from a party's loss or from a client staying in a form or relationship that no longer suits it. There is no self-dealing.
- **G8. Evidence and adversaries.**
  - Collect only for a stated purpose, more narrowly in a home or on a body. Never track what can be declared.
  - Evidence may always help the person. It may count against them only where law requires or permits it and they were told at collection.
  - Design for parties who deceive and improve: provenance gaps trigger scrutiny, and fraud is separated from settlement.
- **G9. Succession of judgement.** The firm designs and funds a route to judgement that replaces the work agents removed, and agrees with professional bodies that it counts.
- **G10. The service survives the firm.** Unchanged. It is not proven until a successor has run the service.
- **G11. Demand in time.** Capacity is shaped to deadlines, seasons, daily peaks, correlated physical surges and promises that run for decades.
- **G12. Authority as a record** (from C5). Who may act, on what, within what bounds, granted by whom and until when, is signed and held in the record. Where counterparties will not yet rely on it, the firm buys them the same assurance and argues for the law to recognise it.
- **G13. Promises priced and backed** (from C1). The firm guarantees what it controls and never guarantees what law already gives. Physical and credit promises and correlated loss are backed by capital sized for a bad year, with outcome risk beyond that placed with a capital partner.

**Conditional**
- **C2. Standards.** Applies when several parties rely on the record or rules. The firm runs the standard as a company only where those who must rely on it would trust one it runs. Rules standards are co-maintained with the rule-writers.
- **C4. Pooled data.** Applies when pooling is lawful and either untraceable or used only for purposes the law names.
- **C8. Selling the definition of good.** Applies when clients, peers or their AI can produce. The firm licenses, opens or sells layers of its work. Where buyers are rivals, it does this through a separate company.
- **C9. Pace set by the person.** Applies when the person served is the principal and the service acts on their body, home or bereavement, or they are in crisis. It does not apply where that person is the opposing party or physics sets the clock.
- **C10. A payer who gains from less service.** Finding need is separated from paying for it.
- **C11. Decline as the trajectory.** Unchanged.
- **C12. Unpaid co-producers.** Applies to parties other than the client. Where they cannot be paid, they get protection and less burden instead.
- **C13. Producing workforce** (from G9). Applies where people or crews produce the service. Supply, contracts, pay and skill are designed to the shape of demand, and owned where speed or continuity decides the outcome.

Blast radius should be tested in the next scoring round. One caution remains: thirteen core elements is close to the checklist the scorecard warned of (7.2). Every element's wording now owes something to one or more of these seven cases, so the next blind test should use a service none of them shaped. Statutory audit is the obvious candidate.
