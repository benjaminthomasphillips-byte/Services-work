# Blind score B: the architecture against seven level 3 redesigns

Independent scorer, 24 November 2026. I read only `blind/architecture-elements.md` and the seven `blind/level3-*.md` designs. I read each element as an operator would: would it guide this firm's builder well if they followed it as written? If following it as written would mislead the builder, I scored it BENDS or BREAKS, even where the design contains something like it.

Key: **H** holds · **B** bends · **X** breaks · **NT** not triggered (conditional only).

## 1. General, conditional, drop or merge, missing

**General (keep as core):** G1, G2, G3, G6, G9, G10 and G11 held or bent mildly everywhere. G4 is general for every service except a human-relationship one. G7 and G8 are general in intent but wrong in wording (section 3).

**Promote to core.**
- **C5 (mandate chain)** was triggered and held in all seven. Authority always passes between people or is checked by counterparties.
- **C1 (priced outcomes)** was triggered in all seven. "Guarantee what you control, put the tail with capital, never sell what law gives" is general.
- **C4 (pooled data)** was triggered in six. Its condition needs fixing (below) more than its status does.

**Keep as conditional:** C2, C7, C8, C9, C10, C11 and C12. Each was genuinely off in some services. Several conditions are wrongly stated:
- **C9.** "Acts on a person's life" catches almost everything, including a sole trader's tax. It should read "acts on a person's body, home or bereavement, or the person is in crisis". It should also say pace is *not* the person's where they are the opposing party (legal) or where physics sets the clock (claims mitigation).
- **C10.** Drop "public" from the title. The condition text ("the payer gains from less service") correctly catches insurers in claims.
- **C12.** Exclude the client itself; otherwise every client who answers a question qualifies. Add that where law limits payment (lay executors), protection and lighter burden replace pay.
- **C4.** "The individual cannot be traced" excludes lawful pooling that must identify people (fraud databases, credit). It should read: lawful, and either untraceable or used only for the purpose the law allows.
- **C2.** The "one owner, therefore platform" test points the wrong way in lettings and probate. The real test is whether the parties who must *rely* on the record would trust one run by a competitor.
- **C7.** It mixes physical surge with correlated financial loss ("many clients failing together").

**Drop or merge.**
- Merge **C7 into G11**: capacity shaped to time, including the correlated tail. Move correlated financial loss into a new capital element (below).
- Merge G8's first clause ("every input has a known source") into **G1**, which already says it.
- **G5** should become a statement about what produces each component (section 3), not a core claim that agents produce.

**Missing.** Several designs needed these, and no element covers them.
1. **Blast radius.** All seven fear one correlated error repeated across hundreds of clients: a tax misreading, a rule pack, a policy template, a pension-tax reading. All seven build circuit breakers on money, filings, declines or terminations, plus limits on how far each release reaches. Nothing in the architecture asks for this.
2. **Adversaries as a design input.** Claims (fraud made with AI, credit hire), payroll and accountancy (payee-change fraud), lettings (mule accounts), legal (the other side) and probate (caveats, coercion) all design provenance, payee rules and breakers against a party who improves. G7 gives duties to opponents; it says nothing about designing against them.
3. **Accountability that insurers and regulators will accept.** In every design, headcount turns on "the review rule" (sampling against per-output review). Every design also settles insurance and liability terms before launch. This is the binding constraint on the people count, and it is absent.
4. **Succession of judgement.** Accountancy, legal, claims and probate all find that the school which made their judges closes, and they build a training route. G9 is written about supply on the client's critical path, not about growing the firm's own judges.
5. **Naming the principal when payer, chooser and beneficiary differ.** Home care (council, family, person), probate (dead client, executors, beneficiaries), claims (insurer and policyholder), payroll (client and employee) and lettings (landlord and tenant) all had to settle this first. G7 lists duties but never says whose outcome the firm optimises.
6. **The boundary rule.** Every design converged on: own the accountable and scarce layer; rent the rails; open what counterparties must trust. Only C2 touches this, and only for standards.
7. **The state and the law as design variables.** HMRC trusted status, statutory nominations and a will proved in life, capitated commissioning, authorities reading the event stream, a protocol owned by a trade body: law reform and the state's own systems sit inside the product plan in five designs.
8. **Capital for physical and credit promises.** Lettings (repair pool), claims (restoration variance) and payroll (correlated client default) each need a balance sheet sized for a bad year. C1 covers outcome guarantees; nothing covers correlated capital.

## 2. The two-part reach claim

The claim: the architecture is the whole design where the product is information, a decision, money or legal status, and only the support layer where the product is a human relationship.

**It holds in outline but is wrong in two ways.**

- **It is true at the extremes.** Accountancy, legal and payroll are almost wholly designed by the architecture. Home care is the clear counter-case: G5 breaks and G4 bends, because the product is a human act.
- **It misses a third kind of product: the physical state of a thing.** Lettings (a home kept legal, dry and warm) and claims (a home restored by a date) are not information products with a physical edge. Their profit pool, their survival in a price collapse and their main risk all sit in owned physical capacity: technicians, turn crews, drying crews, and a repair pool backed by capital. The architecture designs the control layer for these firms (records, policy, mandate, guarantees). It does not design the physical network or the capital behind physical promises. For these firms the architecture is the nervous system, not the whole design.
- **The split runs by component, not by service.** Every service has a human-relationship component: probate's bereavement guides and mediators (the largest line), legal's adversarial edge and fractional GCs, claims' crisis team, payroll's employee relations. In home care, the architecture's information logic is exactly what frees carers' time. And the conditionals added for care (G9, G11, C9 to C12) govern the firm's market, payer and workforce, which is more than a "support layer".

**Restated reach claim.** For each component of a service, ask what is produced. Where the product is information, a decision, money or legal status, the architecture is the design. Where it is a physical change to a thing, the architecture is the control layer, and the design also needs owned capacity and capital. Where it is a human act, the architecture protects and pays for that act and shapes the workforce and the payer around it; it does not design the act itself.

## 3. The three weakest elements as stated, and restatements

### 1. G7, duties and controls
**Why it is weak.** It states absolutes that no good design met. In claims, owned crews earn more as losses grow. In legal, revenue per commitment rises with volume. In lettings, the exit desk earns from a sale it advises on. In home care, a weekly price gains when care falls. Each design controlled the conflict by structure (separation, firewalls, banding, independent re-checks, disclosure); none banned it. G7 also never says whose outcome counts when payer and beneficiary differ. A builder who followed it literally would either reject the best revenue lines or claim a purity they do not have.

**Restated.** Name every party the service touches, whose outcome the firm optimises, and how the law ranks the rest (neutrality, advocacy, or a statutory order). Where any revenue line could rise against a party's interest, break the link by structure: keep who decides apart from who earns, price by band or form rather than by scope or time held, check a sample independently, and disclose what conflict remains. Never earn from holding a client's money, from less care, or from a party's loss.

### 2. G8, evidence rules
**Why it is weak.** "Used against the person who produced it only where the law compels" would stop an insurer declining a fraud on the claimant's own evidence (it breaks in claims). It would also stop a letting agent using move-in evidence in a deposit dispute. The claims design's own rule is better: evidence may always help the person, and may count against them only where they were told at the time. The first clause duplicates G1.

**Restated.** Every item of evidence carries its provenance and is never edited, only added to. Collect only for a stated purpose, more narrowly in a home or on a body, and never track what can be declared. Evidence may always be used in the person's favour. It may be used against them only where law requires or permits it *and* they were told at collection that it could be. Scrutiny is triggered by gaps in provenance, not by suspicion of the person.

### 3. G5, people at the edges
**Why it is weak.** "Agents do the production" is false for home care, where people produce and agents protect their time (it breaks). It also understates lettings and claims, where owned physical crews are the margin and the line that survives a price collapse. "Sized by the service" admits the problem but gives the builder no way to decide the size. Following it, a builder would treat the care worker or the drying crew as an overhead to shrink.

**Restated.** For each component, decide what produces it: agents for information, decisions, money and legal status; people or owned crews for physical change and for human acts. Where people produce, the agents' job is to remove everything that takes them away from the work. Wherever judgement, accountability, opposition, crisis or consent in the moment meet the service, put a named person there. Size each group from measured exception rates and the review rule that insurers and regulators accept, not from capability forecasts.

*Close behind:* **C2**, whose single-owner test for running a platform points the wrong way in lettings and probate, and **C7**, whose condition mixes physical surge with correlated financial loss. Restatements for both are in section 1.
