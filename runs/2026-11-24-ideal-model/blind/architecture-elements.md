# The architecture to be scored: elements only, no verdicts

Each element is a claim about how an AI-native services firm should be built. Core elements are meant to apply to every service. Conditional elements are meant to apply only when their stated condition is met.

## Core elements

- **G1. Shared record.** A shared, live record of the thing the service is about. Every entry has a known source. It is owned by whoever owns the thing, readable by every party with a right to it, written as if its most hostile reader will see it, and able to outlive the relationship.
- **G2. Acting in the flow, with the money position set by legal role.** The firm acts where the client's acts take effect: money, commitments, work, destinations. It does so under a mandate that can be withdrawn, and it is never a payee that nobody checks.
  - Where the firm is the client's agent, it acts from the client's account.
  - Where the law makes the firm the principal, it may advance money by necessity. It then earns nothing from holding it, prices any credit openly, backs the role with capital and makes the role transferable.
- **G3. Policy as work, in layers.** The firm makes, tests and maintains the client's policy, and acts within it.
  - Rules come in layers, each owned by whoever has the right to write it: the state, collective agreements, the client.
  - Overrides are allowed and named.
  - Decisions that harm a party who did not set the policy need a person.
- **G4. Conditions and moments.** The firm sells conditions kept true, and moments made fast. Each condition is designed to shorten its moment.
- **G5. People at the edges, sized by the service.** Agents do the production. People stand where judgement, accountability, physical work, opposition and crisis meet the service. The size of each edge depends on the service.
- **G6. Capability where information is born.** The firm's capability sits where information is born and decisions are taken: in the client's systems, in the thing itself, in counterparties' systems, or with a third party where the client is calm. Where a regulator holds the client responsible, the client oversees the firm live.
- **G7. Duties to every party, and controls on the firm.**
  - The firm has duties to parties who do not pay and to parties who oppose the client: neutrality, advocacy, or duties ranked by law.
  - There is no self-dealing.
  - The firm's revenue rises only when the client's outcome improves.
  - The firm gains nothing when the client's interest moves away from the firm.
- **G8. Evidence rules.**
  - Every input has a known source.
  - Collection is limited where the service enters a home or a body.
  - Evidence is used against the person who produced it only where the law compels, and that person is told.
- **G9. The workforce as a designed condition.** Where workers sit on the client's critical path, their condition, supply and skill are designed and managed as a condition of the service.
- **G10. The service survives the firm.** The client's record, mandate and service can move to another provider, or be taken over by one, if the client leaves or the firm fails. That includes custody over long periods where the promise outlasts the firm.
- **G11. Capacity shaped to demand in time.** Capacity is designed around the time shape of demand: deadlines, seasons, daily peaks.

## Conditional elements, with the condition that switches each on

- **C1. Priced and guaranteed outcomes.** Applies when the firm controls the outcome or can price it. The firm guarantees what it controls and routes outcome risk beyond that to a capital partner. It never guarantees what the law already gives.
- **C2. Standards.** Applies when several parties rely on the record or the rules. The record standard follows the record's owners. The rules standard is opened and maintained with the rule-writers. The firm runs it as a platform company only when the record has one owner.
- **C4. Pooled data.** Applies when pooling is lawful and the individual cannot be traced.
- **C5. Mandate chain with portable proof of authority.** Applies when authority changes hands, or when counterparties need proof before acting.
- **C7. Surge capacity.** Applies when demand is correlated and physically constrained, such as storms or many clients failing together.
- **C8. Licensed definition of good.** Applies when the client has its own team and AI. The firm licenses its playbooks, tests or rule packs, or opens them, rather than doing the work.
- **C9. Pace set by the person.** Applies when the service acts on a person's life or body, or when the person is in crisis.
- **C10. A rationed public payer.** Applies when the payer gains from less service. Finding need is then separated from paying for it.
- **C11. Decline as the trajectory.** Applies when the thing served declines. Performance is measured against an expected path, and the end is planned.
- **C12. Unpaid co-producers.** Applies when unpaid parties produce part of the service. They are brought inside the service and paid where they want to be.

*C3 (holding money) has been folded into G2. C6 (custody beyond the firm) has been folded into G10.*
