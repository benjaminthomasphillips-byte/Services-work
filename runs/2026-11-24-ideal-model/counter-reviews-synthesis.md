# Counter-reviews: synthesis

24 November 2026. Five practitioner reviews of `step-1-the-engine.md` and `step-2-what-is-left.md`: a senior partner at a commercial law firm (Law), an independent SME insurance broker (Broker), an actuary (Actuary), an independent estate and lettings agent (Agent) and a freight forwarder's commercial director (Freight).

**How to read this.** Each reviewer separates what they know from running their business from what they recall or guess. I keep that split. "Experience" means first-hand. "Checked" means the reviewer verified it (Rightmove 2025 figures, Renters' Rights dates, FCA PS25/21, the EU customs reform timetable, Flexport's cuts). "Memory" means unverified recall (for example *Mazur*, *Ayinde*, PACCAR, Munich Re's AI cover, CMR limits, the carrier acquisitions). Every reviewer's 2035 picture is a guess, and they say so.

---

## 1. Where they agree with each other against the papers

**1. The guarantee strategy is wrong as written (P3, Q4). Four of five: Law, Broker, Actuary, Freight.** All four say the bundle of risk and control earns money, and all four say the papers have its mechanism wrong.
- Law (experience): in transactions the bundle already exists, held by warranty and indemnity insurers, and lawyers only supply the evidence.
- Broker (experience): the bundle is an MGA, and capacity can be withdrawn after one bad year.
- Actuary: no capacity provider will price a long-tail guarantee from the operator's own young, self-graded data. Selling a money guarantee without authorisation is probably an offence under FSMA.
- Freight (experience): a forwarder does not control the work, so it cannot bundle anything. The asset owners can.

The Agent is the exception, for a reason section 2 explains.

**2. Correlated error is the central risk, not hygiene. Four of five: Broker, Actuary, Agent, Freight.** The Broker has lived through it with COVID business-interruption wordings. The Agent expects it first in lettings under Renters' Rights. The Actuary joins the two points the papers keep apart: an engine that cuts average error while putting thousands of clients on one playbook can *raise* the capital a guarantee needs. Those are the monoline insurers of 2007–08 (memory).

**3. The incumbent with distribution wins (step 2, section 3). All five.** Each calls the robo-advice paragraph the most important in step 2. Each names the incumbent: elite firms and in-house teams; PE-backed consolidators; insurers and Lloyd's capacity; portals; carriers and CargoWise.

**4. The gatekeeper already exists (Q3). All five.** Q3 treats the trusted checker as something that may emerge. In practice it is already there:
- legal directories and panel reviews (Law, experience);
- insurers' claims data and Acturis (Broker);
- Rightmove, whose revenue per advertiser rose 6% in 2025 (Agent, checked);
- CargoWise (Freight, experience).

The Actuary adds a new one: the independent AI assurer.

**5. Profit is set by things outside the engine. Four of five: Broker, Freight, Law, Agent.** Revenue moves with things the papers' cost-per-case logic ignores:
- the insurance cycle and commission (Broker, experience);
- the carrier rate cycle (Freight, experience);
- leverage and realisation (Law, experience);
- the portal toll (Agent).

**6. Regulation is the gating item, not a counter-argument. Four of five: Actuary, Law, Broker, Agent.** Examples:
- FSMA and insurance distribution rules (Actuary);
- reserved litigation activities (Law, memory of *Mazur*);
- Consumer Duty and fair value of commission (Broker);
- redress schemes, Trading Standards and Renters' Rights (Agent).

**7. The firm's own insurability is missing. Three of five: Law, Broker, Actuary.** The professional indemnity insurer may set the limits on autonomy before the regulator does. The Actuary believes, without checking, that carriers are moving to exclude AI losses.

**8. None would back a new operator competing head-on in their own market.** Four would sell the engine to incumbents instead:
- managed legal operations for in-house teams (Law);
- fair presentation and renewal software for independent brokers (Broker);
- sales progression for agents and conveyancers (Agent);
- a neutral exceptions desk for forwarders (Freight).

The Actuary would back an independent assurer. This is the strongest single signal in the set.

## 2. Where they disagree, and why

- **Does the warranty work?** The Agent says yes: "no sale, no fee" is already a warranty, and a lettings compliance warranty fits. The other four say no. The difference is the Actuary's own test: the agent controls the work, the outcomes are fast (arrears, voids) and the tail is short. Tax and building warranties are long-tail. Freight risk sits on assets the forwarder does not run.
- **How fast outcomes show.** The Agent says agency is quick to check, with sale agreed, fall-through and completion in weeks. The Broker, the Actuary and Freight (customs audits about three years later) say the outcomes that matter are rare and late. The Agent's trap is different: there is no counterfactual price, so the engine optimises towards the agent's interest.
- **Signing for a system (P6, Q4.3).** The Actuary says regulators have accepted it for decades: the Statement of Actuarial Opinion and PRA SS1/23. Law says the direction is towards more personal accountability for each output. The regimes differ. Model governance in insurance and banking signs for processes. Reserved legal activities attach to acts done for a client.
- **Buying books cheaply (Q9).** Freight agrees. The Broker says no: PE consolidators pay full multiples (experience). The Agent says lettings books trade on recurring income. Law notes how poorly outside capital has done in law (memory: Slater & Gordon, DWF). The price depends on whether a funded consolidator is already buying.
- **The talent window (Q8).** Law and Freight agree with it. The Agent says commission-paid negotiators go self-employed on hybrid platforms instead. Pay structure decides where the released talent goes.
- **Who wins by 2035.** Each names a different winner (guess): asset owners (Freight), consolidators and MGAs (Broker), elite firms and larger in-house teams (Law), portals and data platforms (Agent), independent assurers (Actuary). That follows from who holds the scarce asset in each industry, which the papers do not parameterise.

## 3. What each knows that the papers lack

**Law.** The buyer, a general counsel, already diagnoses. The threat is insourcing, not a second opinion. Acting for competitors makes learning across clients a conflicts problem, and outside-counsel guidelines restrict training on client material. Litigation funding is where law already bundles risk with work (experience; PACCAR from memory). US ownership rules cap any outside-capital leader. Partnerships distribute their profit and hold no capital.

**Broker.** In insurance the harm from a wrong diagnosis is under-buying, and the regulator judges by needs, not cheapness. Fair presentation under the Insurance Act 2015 is legally weighted client input. Micro-SME cover is already commoditised. The 2022 price-walking ban covered personal lines only, which corrects a comparison in Q1.

**Actuary.** Credibility theory, reserving, adverse selection, moral hazard, and the capital charge for an accumulated tail. FSMA's definition of insurance. Ready-made governance for "people govern the evaluator". Hartford Steam Boiler (memory) worked because the inspector was independent of the operator.

**Agent.** Clients with an emotional stake use checkers to justify the answer they wanted. Physical coordination and sales progression are most of the job, and roughly a quarter to a third of agreed sales fall through (memory). Hybrid self-employed platforms are a live test of "the engine hosts professionals". Home-buying reform is the property case for Q5.

**Freight.** Revenue is a spread on capacity the forwarder does not own. Concentration comes from buying power and networks. Exceptions arrive in bursts (Suez, the Red Sea). Working capital and customs guarantees absorb the balance sheet. Digital forwarders already ran Strategy A and retrenched (checked). The EU customs data hub, from 2028, is a dated Q5 case (checked).

## 4. Changes the papers should make, ranked

1. **Rewrite P3 and Q4.** Make the legal route (performance warranty, distributed insurance, or a captive) the first decision. Name the MGA or coverholder structure. Price from market data plus independent assurance until the firm's own loss record has developed, which the Actuary puts at about 2032. Add the conditions under which the bundle works: the firm controls the work, the tail is short and correlation is capped. *Why:* four reviewers, including the one whose profession prices this.
2. **Move accumulation management out of hygiene and into P4 and step 1, section 6.** Cap clients per playbook version, define named events and buy event reinsurance. State the tension between scale and correlated tail. *Why:* four reviewers, and it decides whether P3 is possible.
3. **Add revenue drivers to step 1, section 4, and to Q1.** Commission, spread, cycle, leverage and tolls sit beside cost per case. *Why:* four reviewers say the papers misread where profit comes from.
4. **Split Q2** into over-supply credence goods (repairs, litigation) and under-supply ones (insurance, pensions, compliance). Add in-house substitution as a competitor and the emotionally invested buyer. *Why:* three reviewers with three different failure modes.
5. **Restate Q3 as existing gatekeepers**, named per industry, with the independent assurer as a separate actor. *Why:* all five.
6. **Add the firm's insurability to P8 and step 1, section 5.** *Why:* three reviewers. It is a first-year cost and may cap autonomy.
7. **P6:** borrow actuarial and model-risk governance, and note that reserved legal activities point the opposite way. *Why:* the Actuary and Law.
8. **P7:** add conflicts between competing clients, and say that outcome data must be negotiated with insurers or vendors who already hold more of it. *Why:* Law, the Broker and Freight.
9. **Q9:** make the price of books depend on consolidator competition. Add US ownership rules for law. *Why:* the Broker, the Agent and Law.
10. **P4:** demote the fixed cost of evaluation as a concentration driver. Add buying power, network, capital and capacity. *Why:* Freight and the Broker.
11. **P1 and P5:** add a crisis regime for bursty exceptions, and note which way the proxy is biased. *Why:* Freight and the Agent.
12. **Section 4, Q4 and Q8:** count working capital and reserves as calls on capital that come before warranties (Freight, Actuary). Add the hybrid-platform evidence to Q8 (Agent). Correct the price-walking scope in Q1 (Broker).

## 5. What this suggests for step 3

**New parameters:**
- **Revenue mechanism:** fee, commission, spread or contingency; who sets the price; exposure to the cycle.
- **Direction of credence:** over-supply or under-supply.
- **Control of the work that creates the risk:** whether the firm runs the operation or coordinates others' assets.
- **The liability itself:** length of tail, how correlated it is, and the legal route for carrying it.
- **The named incumbent toll-holder:** portal, operating-system vendor, directory, platform or capacity provider.
- **In-house capability of the buyer.**
- **Insurability** in the professional indemnity market.
- **Working capital intensity.**
- **How bursty exceptions are.**
- **Conflicts across clients.**
- **Competition for acquisitions:** whether a funded consolidator is already buying.
- **Pending reforms with dates,** as timing parameters: agent licensing, home-buying reform, the EU customs hub, Renters' Rights phases, PACCAR legislation.
- **The engine as a product for incumbents.** Score each service on whether selling the engine to existing players beats operating. The reviewers' convergence on this suggests it is a winning form in its own right.

**Parameters to change or drop:**
- Split "how fast outcomes can be checked" into **signal speed** and **counterfactual visibility**.
- Promote "share of coordination and physical work" from seventh in the list to first-order.
- Replace "whether risk can be bundled" with the control, tail and route parameters above.
- Replace "jurisdiction: local or convergent" with **ownership rules and reserved activities**.
- Test "who holds live records" for portability: Freight says shipment records move easily.
- Drop the fixed cost of evaluation as a ranking factor for concentration. Keep it only as a cost.
