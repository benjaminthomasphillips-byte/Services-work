# Practitioner review of the level 3 accountancy redesign

Review by the managing partner of a cloud-ledger practice, 24 November 2026. Labels: **[seen, my practice]** is my own experience with clients, staff and HMRC; **[seen]** is a public fact; **[reasoned]** follows from logic; **[conjecture]** is plausible towards 2035. I have kept experience and conjecture apart on purpose.

## My overall view

This is the right shape. The gap is the problem, and closing it removes work rather than speeding it up. I have watched bank feeds and cloud ledgers cut our bookkeeping hours, but the January peak never went away. It never went away because the tax stock still built up unpaid and unapproved. The paper names that correctly [reasoned].

My concerns sit in three places. The first is the client's hand on the money. The second is the true size of the exceptions. The third is where the next generation of judgement comes from. On some points the paper is too cautious.

## Where I think it will not work as written

**1. A revocable pot is not "tax already gone".** The pot belongs to the client and one tap revokes the mandate. In my experience, owners raid any tax pot they can see when cash is tight. They do it in the very months it matters [seen, my practice]. So "safe to spend" will be true in good months and false in bad ones. The design needs friction on withdrawal, not just on granting. One option is a pot that pays HMRC directly on a schedule, so the money has left the client's control. HMRC's Budget Payment Plan already lets self-assessment customers pay weekly or monthly towards their next bill [seen, gov.uk, general knowledge, not checked in this run]. That is closer to "gone" than a revocable sweep. It also works today, without waiting for commercial VRP to widen.

**2. One hour of policy will not remove 90% of questions.** Owners do not think in policies. They think in stories about last month [seen, my practice]. The hard transactions are not the repeating ones. They are Amazon orders mixing personal and business items, a personal card used for fuel, cash takings, and a payment to a brother-in-law that is partly a loan and partly wages. Policies handle the easy 70%. The bank-rule features in today's ledgers already do that [seen]. The rest is where the questions live. I would model 60–75% removal and test it. At that rate partner ratios sit nearer 400 than 1,000 [conjecture].

**3. One-tap moment questions decay.** Reply rates on receipt-capture prompts start high and fall within a quarter. Owners learn to ignore the buzz [seen, my practice]. Design for that. Ask fewer questions, rank them by tax value, and let unanswered items fall back to a policy default that is safe for the owner. Do not assume a minute-fresh answer. Also, a dated answer helps in an enquiry, but HMRC tests substance, not timestamps [reasoned].

**4. "Side effect" undersells the exceptions.** Payroll is not a side effect of contracts. It is starters, leavers, statutory sick pay, maternity, attachment orders, pension auto-enrolment and the director who wants a bonus tomorrow [seen, my practice]. Year-end accounts still need stock, work in progress, accruals and director judgement. FRS 102 changes from 2026 add lease and revenue judgements [seen, general knowledge]. Landlord data from letting agents is late, messy and guarded [seen, my practice]. None of this breaks the design. But the judgement desk at 3 people for 5,000 clients in 2035 looks thin to me [conjecture].

**5. The embedded lead ratio carries the whole profit case, and 1:15–25 is too high.** Fractional finance directors I know handle about four to eight companies [seen, my practice]. Agents will lift that, but board meetings, investor calls and bad weeks do not compress. I would plan for 1:8–12 [conjecture]. The fix is price, not ratio. At £12,000–30,000 the Function tier is cheap against a part-time FD. Charge £30,000–50,000 for the top band. The paper is too cautious here.

**6. Named partners also field everything else.** Owners ring their accountant about mortgage references, SA302s, student finance, child benefit, grant forms and council letters [seen, my practice]. None of it is in the 24 case types. It is real demand and a real source of trust. Either price it in Clear and Core, or a cheaper rival will win the relationship on it [reasoned].

**7. Money movement changes the firm's risk class.** Once we initiate payments, we are in the fraud chain. Mandatory reimbursement for authorised push payment fraud came in from October 2024 [seen]. Liability between bank, payments partner and firm will be argued case by case. Our professional indemnity insurers will ask whether the cover responds to a payment error or only to advice [seen, my practice: insurers ask about scope every renewal]. Settle that before the wedge, not after the first incident [reasoned].

**8. Who trains the judges?** "Nobody prepares anything" is the right aim. But my best seniors learned judgement by preparing accounts and getting them wrong under review [seen, my practice]. Apprenticeships and ACA or ACCA training need practical experience. If agents do all preparation, the pipeline to the judgement desk dries up by the early 2030s [reasoned]. The paper needs a deliberate training route: shadowing the recomputer, building test cases, owning a small book of exceptions under supervision.

## Where it is too cautious

**Taking HMRC as fixed.** HMRC wants digital interaction and on-time payment [seen]. A firm that can show high pot balances and clean data for thousands of clients has a case for a trusted-agent status: pre-cleared returns, lighter enquiries, a direct payment interface. I would pursue that from 2028, not 2034 [conjecture].

**Treating certainty as a later product.** Fee protection insurance is sold widely today [seen]. A guarantee that the bill matches the estimate, for reasons we could have known, can launch inside year one on a capped basis. It is the clearest sales message we have [reasoned].

**The sole-trader named partner.** At 1:1,000, the partner is a phone number most clients never use. Pool it instead. Have a small team for life events and decisions, and say so plainly. Owners value a fast answer over a name [seen, my practice].

**Banks as partners only.** The paper fears banks taking Clear. I think banks do not want to carry tax error risk [reasoned]. We should offer to carry it, white-labelled, earlier than 2028.

## What I would test first

1. Pot withdrawal rates, by month, with and without the Budget Payment Plan route.
2. Questions removed per hour of policy-setting, by segment.
3. Moment-question reply rates in months one, three and six.
4. Feed breakage per client per year. Re-consent and bank outages still break feeds in my practice [seen, my practice].
5. Insurer and payments-partner terms for firm-initiated payments.

## Bottom line

The design survives contact with my clients better than most I have read. Its failures are behavioural, not technical. Owners will revoke, ignore and phone. Build for that and the economics still work, mostly through a better-priced Function tier. Ignore it, and the 2035 core of 24 becomes something nearer 40 [conjecture].
