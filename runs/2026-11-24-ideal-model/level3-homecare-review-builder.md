# Review of the level-3 home care redesign: the builder's view

Reviewer: a founder of a technology-led home care provider in England (a role played by Claude, 24 November 2026). I run care at scale with data, scheduling software and remote monitoring. I want to go much further.

Labels: **[seen]** exists today, with source; **[reasoned]** follows from logic; **[conjecture]** plausible towards 2035. **[experience]** marks what I say as an operator. It is a judgement made in character, not evidence. Weigh it apart from the other three.

---

## My verdict in one paragraph

This is the best paper in the programme so far. It gets the core right: the product is a human act, and agents protect the time for it. But its picture of 2035 is Buurtzorg in 2006 with better software. It keeps the hour as the hidden unit. It keeps home care and community nursing as separate trades. It leaves the night, the years before eligibility and the family carer mostly as they are. And two of its architecture verdicts are wrong.

## 1. The hour is still the unit, under a new name

The paper prices "per person per week", then defines the bands as "an 11-hour band" with "a floor of human hours" [seen in the paper, s. 8.1]. That is the timed visit, added up over seven days. The council will audit hours against the floor. So the team will plan to the floor. I have watched this happen [experience]: any number you guarantee becomes the number you deliver.

The bolder move: guarantee **conditions and continuity**, not hours. "Seen by a known face every day you want it"; "no more than four faces a month"; "a change acted on within four hours". Price on the person's need and trajectory, reset at review. Keep a human-time measure as a warning light, not a contract term [reasoned]. Without this, the rest of the design leaks back to the minute.

## 2. Merge with community nursing; stop bolting on "delegated tasks"

The paper treats health tasks as an NHS add-on, priced on top [seen in the paper, s. 8.1]. That is today's split between health and social care, kept by habit. Insulin, wound checks, observations and catheter care happen in the same home, often on the same day, by two services that do not talk [experience].

By 2035 the natural unit is **one neighbourhood team for health and care at home**, with nurses inside it. The NHS and council pay one capitated amount for everyone in the patch who needs care at home [conjecture]. The paper puts pooled budgets at 2033–35, "where offered". I would make them the target from the first contract. Capitated neighbourhood contracts are being tried now in parts of the NHS [seen, general knowledge: integrated neighbourhood teams in the 2025 ten-year health plan]. This is where the savings the paper says fall "in the wrong pocket" get captured.

## 3. Start before the person is eligible

The paper cuts hours per person from 11 to 10 by 2035 [seen in the paper, s. 7.3]. That is timid. The biggest lever is not doing care faster. It is people needing less of it, later. Councils meet people only once they are in crisis. The data to spot frailty early already sits in GP records and, soon, in homes [reasoned].

So sell **a "before care" service**: light-touch monitoring, strength and balance work, home adaptation, a named neighbourhood practitioner, for people a year or two from eligibility. Pay for it from the NHS and from self-funders. If it delays the first package by a year for a fifth of people, that beats every efficiency in the paper [conjecture]. The paper's own constitution says "freed time goes to the person first". Freed years matter more.

## 4. Redesign the night

The paper gives the night one line: "the night desk hands over". Nights are where home care is weakest and dearest. Waking nights and night sits cost a great deal. Families burn out. People move to care homes because of nights more than days [experience].

By 2035, nights should be **remote-first, with a mobile responder**: chosen sensors, a voice line, a night practitioner covering a patch on an e-bike or in a car, and a shared response across providers in a town [conjecture]. Responder services already exist in some areas [seen, general knowledge]. The paper has the parts. It has not made the night a designed product.

## 5. Pay family carers through the team

The paper puts the family carer "inside the team" with strain watched. That is kind, not bold. Families give most of the care [seen in the paper, s. 10.2]. The bolder move: **offer family carers paid, trained, part-time roles in the team**, for their own relative where the person wants it, with supervision, respite and pension. Direct payments can already employ some relatives in limited cases [seen, general knowledge]. This is the largest untapped workforce in England [reasoned]. It also eases the labour constraint the paper calls binding.

## 6. Too cautious on machines in intimate care

The paper says robots will not wash or dress people by 2035, and offers machine help for privacy as a side note. I would turn this round. Transfer aids and single-handed moving already cut double-handed calls [experience; seen, general knowledge: single-handed care reviews]. By 2035, assisted bathing, toileting aids and assisted dressing are likely for many people [conjecture]. Many people would rather a machine helped them on the toilet than a stranger [reasoned]. Offer it first as **dignity**, not as saving. The labour saved is then a side effect, which is the paper's own principle.

## 7. The architecture verdicts

- **G5 BREAKS is too harsh.** G5 says people stand at the edges, "sized by the service". Here the service needs people at the centre, so sizing by the service puts them there. That is a bend in wording, not a break. The paper's own restatement admits as much [reasoned]. Call it BENDS (heavily).
- **G6 HOLDS is too generous.** Information is born in the home, but authority still sits with the council assessor and the NHS record owner. The paper's own core fault (s. 2.1) survives in landscape B. Call it BENDS [reasoned].
- **C6 HOLDS is too generous.** Home care providers fail and hand back contracts often [seen, general knowledge]. The person's record, wishes, advance decisions and team history must outlive the firm and move with the person. That is custody beyond the firm. Call it BENDS, with a portability duty [reasoned].
- **C8 HOLDS undersells it.** Licensing the operating system is not a hedge for 2031. In landscape D it is the business. I would license from 2028 [experience].
- **C7 BENDS is right**, but should say surge staff come from the float pool *and* trained family carers (point 5).
- **G2, G3, G4, G7, G8, C1, C3, C5 and the C9 promotion** I accept. **G9, the workforce as a designed condition,** is the paper's best idea. Keep it.

## 8. Smaller points

- The 2027 margin of minus 40% will not raise money from anyone I know [experience]. The discharge wedge is right. Run it with the software licence and private clients from day one.
- Target turnover below 12% is right. My experience is that guaranteed hours do more than pay rises [experience].
- Add **portability** to section 10.2: the person keeps their team if the contract changes hands.

## What I would change first

Replace hour-bands with conditions and continuity. Make the neighbourhood team health and care together, on a capitated contract. Add "before care", nights and paid family roles as products. Re-score G5, G6 and C6.
