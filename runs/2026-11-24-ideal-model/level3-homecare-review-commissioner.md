# Review of the level-3 home care redesign: a commissioner's view

Reviewer: a director of adult social care commissioning at an English county council, written in character, 24 November 2026. I read the whole paper.

Labels: **[seen]** exists today; **[reasoned]** follows from logic; **[conjecture]** plausible by 2035. I add **[experience]** for what commissioners like me routinely meet. It is a role, not a source, so treat it as informed judgement, not evidence.

---

## My verdict in one paragraph

I would buy this, but not as written. The diagnosis is right: we buy minutes because minutes are what we can count, and that is why we get 15-minute calls, call monitoring and churn [reasoned]. The fix, a weekly price held by a neighbourhood team, is the right shape. But the paper treats me as a barrier to get round, through the NHS and private clients first. It should treat me as the co-designer of the contract. And it is too timid exactly where my money is: pooled budgets, trusted assessment and shared savings.

## Would I pay the price?

The paper quotes £390 to £420 a week for an 11-hour band. That is about £35 to £38 an hour. My average rate is nearer £25 [seen, secondary, as in the paper]. On a like-for-like hourly view I cannot afford it [reasoned].

But I do not need it to be cheaper per hour. I need it cheaper per person per year. A residential placement in my county costs well over twice a home care package [experience]. If the team keeps one person in ten at home for six months longer, much of the premium is paid back [reasoned, on model figures]. Add packages not handed back, fewer reassessments and fewer emergency re-brokerages after provider failure, and the case is plausible. I would test it on a year's total cost per person in one patch, against a matched patch. The paper proposes this. It should be the headline, not a line in 9.2.

## How I would pay for outcomes

1. **A band price per person-week**, with open-book accounts and a yearly reconciliation of bands. Per-person pricing invites up-banding and choosing easy people [experience]. I need an independent band review and a duty to accept every referral in the patch.
2. **A larger outcome share than 10%.** At 10% nothing changes in behaviour [experience]. I would put 15 to 20% at risk, but only on things the team controls: continuity, reliability, person-reported quality of life, and reablement results at six and twelve weeks [reasoned].
3. **A shared-savings pool with the NHS from year one.** The paper waits until 2033 for pooled budgets "where offered". The Better Care Fund already pools council and NHS money under section 75 [seen]. Discharge is where both of us bleed. Put the savings share in the first discharge contract [reasoned].
4. **The floor of hours, but time-limited.** A permanent floor fights reablement: a person who regains independence should need fewer hours. I would set the floor per review period, then reset it from evidence [reasoned].

## Where it is too cautious

- **The council keeps assessment.** Why? Trusted assessor models already exist in discharge [seen]. Let senior practitioners change the plan within the band and notify me. I audit, rather than approve. That is the real cut in my staff's workload [reasoned].
- **One town and private clients first.** Private clients fund early losses, but they pull the firm away from the people I serve [experience]. I would rather co-fund the losses for a patch through a development grant than see a self-funder business with a council sideline.
- **Robots and sensors are hedged to death.** Fine on cameras. But medication dispensers and night sensors already cut double-handed calls and night sits [seen, in council telecare services]. Count them in the 2027 model, not as a 2035 hope.

## What worries me

1. **A patch monopoly is a market risk I own.** Under the Care Act I must shape a sustainable market and step in if a provider fails [seen]. One provider per neighbourhood on a 2% margin (landscape B) is a single point of failure [reasoned]. I would require step-in rights, a shared float pool with a second provider, and records that move with the person.
2. **Thin margins and a −40% start.** Who capitalises this? If it is private equity, I want covenants on debt and on the floor [experience].
3. **Rural patches.** A third of my county is rural. Density fails there. The paper mentions cars and moves on. I need a rural model, perhaps micro-providers linked to a team [seen: Somerset's community micro-enterprises].
4. **"Advocate for need against the payer."** Right in principle. But I have seen providers inflate need to fill hours [experience]. Advocacy must go through evidence to an independent reviewer, not through pressure on my social workers.
5. **Data used against people.** The constitution says home data never cuts a budget unseen. Good. But councils, including mine, will be tempted [experience]. Put it in the contract, with an external audit.
6. **Transition of staff.** Converting an agency means TUPE, existing contracts and people who like zero hours. The 90-day conversion is optimistic [experience].

## The architecture verdicts

- **G6 HOLDS: too generous.** Capability where information is born, yes. But oversight both ways needs my systems and the GP's to read the live view. Today they cannot [seen]. Call it BENDS until the shared record carries social care data.
- **C4 HOLDS "with care": too generous.** The evidence for prediction is mostly company claims, as the paper admits. Pooling also needs every council in the region to sign data agreements. BENDS.
- **C8 HOLDS: too generous.** My in-house team will not license its operating system from a rival bidder in the same patches. Procurement and conflict of interest get in the way [reasoned]. BENDS, or needs a separate, neutral company.
- **C7 BENDS: right, but the fix is too small.** Five per cent slack will not meet a winter discharge surge. Surge should be a jointly funded NHS retainer, not the firm's cost [reasoned].
- **C1 BENDS: right.** But the payer's side is missing. A guaranteed outcome needs a payer who can hold the saving. That is pooled money, not a firm's promise.
- **G7 BENDS (much heavier): right,** and it should name the market duty. The firm owes duties to the person; I owe duties to the whole market.
- **G5 BREAKS and C9 promoted: agreed.** This is the paper's best finding.
- **G2 BENDS: slightly harsh.** In a council contract I am already the check on the payee. Open books and an outcome audit give "beside, not between" in substance.

**Missing from section 10.2:** the market as a condition. I do not buy a firm; I buy a market that must survive any one firm failing. The architecture needs that element as much as it needs the workforce.

## Terms on which I would sign

A three-year patch contract, extendable to seven. Per-person-week bands with independent review. 15 to 20% at risk on controllable outcomes. A section 75 savings share with the ICB from day one. Trusted assessment within the band. Step-in rights, open books and data clauses. A matched comparison patch, judged on total cost per person per year. If it beats the comparison at two years, I would scale it [reasoned]. If it does not, I would still keep the pay and team model, because retention alone is worth it [experience].
