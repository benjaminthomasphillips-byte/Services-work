# What the Pluris case collection offers the design

Claude, 24 November 2026. Read-only review of the Pluris collection in the Cases data source of the Thoughts Library (Notion). Nothing in Notion or in the design files was changed. Nothing here is a recommendation to change the design; it is material for the owner to weigh.

## What I read, and how much to trust it

- **Titles of all 231 Pluris records,** P001 to P231. They are listed in the appendix.
- **Bodies of 30 records, read in full** (each page is short: properties, reported result, analyst reading where there is one, limitations, source):
  - retrofits, integration and acquisitions: P009, P030, P039, P100, P119, P128, P165, P179, P187, P210, P216;
  - verification and control: P006, P048, P068, P070, P111, P146, P173, P203;
  - back office and finance: P012, P086, P089, P145, P174;
  - workforce and adoption: P057, P113, P155, P159, P162, P191.
- **Not read:** the other 201 bodies. I chose by title, so I may have missed relevant material behind weak titles. P038 ("How AI/ML Diligence Stopped a SaaS Acquisition") and P080 ("How One PE Firm Dodged a 15–20% Overpayment") are the only titles that hint at a negative result; I did not read them.
- **Design files:** architecture-v1 and step 4 read in full; step 1 headings skimmed.

**Reliability.** Every one of the 231 records is "Imported unverified", with evidence type "Promotional library / implementer report; not audited". Every source is a checkpluris.com case-study page written for the implementer. Most pages say "Analyst reading: Not independently analysed for this library". Seven of my thirty carry a short analyst reading from the old library (P006, P068, P089, P111, P159, P173, P191); those readings are the library owner's interpretation, not evidence. Several records overlap with B-numbered records (P006 with B10, P068 with B17, P089 with B27 and N016, P111 with B19, P159 with B14, P173 with B4, P191 with B23), so they are not independent confirmation of those.

**Selection.** This is a vendor showcase. All thirty bodies I read describe success. None reports a failure, a rollback, staff outcomes, full cost or a later re-measurement. One provider, TrustEvals, accounts for five of my eight verification and retrofit records (P119, P146, P203, P210, P216), so the "verification" picture is partly one vendor's sales pitch.

**Labels.** Every figure below is a **[vendor claim]** unless marked otherwise. **[Analyst reading]** is the old library's interpretation. **Inference** is mine.

## 1. Useful material the design does not yet use

**1.1 A retrofit sequence for a messy incumbent (step 4, §10 "Still to do", and the "Buy firms" and "Buy books" routes in §8).** The design names retrofitting as open. The Pluris records, taken together, sketch a sequence that no single record states. Inference: the order below is mine, assembled from separate claims.

1. *Read only, first.* P210: "A read-only extraction layer means reporting can never destabilise the live production ERP" ("Zero-write ERP integration").
2. *Map what is actually done, not the org chart.* P179: integration "shortened by 80%, driven by having actual process maps rather than relying on org charts and interviews"; "40% of work being performed across the roll-up was redundant". P128, weeks 1–3: "Mapped all 35+ disconnected systems, analysed 400,000 files, identified what mattered." P039: the tool "read and mapped 290+ instances up front", claiming "60% less rewrite effort".
3. *Find leakage early, so the retrofit pays for itself.* P100: "\$100K a month in missed billing found and stopped", caused by "Labor hours rounded down before invoicing", "8 days to the first useful answer" against "a quoted 16–20 weeks and \$300K–\$500K for a data warehouse build". P165: a "\$100K Error Class Eliminated" once the system flagged "mismatches between project timelines and inventory status".
4. *Write the policy down before building agents.* P145: "The foundational unlock was building the knowledge base artifact before writing a single line of agent logic", encoding "accounting policies, chart of accounts, and close decision tree". P089: "translating the CEO's negotiation expertise into structured, AI-executable rules … before any code was written".
5. *Prove the new path matches the old.* P216: "Real production data is replayed to catch regressions before cutover"; "A reference design validates that the old and migrated systems produce the same results."
6. *Cut over quickly, in phases, and switch the old tool off.* P128 gives a 12-week plan ending "Decommissioned Wrike" and claims "Delay kills adoption." P030: "Phased rollout completed with no operational downtime."
7. *Make onboarding of each acquired site repeatable.* P187 claims "a repeatable onboarding playbook" across 15 schools with handwritten inputs.

This bears directly on the step 4 build path (2027 and 2028–2029 rows) and on G1 (the record): the records suggest the record in a retrofit is built *beside* the incumbent's systems, read-only, before anything is written back.

**1.2 Acquired firms bring ungoverned AI with them (step 4, §6.2 "Tool gateway and permissions"; §8 "Buy firms").** P119 (a banking roll-up): "75 Shadow AI Cases Found", "20 Unauthorized MCP Paths … Exposed across multiple entities, previously invisible", "Consolidated to 12 Tools". Inference: after 2027, every acquired firm will arrive with its own agents and connectors. The design's tool gateway is written for the firm's own agents ("Every tool off by default"); it does not yet say what happens on day one of an acquisition.

**1.3 A missing role: someone who turns practice into maintained software (step 4, §2.1 "Builders"; §4).** P159, a recruiter's account: "the absence of a product management function was the single consistent barrier to AI adoption", across "law, freight brokerage, medical billing" firms of "\$4M–\$100M+ revenue". The library's analyst reading: "Someone must translate practice into maintained build-and-test work; tools alone may not suffice", and it does "not accept" the "universal missing-link claim". The design's builders layer pairs "domain engineers … with experts". Inference: P159 suggests naming a product owner per case type, separate from engineering, especially inside an acquired incumbent.

**1.4 Moving the check to the person who makes the work (G6; G5 sizing; step 4 §2.4 "Review and sign-off").** P068: content reaching compliance "improved from ~30% compliant to 95% compliant after internal teams were given access to the custom GPT for self-screening" ("Guest-reported; not independently verified"); compliance moved "from full-volume content reviewer to lightweight exception-checker". Analyst reading: "Move checking to the originator and change what reaches the scarce expert." Inference: the design sizes review from "the review rule … multiplied by volume". P068 suggests a second lever: raising the quality of what arrives, so the volume needing review falls before the rule is applied.

**1.5 Outcome pricing needs deterministic execution (G4, G13).** P048: "Deterministic execution and reliable document comprehension let the company price on the outcome delivered instead of seats sold". Inference: this supports G4's "conditions and moments", and gives a stated precondition for it.

## 2. Counter-opinions and challenges

**2.1 Watching people, against G8 and step 1 P10 ("do not watch people"). Strength: strong as a signal of where the market is going, weak as evidence.**
- P179's method "requires deploying lightweight screen and keystroke capture infrastructure across employee endpoints", with "PII redaction" noted as "significant engineering and compliance effort". The 40% redundancy and 80% faster integration claims depend on this.
- P155 (a healthcare network): computer vision scores caregivers, with "99% accuracy in identifying positive caregiver interactions", feeding an "incentive pay system", after "two and a half years of iteration". The claimed outcome is "Care Culture Visibly Transformed"; incident reduction is a "Target Metric in Progress".
- P086 joins "HR systems with IoT GPS data from field devices" to alert supervisors.
- Inference: the design's rule "Never track what can be declared" and "capture decisions as a by-product; do not watch people" is a deliberate stance against the cheapest retrofit tool on offer. Rivals buying incumbents will use these tools. P155 bears hardest on home care (C13, C9): it is the market's answer to the same workforce problem, and the opposite of the design's.

**2.2 Plain rules and integration, not agents, may carry most of the gain (principle 15, "The burden of proof is on complexity"; G5 "agents for information"). Strength: moderate.** P173: compliance checks went "from about 30 minutes to about a second", "~2,000x", but the analyst reading says "Integration and explicit rules can produce the central gain; AI is supplementary analytics here." P165 and P100 likewise credit joining systems. Inference: this supports principle 15, but cuts against any reading of G5 that makes agents the default producer. In a retrofit, much of the early gain may come from deterministic software.

**2.3 A third review rule, between sampling and every output (G5; architecture §8 "The review rule is the biggest unknown"). Strength: moderate.** The design frames the choice as "sampling, or review of every output". The records show other forms:
- P048: "Agents-on-reins keeps every high-frequency task inside a bounded, auditable action space, so the platform runs at enterprise volume without a person reviewing each step." Control is by constraint of what the agent may do, not by review of what it did.
- P070: "human-in-the-loop review for lower-confidence scenarios", required by "Regulatory needs". Review by confidence band.
- P145: "for auditability and SOX-adjacent control purposes, the agent never finalizes anything without a human approval step". A human gate on the final act, not on each line.
Inference: these are three distinct review rules (bounded action space, confidence-tiered review, gate at finalisation) that a regulator might accept where sampling is refused.

**2.4 Approved decisions written back into the agent's knowledge (engine; principle 8 "the improver never touches the test"). Strength: moderate, as a design risk.** P145: "Each approved close decision is written back into the knowledge base, so the system's accuracy and autonomy increase over time". P174: "a review loop that learns from approved corrections". Inference: this is the engine's correction loop in practice, but with no sign of a held-out evaluator. If approvals are rubber-stamped (L014's deferring reviewers), errors become precedent. It strengthens the case for the design's independent evaluation function rather than weakening it.

**2.5 Adoption-first, not redesign-first (lesson 1, "Redesign, do not automate"). Strength: weak.** P057 ("30 mid-to-senior CPA professionals went from zero AI usage to active, structured experimentation within a single month"), P162 ("~300 people … enabled"), P113 (low-code tools over "three months"; "The primary investment was strategic diagnosis and change management, not engineering"). These claim positive return from level-1 change. Inference: they are consistent with lesson 1 (this is what incumbents already buy) and do not show it is wrong. They are a reminder that an acquired incumbent's staff may already have a habit of tool use that the design must replace.

**2.6 Adversaries adapt (G8 "parties who learn"; claims design). Strength: weak to moderate.** P089: an AI pipeline compares restoration proposals and recovers "~\$500 More Per Proposal". Analyst reading: "Cheap comparison makes more discrepancies worth negotiating"; limitation: "insurer response unknown". Inference: in claims, both sides will automate negotiation. The design's claims service assumes it controls restoration; P089 shows contractors are arming against insurers at the same time.

**2.7 Juniors learning from a knowledge base (G9). Strength: weak.** P191: "Senior time consumed by onboarding and supporting juniors dropped about 60%"; "Juniors self-serve precedent". The analyst limitation is the point: "No measure of independent judgment, errors or later competence". Inference: this is the opposite pull to G9. A firm can save senior time by letting juniors self-serve, and lose the apprenticeship G9 is trying to rebuild.

## 3. Specificity

**3.1 A 12-week retrofit timetable (step 4, §8; §10).** P128 [vendor claim], care payroll firm:
- weeks 1–3: discovery, "Mapped all 35+ disconnected systems, analysed 400,000 files";
- weeks 4–8: "Designed 10-site SharePoint architecture. Built compliance registers. Created security model with 20 Entra ID groups";
- weeks 9–11: "Migrated critical data. Trained 6 teams. Decommissioned Wrike. Extracted two years of compliance knowledge";
- week 12: go-live.
It adds that "AI processed 400,000 files and extracted patterns. Humans made every decision that mattered", and "Two years of compliance tickets became structured FAQ content". Inference: the useful pattern is that past tickets become the first playbook and test material. That maps onto step 1 P8 (the cold start: where the first evidence comes from).

**3.2 A feasibility gate before investment (step 4, §8 stop rules).** P012: "Three weeks of disciplined AI R&D plus three weeks of engineering" reached "80% accuracy on the OCR-heavy documents where table-transformer approaches had failed", with "a model-swappable framework". Inference: a dated six-week gate per new case type, with an accuracy threshold set in advance, would make the design's general "every autonomy level has a dated test" concrete.

**3.3 The verification stack (step 4, §6.2 "Verification").**
- P203: "Pre-validation, coding, and an independent reviewer model, with human accept/edit gates"; "Each suggested code links back to the exact source chart text with confidence scoring". Three stages, source-linked, scored.
- P146: checks run "on every single generation" for "accuracy, PII-safety, and authorization"; "An independent model flags inconsistencies before any analysis is signed off"; "Generation draws only on permissioned P&L data feeding pre-vetted analysis templates".
- P111: "Hallucinated endpoint paths dropped from 26% to less than 1%"; "Prompt caching cut cost by roughly 25% (about \$6) per run". Analyst reading: "verification is not necessarily fixed human reading time". Limitation: "Endpoint error rate is not end-to-end connector reliability".
- P006: "100% Query Accuracy in the P&L Pilot"; the limitation says "Unknown test size … 100% is not a general reliability rate". The analyst reading is useful: "Definitions and approved context are part of the product".
Inference: these give the verification layer a concrete checklist per output: authority to act checked (G12), source link with confidence, independent model, permitted data only, human gate by confidence. "Authorization" checked on every run is the operational form of G12.

**3.4 Equivalence testing for model changes and migrations (engine; step 4 §6.2 "Model harness"; §6.3).** P216's "production data replay" is the same mechanism the engine needs when adopting a new model: replay recent real cases, compare against the reference, release only on equivalence. Inference: one harness can serve both retrofits and model upgrades.

**3.5 Layered rules in a multi-entity setting (G3).** P174: "connectors across multiple ERPs and spreadsheets, locally hosted models, a review loop that learns from approved corrections, and a jurisdiction-aware rules layer"; "90% Of transaction records classified correctly with no manual touch, checked against tax-expert review". Inference: a working example of G3's layers (jurisdiction as one layer) and of an exception rate (10%) that could size the judgement desk (step 4 §2.4).

**3.6 Deadlines as the design constraint (G11).** P187: the month "now closes in about 3 days across every school, inside the 5-day federal window"; "Every receipt line is traceable and auditable by the team and state reviewers". P145: close "from 10 days to 1 day". P030: "15+ days to under five". Inference: each gives a measurable condition of the G4 kind ("books closed today").

**3.7 Leakage as the first proof in a retrofit (step 4, §8, 2027 row "Make one public promise").** P100's failure mode is precise: "Labor hours rounded down before invoicing". P086: "Each day of operational leakage costs over \$100,000". P165: "\$100,000 in emergency air freight". Inference: in an acquired incumbent the first measurable win is likely to be money already lost between systems. That is a candidate first promise, and a way to fund the retrofit.

**3.8 Post-merger engineering (G-group E, engine change pipeline).** P009: after a merger, "Standardized agent deployment and workflow changes" gave a "65% Improvement in PR Revert Rate" and a "Ticket-to-PR Agent Live in Production". Inference: revert rate is a usable engine metric for the change pipeline ("rolled back if they fail").

**3.9 Replacing licensed parts of an incumbent's stack (step 4 §6.2 "Build, rent or open").** P039: "The licensed image viewer became diagnostic imaging the company owns, removing the per-customer fee that grew with every deal" (estimated "\$500K+/yr"). Inference: in a retrofit, per-client licence fees inside the incumbent's stack are a scale tax; the build-or-rent table could name them.

## 4. Gaps: what the design asks that this collection cannot answer

1. **Failure.** No record I read reports a failed retrofit, a rollback or an error that reached clients. The design's §6.3 (blast radius, rollback, "an error that has already reached thousands of clients") gets no evidence here.
2. **What happened to the people.** Records count "hours reclaimed" (P179 "200–250 Hours/Month"; P113 "10–15 Hours Per Week") but never say whether staff were redeployed, retrained or let go. The design's "Acquired staff" row (step 4 §4) needs exactly that.
3. **Whether a regulator accepted the review rule.** P070 and P145 cite regulatory and "SOX-adjacent" needs, but none reports a regulator or insurer agreeing to less than full review. The architecture's biggest unknown stays unknown.
4. **Full cost and duration.** Only P100 gives a cost comparison, and it is the alternative's quote, not the project's cost.
5. **Durability.** Nothing is re-measured after go-live. P155's own outcome metric is "in progress".
6. **The parties the firm does not serve** (G7, lesson 5). No record measures outcomes for tenants, claimants, patients or employees, except P155, which measures employees in order to pay them.
7. **The record as the client's, and portability** (G1, G10). Every retrofit builds the record inside the client's or the implementer's systems; none addresses who owns it or how it would move.
8. **UK context.** Nearly all records are US or unstated. Nothing bears on Making Tax Digital, UK lettings law or the UK professional bodies the design must engage.
9. **Services firms as the subject.** Few of the thirty are services firms in the design's sense (P057, P068, P089, P113, P128, P159, P191). Most are internal functions of product companies.
